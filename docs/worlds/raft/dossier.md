# New setting direction for game-world

We're moving away from the cozy Stardew-style village. It gives change without stakes, and I want something punchier, with a tense surface, more weirdness, and more room for soap-opera drama. The earlier design direction still applies (legibility, progressive environmental change, characters making lasting changes, visible intentions that others support or oppose, group intentions/proposals, an extensible behavior-module library, perturbation tests). This setting gives those systems something to chew on. Keep core systems setting-agnostic where reasonable; a generation-ship setting may get the same engine later.

Please read this, look at how the current village is built, and propose how to move toward this setting incrementally (what to keep, what to reskin, what new systems are needed first). Don't try to build it all at once. Names below are placeholders.

## Why this setting
- Flavor inspiration is China Miéville's New Weird (the floating city of Armada in The Scar), but do NOT copy it. Borrowed flavor: a city of incompatible little communities physically lashed together, each with its own way of life, each giving something up for what it has.
- Central theme: people trading away a bit of their humanity for a substance that's useful, valuable, and intriguing, not just economically necessary. The seep is a little like spice in Dune: used locally AND sold.
- I want neuroses, lore, weirdness, and conflicts between neighbors. One mixed city, not hard-walled factions: groups overlap in bars, markets, families.
- No cheap death / resurrection. Relationships and grudges should last.

## The world
- A city of boats and rafts tied together, anchored (it doesn't travel), on a wide, shallow, fully submerged shelf with no land anywhere close.
- Across the shelf, a substance ("seep") bubbles up from below in many places. The fact that it rises in multiple spots matters: prospectors find new seeps, stake claims, and boats lash on nearby. Old seeps can decline. This is a main engine of progressive change and conflict.
- Ships don't slowly sink; don't build on that idea.

## Ways the seep is harvested and used
- **Kelp (and other seep plants):** kelp is like coca, a mild stimulant everyone chews all day, ordinary background life. Other undersea plants have other effects. Bars serve drinks/snacks made from them. Map plant effects onto the moods/emotions that already exist in the sim, so I can tinker with them and so moods become visible.
- **Fish:** the area is bountiful; people need food. Seep potency survives raw (sushi) and is lost when cooked. Raw eaters tend to be a bit nuts; some people just want food and cook it. Randomness: a given piece of sushi can have an unplanned effect on someone's mood that day, sometimes bad. Bad batches can hit many people at once (traceable through who bought where).
- **Fish catchers/trainers:** distinct from food fishers. They catch exotic fish for sale, do captive breeding, and train worker fish to find things (e.g. seeps). People who catch fish this way become fish-like over time. A go-between group helps them deal with everyone else because they're really weird. Convention: if you catch a protected/worker fish, you put it back; breaking that causes real disputes.
- **Crust:** seep crystallizes on the seabed and is mined by divers in hardened suits. Most valuable export. Divers spend long stretches submerged (bends) and live in bell-jar habitats below, taking things/wearing things that make it possible and that affect them. Surfacing is a big deal. The bell-jar levels are NOT a separate society; they're part of the same city. (Physical cost of crust exposure: undecided.)
- **Gas:** bubbles are captured where they rise. A little raw gas does something, but the good stuff needs refining. It can't be burned as fuel; it has versatile properties, e.g. doing something over a fire, or infusing into iron to make superior metal. It's also addictive: junkies, dealers, black market. Restricted most places.

## Groups (trade-defined and lifestyle-defined zones, plus roles that cut across zones)
- **Puritans:** a lifestyle zone, not a trade. They do all the normal jobs, but with a regimented, devotional life; they cook everything and oppose the corrupting parts, believing you can be good and productive a better way. Their fishers don't get fishy but catch worse fish. They're good gas refiners because their discipline keeps them from using their supply. Some secretly sell to addicts, against their own creed: a hidden intention / scandal source.
- **Scrap zone:** a low-life raft shantytown built from discards. Cheap, lower-quality gas refining, sold openly off a table even where restricted.
- **Firefighters:** fire is the big shared danger on a wooden city. Cooking fires are normal; heavy uses like smelting may be more regulated. Firefighters are a group with a little Roman-style extortion (slower if you haven't paid), not too heavy.
- **Clerks/business people:** a role found everywhere, concentrated in a downtown where people wheel and deal, maybe with a commodities exchange (crust, gas, live fish, kelp). Prices double as a readout of what's happening in the world (good for legibility).
- **Docks:** sailors and shipping coming and going to distant places; outside trade fleets buy exports here; newcomers arrive here.
- Possibly also: lashers/shipwrights who hold the city together, apothecaries, a weak council of zones (where proposals/group intentions would live).
- Division of labor (e.g. by gender) is a zone- or guild-specific convention, not citywide.
- Lore idea: a haunted, unoccupied part of the city nobody will unmoor.

## What I want out of it
A world I can drop into and explore with some lens or goal, where details matter, the environment visibly accumulates history, characters pursue intentions that collide, and odd local customs, addictions, and changes produce stories worth following.
