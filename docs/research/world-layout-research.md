# Build the town as a validated door graph

Lay the town out as a graph of places joined by doors, author each place as an ASCII block with its own legend, and test the result with flood-fill connectivity, door-pair and schedule-reachability validators plus ASCII file snapshots. That is what the evidence supports. Every shipped game examined ends with a two-level structure: a coarse graph of places (Stardew locations, Pokémon maps, Zelda rooms, Sims rooms, RimWorld regions, Shadows of Doubt rooms) and a local grid search inside each. The real decision is whether that graph is authored or derived from geometry. Authored graphs (Stardew, Pokémon, Ocarina of Time) fail by omission, and the failure appears at load time as a missing warp. Derived graphs (The Sims, RimWorld, Dwarf Fortress) fail by geometry at runtime as a stuck agent, and a continuous world with a Z axis also obliges every schedule record to carry Z, which Ultima VII never did, so its upper floors stayed empty. For an agent with no screen the authored graph is also the testable one: DCSS refuses to load an unreachable vault, Stardew throws during day setup when a schedule route has no warp, and both checks are a short BFS. The existing proposal in `docs/design/world-layout.md` has the right shape. The research changes three of its claims (NPCs should walk real tiles in every place every tick rather than hop at the place level; a door should be one bidirectional record with an explicit one-way flag rather than two warps; the route table must be shortest-path rather than first-found) and adds about a dozen validators and format rules, listed in the check section near the end.

Throughout, sentences with an inline link are verified against a source in the research notes. Sentences marked "Inference" are my reading of that evidence and have no direct source.

## Shipped games all end with a two-level graph, authored or derived

Stardew Valley is the canonical authored graph. Every interior is its own `GameLocation` with its own map, and a warp is a per-tile record `Warp fromX fromY toArea toX toY`, repeatable in any map's properties, with `NPCWarp` for NPC-only crossings and `LockedDoorWarp toX toY toArea openTime closeTime [NPC] [friendship]` for doors with hours ([Modding:Maps](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Modding:Maps)). Buildings are defined with the exterior and interior as separate data: `Size` is the exterior footprint in tiles, `IndoorMap` names the interior map, `HumanDoor` is the door tile relative to the building's top-left, and `CollisionMap` lists which footprint tiles are walkable ([Modding:Buildings](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Modding:Buildings)). Because the two are decoupled, the basic Shed has a **7x3 exterior and an 11x9 interior** ([Stardew Valley Wiki, Shed](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Shed)), and a fan survey of farm buildings finds footprint-to-interior ratios from about **2.7 (coop) to 7.4 (deluxe barn)** ([Nexus Mods article](https://www.nexusmods.com/stardewvalley/mods/4202)). Vanilla Stardew has no upper floor on any building; the farmhouse grows by map overrides on one location, its cellar is a distinct location, and upstairs floors exist only through mods that point each floor at its own map file ([FarmHouse.cs](https://github.com/AcidicNic/StardewValleyDecompiled1.6/blob/2878fb248092f9f5b8704ad2cc7e19d0abe1cf45/StardewValley.Locations/FarmHouse.cs); [Nexus Mods 23068](https://www.nexusmods.com/stardewvalley/mods/23068)).

NPC routing in Stardew is a two-level search and its costs are concrete. `WarpPathfindingCache.PopulateCache` runs a depth-first enumeration over every location's warps and interior doors, skipping locations with `ExcludeFromNpcPathfinding`, and records every location sequence it finds; `GetLocationRoute` returns the **first** recorded match, and routes are neither sorted nor length-limited, so the result is not guaranteed shortest ([WarpPathfindingCache.cs](https://github.com/AcidicNic/StardewValleyDecompiled1.6/blob/2878fb248092f9f5b8704ad2cc7e19d0abe1cf45/StardewValley.Pathfinding/WarpPathfindingCache.cs)). For each hop, `NPC.pathfindToNextScheduleLocation` asks the current location for a warp point to the next one and throws `"it requires a warp from X to Y, but none was found"` if there is none, then runs a per-location A* with a **30,000-iteration cap** ([NPC.cs](https://github.com/AcidicNic/StardewValleyDecompiled1.6/blob/2878fb248092f9f5b8704ad2cc7e19d0abe1cf45/StardewValley/NPC.cs)). That A* treats any tile that is a warp source as impassable, along with `LockedDoorWarp` tiles, `NoPath` tiles and Buildings-layer tiles without `Passable` or `NPCPassable` ([PathFindController.cs](https://github.com/AcidicNic/StardewValleyDecompiled1.6/blob/2878fb248092f9f5b8704ad2cc7e19d0abe1cf45/StardewValley.Pathfinding/PathFindController.cs)). The documented failures follow directly: a representative crash log shows `schedule pathing tried to find a warp point that doesn't exist` thrown inside `dayUpdate`, so one bad NPC route stops the whole day from starting ([Stardew forums 27022](https://forums.stardewvalley.net/threads/schedule-pathing-error-in-update-loop-dont-know-the-source.27022/latest)); the modding troubleshooting page lists schedule points too dense to walk between, pathing into Buildings-layer tiles, pathing into excluded maps, "voidwalking" when a warp cannot be reached, and unintended warps where NPCs walk into a warp tile added by another mod and end up elsewhere ([Troubleshooting: NPCs](https://stardewmodding.wiki.gg/wiki/Troubleshooting:_NPCs)). Off-screen simulation is the simplest possible: the host calls `updateEvenIfFarmerIsntHere` on every location every tick, which updates every NPC there, so NPCs in other maps walk their real tile paths ([Game1.cs](https://github.com/AcidicNic/StardewValleyDecompiled1.6/blob/2878fb248092f9f5b8704ad2cc7e19d0abe1cf45/StardewValley/Game1.cs)).

Pokémon Gen 1 to 3 uses both patterns at once and exposes the bookkeeping cost of each. Towns and routes are separate maps stitched edge to edge by `MapConnection { direction; offset; mapGroup; mapNum }` records, while every interior is a separate map entered through a `WarpEvent { x, y, elevation, warpId, mapNum, mapGroup }` ([pokeemerald global.fieldmap.h](https://github.com/pret/pokeemerald/blob/master/include/global.fieldmap.h)). Porymap's manual states that "Connections are one-way. Each map's side of a connection must be set separately," and offers a "Mirror to Connecting Maps" option to keep the pair consistent ([Porymap manual](https://huderlem.github.io/porymap/manual/editing-map-connections.html)). Multi-floor buildings are one map per floor joined by warps on stair tiles, for example `MAP_LITTLEROOT_TOWN_MAYS_HOUSE_1F` ([LittlerootTown map.json](https://github.com/pret/pokeemerald/blob/master/data/maps/LittlerootTown/map.json)). A randomizer that had to reason about every warp documents that a single doorway is often two or three warp events acting as one logical warp, and that some warps are one-way because the destination does not warp back ([Archipelago warps.md](https://git.hollymcfarland.com/monorail/Archipelago/src/branch/main/worlds/pokemon_emerald/docs/warps.md)). Ocarina of Time stores the door differently: a scene's "transition actors" are scene-level objects that each define "a room to load from the front and a room to load from the back," so one record owns both directions ([CloudModding, OoT Scenes and Rooms](https://wiki.cloudmodding.com/oot/Scenes_and_Rooms)).

Ultima VII is the strongest historical case of a continuous world with Z in a 2D game. The map is **3072x3072 tiles** in a 12x12 grid of superchunks, each object carries a 4-bit lift (0 to 15) as its height, and second-storey walls and roofs are ordinary objects at higher lifts stored in the per-superchunk IFIX files ([Ultima Codex, game structure](https://wiki.ultimacodex.com/wiki/Ultima_VII_game_structure)). The original `SCHEDULE.DAT` entry is four bytes holding activity, a 3-hour time slot, X, Y and superchunk, with **no Z field** ([Ultima Codex, SCHEDULE.DAT](https://wiki.ultimacodex.com/wiki/Ultima_VII_internal_formats_%E2%80%93_SCHEDULE.DAT)). Exult added an 8-byte entry with Z (`set8`, `write8`) to fix this ([schedule.h](https://github.com/exult/exult/blob/master/schedule.h)), and the forum history explains why: a developer stated "U7 doesn't provide a z-coord for schedules. In other words, they have to start on ground level," and a poster observed this "was why you almost never see characters on the second floors of buildings" ([Exult forum p=26491](https://exult.sourceforge.io/forum/viewtopic.php?p=26491)). The authoring cost is also recorded: Exult Studio's documentation says constructing a roof "can be a long and difficult process because it involves properly positioning multiple small objects and then manually setting each object's height" ([exult_studio.txt](https://github.com/exult/exult/blob/master/docs/exult_studio.txt)).

The Sims lineage shows what a derived graph looks like. In FreeSO's reimplementation a lot has `Stories = 5`, walls live on tile edges in per-level `WallTile[]` arrays with doors as flags on those edges, and rooms are never authored: `VMRoomMap.GenerateMap` flood-fills each level whenever architecture changes ([VMArchitecture.cs](https://github.com/riperiperi/FreeSO/blob/master/TSOClient/tso.simantics/VMArchitecture.cs); [VMRoomMap.cs](https://github.com/riperiperi/FreeSO/blob/master/TSOClient/tso.simantics/Model/VMRoomMap.cs)). Routing is A* over room portals (doors and stairs as nodes) followed by in-room pathfinding, and "portals are used to traverse floors, so we do not care about the floor each point is on" ([VMRoutingFrame.cs](https://github.com/riperiperi/FreeSO/blob/master/TSOClient/tso.simantics/Engine/VMRoutingFrame.cs)). The cost is that portal validity is inferred from geometry, and Sims 4 players report routing failures from a platform under a turned staircase, too little headroom at the top of stairs, open staircases without railings being treated as platforms, and roofs over stairs ([EA Answers](https://answers.ea.com/t5/Bug-Reports/WORKAROUND-Open-staircase-cause-routing-failures/m-p/9734960); [EA forums 8541511](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/the-sims-4-gameplay-en/floor-issue-on-top-of-customised-stairs/8541511)). RimWorld matured the same idea for a single level: the map is divided into regions that never exceed a 12x12 cell, a single-tile door creates its own 3-tile region that "doesn't interact with any other regions," and a room is at most 36 regions, which caps room size and penalises complex shapes ([RimWorld Wiki, Rooms](https://www.rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Rooms)). Tynan Sylvester's 2013 post gives the motive: A* "has a known weakness when no path exists," so every walkable cell gets a zone index by flood fill and pawns compare indexes before searching; a full regeneration took about 5.5 ms on a 200x200 map, too slow per frame, hence incremental updates ([Ludeon blog](https://ludeon.com/blog/2013/07/reachability-at-last)).

Dwarf Fortress is the full Z-axis case and its author's account is the clearest statement of the price. Embarks default to about 50 land levels plus 15 of sky ([DF Wiki, Z-level](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Z-level)). Tarn Adams says A* "is not good enough by itself," that precomputed hierarchies "were too hard to maintain as terrain changed," that the core trick is a walking-connectivity component index maintained by flood fill, and that adding a Z coordinate to code written around X and Y "took weeks and was the most tedious task he recalls" ([Stack Overflow Blog](https://stackoverflow.blog/2021/07/28/700000-lines-of-code-20-years-and-one-developer-how-dwarf-fortress-is-built/)). Project Zomboid and Shadows of Doubt, both continuous worlds, still carry an explicit place layer: PZ's `BuildingDef` owns a list of `RoomDef`s, each a named union of rectangles with a `level` ([PZ JavaDocs, RoomDef](https://albion.codeberg.page/PZ-JavaDocs/zombie/iso/RoomDef.html)), and Build 42 added basements by injecting prebuilt binary buildings at stream time to avoid disturbing the existing map and tools ([NME on Build 42](https://www.nme.com/news/project-zomboids-very-big-build-42-update-will-introduce-basements-3320807)). Shadows of Doubt "breaks everything down into rooms connected with doors," treats streets as rooms, and uses the door graph for both culling and movement ([DevBlog #22](https://colepowered.itch.io/shadows/devlog/164492/shadows-of-doubt-devblog-22-doors-galore)). HPA*, the academic form, abstracts a grid into clusters whose border entrances become abstract graph nodes and reports paths within 1% of optimal at up to 10x the speed of plain A* ([Jansen & Buro](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/18791/18791-52-22495-1-10-20210929.pdf)).

| Game | Data model | How an NPC crosses a boundary | Cost observed in sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stardew Valley | One map per place; per-tile warps; interiors sized freely | Route table over locations, then capped A* per location; walks every tick everywhere | Missing warp crashes day start; warp tiles double as barriers; first-found route |
| Pokémon Gen 3 | Stitched exterior maps plus warped interiors; one map per floor | Warp events; NPCs never leave their map | Connections authored twice; doorways are several warp events |
| Ocarina of Time | Scene owns rooms; transition actor names front and back room | Door is one record | Room object-list ordering unloads actors |
| Ultima VII | One 3072x3072 world; 4-bit lift per object | Walkability over lift; schedules had no Z | Upper floors empty; roofs built from many objects by hand |
| The Sims (FreeSO) | One lot, 5 levels, walls on edges, rooms flood-filled | A* over portals (doors, stairs), then in-room | Geometry-inferred portals fail (headroom, railings, platforms) |
| RimWorld | One level; 12x12-bounded regions; doors as own regions | Region index check, then A* | Room cap of 36 regions; complex shapes break |
| Dwarf Fortress | Full Z, ~65 levels | A* plus walking-component index | Weeks to add Z; hierarchies abandoned under terrain churn |
| Project Zomboid | Continuous isometric cells; `BuildingDef`/`RoomDef` with `level` | Per-tile Z movement | Basements required binary injection; 8 then 32 levels per chunk |

Inference. Every continuous-world game in the table builds a graph of places anyway, as regions, rooms or clusters, and uses it as the top level of its pathfinder. The only real choice is whether that layer is authored or derived. Authored layers fail by omission and the failure is a load-time check. Derived layers fail by geometry and the failure is a stuck agent at runtime. For a town of a few dozen buildings maintained by an agent that cannot watch the screen, load-time failure is the property that matters most.

## Editor JSON and roguelike ASCII can both be written from Node

Tiled's formats are plain JSON and small. A `.world` file is `{"type": "world", "maps": [{ "fileName", "x", "y", "width", "height" }]}` in pixels, optionally with regex `patterns` for file-name-derived placement, and Tiled reloads it when the file changes on disk ([Tiled manual: Worlds](https://doc.mapeditor.org/en/stable/manual/worlds/)). Map JSON stores tile layers as a GID array in `data` (CSV or base64 with zlib, gzip or zstd), objects with `x`, `y`, `width`, `height`, `polygon` and typed `properties`, and tilesets with `wangsets` whose tiles carry a `wangid` array of colour indexes ([Tiled JSON Map Format](https://doc.mapeditor.org/en/stable/reference/json-map-format/)). Two traps matter for a parser: the object `type` field was saved as `class` in Tiled 1.9 only and reverted in 1.10 ([Tiled JSON Map Format](https://doc.mapeditor.org/en/stable/reference/json-map-format/)), and command-line export "still requires an X server" on Linux, so headless use means `xvfb-run tiled --export-map` ([Tiled manual: Export](https://doc.mapeditor.org/en/stable/manual/export/)). Tiled has no floor concept; the forum consensus is "one tmx per level" because layers-as-floors "quickly become super-tedious" ([Tiled forum](https://discourse.mapeditor.org/t/floors-levels-aka-z-axis/1408)). On the Node side, `@kayahr/tiled` provides TypeScript types, a JSON schema and `decodeTileLayer`, but no loader and no `.world` support ([kayahr/tiled](https://github.com/kayahr/tiled/)).

LDtk is documented field by field and publishes a Draft 7 JSON Schema from which QuickType parsers are generated ([LDtk JSON structure](https://ldtk.io/json/); [docs.rs: ldtk](https://docs.rs/ldtk)). Three features bear on floors and doors. Each level has `worldX`/`worldY` in pixels and a `worldDepth` where "Higher values sit 'above' and lower values sit 'below.' It is mainly for display"; the computed `__neighbours` list includes `<` and `>` for levels at lower or greater depth and `o` for overlap at the same depth; and entity fields can be `EntityRef`s pointing across levels as `{entityIid, layerIid, levelIid, worldIid}` ([LDtk JSON_DOC.md](https://github.com/deepnight/ldtk/blob/master/docs/JSON_DOC.md)). `intGridCsv` is a logical grid of small integers, which makes it a natural target for glyph-to-value conversion. The catch for a code-first pipeline is that auto-layer rules are resolved by the editor and baked into `autoLayerTiles` on save; the JSON doc tells game developers to "ignore this section, since the editor resolves these rules before saving" ([LDtk JSON_DOC.md](https://github.com/deepnight/ldtk/blob/master/docs/JSON_DOC.md)). The matcher itself is small: the pattern is a flat `size*size` array indexed `px + py*size`, `0` ignores a cell, `±1000001` means any non-zero or zero, `abs(v)` in 1..999 requires or rejects an exact value, values above 999 address a value group, and out-of-bounds reads use `outOfBoundsValue` or fail the match ([AutoLayerRuleDef.hx](https://github.com/deepnight/ldtk/blob/master/src/electron.renderer/data/def/AutoLayerRuleDef.hx)). Super Simple Export writes a `_composite.png`, one PNG and one CSV per IntGrid layer and a `data.json` per level on every save ([LDtk Super simple export](https://ldtk.io/docs/game-dev/super-simple-export/)).

Every mature ASCII map format converges on one grammar. DCSS vaults are a header plus a fixed-width `MAP`/`ENDMAP` block; digits `1`-`7` bind to a `MONS` list, `d`-`k` to an `ITEM` list, `@` marks the entry, and `KFEAT`/`KMONS`/`KITEM` redefine a glyph locally so one glyph can stack terrain plus a monster ([DCSS syntax.txt](https://github.com/crawl/crawl/blob/master/crawl-ref/docs/develop/levels/syntax.txt)). Randomisation is a handful of operators: `SUBST: ? = T:20 U V` (weighted per cell), `SUBST: ? : TUV` (one pick shared), `NSUBST: ? = 3:w / *:l` (counted), `SHUFFLE: 123w`, and `SUBVAULT` to embed another vault ([DCSS syntax.txt](https://github.com/crawl/crawl/blob/master/crawl-ref/docs/develop/levels/syntax.txt)). NetHack's des-files require all `MAP` lines the same length, up to 76 wide and 21 high ([NetHackWiki, Des-file format](https://nethackwiki.com/wiki/Des-file_format)), and NetHack 3.7 moved the whole format into Lua as `des.map({ map = [[ ... ]], contents = function(map) ... end })` with `des.door`, `des.stair`, `des.room` and `des.levregion` calls around it ([NetHack 3.7 lua.adoc](https://github.com/NetHack/NetHack/blob/NetHack-3.7/doc/lua.adoc)). PuzzleScript binds a glyph to a stack with `and` (`@ = Crate and Target`) ([PuzzleScript levels](https://www.puzzlescript.net/Documentation/levels.html)). Quickfort is the only format surveyed with native multi-level support: `#>` and `#<` between blocks move down or up a z-level, and `#meta` blueprints compose labelled blocks with `shift`, `transform` and `repeat(down N)` ([DFHack Quickfort guide](https://docs.dfhack.org/en/stable/docs/guides/quickfort-user-guide.html)). Caves of Qud takes the opposite route, XML with an explicit `<object>` list per `<cell>` and `Load="Merge"` to append rather than replace ([Qud Modding:Maps](https://wiki.cavesofqud.com/wiki/Modding:Maps)). Spelunky avoids the glyph problem by using numeric glyphs in 10x8 room templates and substituting 5x3 obstacle sub-templates into placeholder cells ([Spelunky Generator Lessons 2](https://tinysubversions.com/spelunkyGen2/)).

| Format | Glyph block | Legend | Stacking on one cell | Variation | Floors | Known failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DCSS `.des` | Fixed width | Global table plus per-vault `KFEAT`/`KMONS`/`KITEM` | Yes, via local redefinition | `SUBST`, `NSUBST`, `SHUFFLE`, Lua | Separate vaults | Unmapped glyph silently becomes floor |
| NetHack des / Lua | Fixed width, max 76x21 | Fixed table | Via separate `des.*` calls | `random`, percent syntax | Separate levels | Ragged lines rejected |
| Quickfort | CSV cells | Per-mode symbol table | Expansion syntax `wm(60x3)` | None | `#>` / `#<` in one file | None documented |
| PuzzleScript | Fixed width | `LEGEND` with `and` | Yes | None | None | None documented |
| Caves of Qud `.rpm` | None (XML per cell) | Object names | Yes, verbose | External | Separate zones | Verbose |
| Spelunky | 10x8 numeric | Fixed | Sub-template substitution | Chance digits | None | None (solvable by construction) |

Autotiling from a logical grid is a solved problem with three re-implementable cores. Tiled terrain sets come in Corner (16 tiles for two terrains), Edge (16) and Mixed (256, or a 47-tile Blob) types, with tile probability and transformations that shrink a Blob set to 15 tiles ([Tiled manual: Terrain Sets](https://doc.mapeditor.org/en/stable/manual/terrain/)); the manual notes terrains "can reliably place cliff tops but not the vertical cliff sides, which Automapping handles" ([Tiled manual: Automapping](https://doc.mapeditor.org/en/stable/manual/automapping/)). Godot exposes `set_cells_terrain_connect(cells, terrain_set, terrain)` as a runtime API ([Godot TileMapLayer](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_tilemaplayer.html)). Inference: for a top-down building, a Wang-set resolver handles floors, grass and water, while wall tops versus wall faces need an LDtk-style pattern rule, because the tile depends on what is above it.

Procedural layout methods are available at the scale of a town, but the sources argue against making them primary. The Marson/Mirahmadi pipeline takes a rectangular footprint, groups rooms into zones, subdivides with a squarified treemap (standard treemaps "produce elongated rooms"), then adds a corridor only where a required adjacency is missing, using Dijkstra over interior walls and a connection graph with prohibitions such as bedroom-to-kitchen ([Mirahmadi & Shami](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1211.5842)). Watabou's Procgen Mansion is "generate a polyomino, split it into rectangles" ([Procgen Mansion comments](https://watabou.itch.io/procgen-mansion/comments?before=14)). Shadows of Doubt plans floors on a 5x5 grid with zoning and presets, and keeps layouts simple because of citizen pathfinding ([SoD DevBlog #13](https://colepowered.com/2019/02/)). Furniture placement in Make it Home is simulated annealing over accessibility, visibility, a door-to-door pathway term, and prior wall distance, taking **22 to 376 s per scene** in 2011 ([Yu et al.](https://ww3.math.ucla.edu/camreport/cam11-49.pdf)); its ablations show that dropping the pathway term blocks doors and dropping prior distance leaves furniture off the wall. WFC "will often generate a bunch of disconnected rooms" without a global path constraint ([Boris the Brave, WFC tips](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2020/02/08/wave-function-collapse-tips-and-tricks/)), and Unexplored generates the abstract graph first, keeps lock-and-key relationships in the output, and uses them for a "Pray For Help" completability repair ([Boris the Brave, Unexplored](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2021/04/10/dungeon-generation-in-unexplored/)). Inference: for a Stardew-scale town the generators that fit are the small ones (treemap rooms, polyomino footprints, a greedy tile-grid version of Make it Home's terms), used to draft a building that the agent then edits as ASCII, with the generator's room graph kept as data for the validators.

## Scene swap commits the renderer to the least

The scene-swap convention is Stardew's own data shape. A house interior "is a standalone map, not a patch, so it needs its own location data," the door is a tile with an `Action` such as `LockedDoorWarp 9 16 {{ModID}}_DaoHouse 900 2200 AirynDao 1000`, the way back out is an `AddWarps` entry, and the tutorial warns that the entry warp must be at least one tile further in than the exit warp "or players get stuck warping back out" ([Stardew Modding Wiki, Map Patches and Warps](https://stardewmodding.wiki.gg/wiki/Tutorial:_Map_Patches_and_Warps)). Interiors get a `DefaultArrivalTile` so that any warp into the location "put[s] the player at the door instead of in the void" ([same](https://stardewmodding.wiki.gg/wiki/Tutorial:_Map_Patches_and_Warps)). The "bigger on the inside" convention is accepted openly: Pokémon Pokopia's building kits document a 3x3x3 hut with a 6x5x4 interior and a loading screen on entry ([Bulbapedia, City hut kit](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/city_hut_kit), excerpt), and a 2015 gamedev.net thread gives the opposing cost in one line: with a transparent roof "You can't make a 3x3 tile house work" and medium houses "can fill a whole screen" ([gamedev.net](https://nene.gamedev.net/forums/topic/668452-how-to-do-rpg-interiors/5231571), excerpt).

Seamless roof hiding is one integer comparison in Exult. Every object has a lift, the engine probes four lifts above the camera actor for the lowest blocking object ("Roof can be any object, not just a literal roof"), and `paint_object` skips anything whose `lift >= skip` ([gamerend.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/exult/exult/master/gamerend.cc); [chunks.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/exult/exult/master/objs/chunks.cc)). The same cutoff drives "inside" detection, which the engine uses to decide whether to play birds or crickets ([gamewin.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/exult/exult/master/gamewin.cc)). The authoring failures appear where the mechanism is per building and hand-triggered: RuneScape Classic's Sinclair Mansion roof "does not display while you are between the hedges" and the Digsite shed roof "does not remove itself when you enter it" ([RuneScape Classic Wiki, Roof](https://classic.runescape.wiki/w/Roof)). Godot users describe the shared-layer trap, where one roof layer means "turning one off would turn of other ones," and the alternatives of per-roof nodes with an Area2D or a shader ([Godot Forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/how-to-make-tiles-transparent-when-the-player-goes-behind-them/44438)). Engines that bake tile layers into big images make roof hiding "a pretty involved modification" ([Ascension forum](https://ascensiongamedev.com/topic/3248-walking-inside-building), excerpt).

Floor slicing has a documented readability failure at the stairs in every game found. Project Zomboid players report that at the top of stairs "the entire room is revealed as if it just appears," with the rule "if your Z coordinate is 0.99 instead of 1.00, you are not the second floor" ([Steam, PZ](https://steamcommunity.com/app/108600/discussions/0/3459345750024537580)); two mods exist to fix it, one of which "renders the upper floor while you climb" ([Peek a View](https://catalogue.smods.ru/archives/430878), excerpt). The Sims offers walls up, cutaway and walls down plus manual floor stepping, and players still complain that cutaway "varies considerably if you have small rooms" ([EA Forums](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/the-sims-4-gameplay-en/request-to-add-another-walls-up-view/8654173), excerpt). Dwarf Fortress shows each level as a 2D plane with a coloured depth indicator ([Steam, DF](https://steamcommunity.com/app/975370/discussions/0/3716062978749212573)). Two recent games that wanted dense multi-floor interiors, Prison Architect 2 and Shadows of Doubt, left 2D rather than extend slicing ([TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2024/05/16/prison-architect-2-preview-incarcerating-the-third-dimension/); [Wireframe](https://wireframe.raspberrypi.com/articles/walking-the-voxelated-city-streets-in-shadows-of-doubt-a-fully-simulated)).

The renderer mechanics are the same for a web canvas as for these engines. Phaser's maintainer confirmed "Tiles have no depth," so tiles that must sort against the player become sprites ([Phaser Discourse](https://phaser.discourse.group/t/set-tile-depth-access-image-or-sprite-of-tile/13502)); PixiJS sorts by `zIndex` only among children of one container and warns that sorting "can be expensive for large numbers of children" ([PixiJS Guides](https://pixijs.com/8.x/guides/components/scene-objects/container)); Godot's `y_sort_origin` "allows, for example, to fake a different height level" ([Godot TileMapLayer](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_tilemaplayer.html)). Exult filters hit-testing by the same lift cutoff so the player cannot click hidden objects ([gamewin.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/exult/exult/master/gamewin.cc)).

| Presentation | What the renderer must have | Authoring it adds | Documented failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scene swap (Stardew, Pokémon) | Per-map camera bounds; a fade; an arrival tile per map | Nothing beyond the door record | Entry/exit warp ping-pong; NPCs invisible between maps |
| Roof fade, single cutoff (Exult) | A height on every drawable; a probe above the player; skip in paint and hit-test | Heights on every roof piece | Everything above the cutoff vanishes, neighbours included |
| Roof fade, per building (RuneScape, Godot) | One drawable group per building; alpha; trigger from footprint | A roof group and trigger per building | Trigger too large or missing |
| Floor slicing (PZ, Sims, DF) | Draw floor N; dim or hide N-1 and above; stair transition | Per-floor layers; wall modes | Hard flip at the stairs; small-room cutaway |

Inference. Under scene swap the exterior can only tell the player about an interior through indirect cues. Stardew ships lit windows and doors with hours, and leaves NPC tracking to the NPC Map Locations mod, which reviewers justify because the map is "complex enough to make wandering around stressful if you're looking for a specific NPC before a certain time" ([Nexus Mods 239](https://www.nexusmods.com/stardewvalley/mods/239), excerpt); vanilla 1.6 added real-time player positions to the world map but not villagers ([Version History](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Version_History)). Majora's Mask's Bombers' Notebook, a grid of people against time with event bars and promise stickers, is the in-game answer that shipped ([Zelda Wiki](https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/Bombers%27_Notebook)). A living-town game built on scene swap should budget for one of these from the start, because the legibility cost is the one players actually name.

## Validators and file snapshots replace the human walking the map

The roguelike community already runs layout validation without a human. DCSS's `validate` block runs after `SUBST` and `SHUFFLE`; returning false makes the builder reapply the random transforms and try again, and persistent failure discards the level. The stock checks are `validate {{ return glyphs_connected('{', '>') }}` and `has_exit_from_glyph('{')`, with `points_connected` and `num_matching_markers` also in the API ([DCSS advanced.txt](https://github.com/crawl/crawl/blob/master/crawl-ref/docs/develop/levels/advanced.txt)). Separately, "A vault that is completely inaccessible will fail to load," and wizard mode can force a level to contain a named vault for testing ([DCSS introduction.txt](https://github.com/crawl/crawl/blob/master/crawl-ref/docs/develop/levels/introduction.txt)). Spelunky never validates because its path algorithm guarantees exits by construction ([Spelunky Generator Lessons 1](https://tinysubversions.com/spelunkyGen/)). Unexplored keeps which key opens which lock in the output and uses it to detect and repair blockers ([Boris the Brave, Unexplored](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2021/04/10/dungeon-generation-in-unexplored/)). The industrial end is Rebellion's numeric planner that "plays entire games from start to finish like a player would" ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027236/AI-Summit-Automated-Game-Testing)); inference: for a 2D town a BFS over walkability plus a schedule check does the same job.

Stardew's own checks are the ones a door-graph town needs, and they are currently crashes rather than tests. The missing-warp exception at day start, the `GOTO` to a missing schedule key that "crashes or errors under SMAPI," and the note that a schedule time of 600 may not trigger movement ("use 610") are all load-time or first-tick conditions ([Modding:Schedule data](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Modding:Schedule_data)). SMAPI and Content Patcher report the structural ones (missing tilesheet paths, a `FromFile` matching a non-existent file) at load ([SMAPI log tutorial](https://stardewmodding.wiki.gg/wiki/Tutorial:_How_to_read_your_SMAPI_log)). The warp-consistency check comes from the Pokémon randomizer: each door is a set of warp events that must be mutually consistent unless explicitly marked one-way ([Archipelago warps.md](https://git.hollymcfarland.com/monorail/Archipelago/src/branch/main/worlds/pokemon_emerald/docs/warps.md)). LDtk's only built-in content constraints are entity `maxCount` with `limitScope` and field `min`/`max`/`regex`/`allowedRefs`; neither editor validates gameplay ([LDtk JSON_DOC.md](https://github.com/deepnight/ldtk/blob/master/docs/JSON_DOC.md)).

The review artefact is text. Vitest's `toMatchFileSnapshot('./test/basic.output.txt')` compares against a named external file with any extension, `vitest -u` updates, and in CI snapshots are never written so drift fails the build ([Vitest Snapshot guide](https://vitest.dev/guide/snapshot)). Cogmind exports maps as ASCII text that REXPaint imports, a precedent for ASCII as the shared review format ([Cogmind, ASCII morgue maps](https://www.moddb.com/news/building-the-ultimate-roguelike-morgue-file-part-2-ascii-maps)). For a picture without a display, `sharp.composite()` places one overlay per non-empty cell at `left = x*tw, top = y*th` from Node ([sharp composite](https://sharp.pixelplumbing.com/api-composite/)); LDtk's Super Simple Export does the same on save if the editor happens to be open ([LDtk Super simple export](https://ldtk.io/docs/game-dev/super-simple-export/)). Inference: the loop is watch, parse, validate, write `renders/<place>.txt` and `.png`, snapshot the text. Flood-fill room detection falls out of the glyph model (walls are a glyph class, rooms are components of non-wall cells, each component needs a door cell), and the Make it Home ablations say which furniture checks matter: a free path between every pair of doors, and nothing standing in a door's accessible space.

Two pitfalls from the research do not become validators but belong in the design notes. Stardew documents that a map wider than 200 tiles breaks layering ([Modding:Maps](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Modding:Maps)), which is a renderer-specific limit but a reminder that the compiler, not the author, should decide when an exterior place is too large. And RimWorld's advice that complex room shapes "run into map region problems" ([Rooms](https://www.rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Rooms)) is a warning against deriving rooms for gameplay; derive them for validation only, and let the authored place be the unit schedules address.

## Recommendations

1. Keep the graph of places joined by doors. Store a door as one record with both endpoints and an optional one-way flag, owned by the building or the town, never by a single place (the Ocarina of Time shape).
2. Simulate every NPC on real tiles in every place every tick, as Stardew does. At a few dozen NPCs and a few dozen places this is cheap and removes travel-time estimation. Add place-level LOD only if a profile shows a need.
3. Build the route table as a shortest-path search weighted by precomputed door-to-door distances within each place (HPA* style). Test that the chosen route does not change when door declaration order is shuffled.
4. Treat every schedule destination as a place id plus tile or object, and validate reachability, door permissions and travel time at load. Make these failures test failures, not runtime exceptions.
5. Author places as ASCII blocks in TypeScript files: a template string for the grid, a legend object that can bind a glyph to a stack, and door, object and stair declarations beside it. This is NetHack 3.7's shape and gives types without a parser for the outer structure.
6. Write the autotiler yourself, as a Wang-set resolver for floors and ground plus a small pattern matcher (LDtk's semantics) for walls. Compile to the renderer's own layers first; add Tiled or LDtk export only if a human wants to open the map in an editor.
7. Make the ASCII render the review artefact. Snapshot `renders/<place>.txt` with Vitest file snapshots, generate a PNG with sharp for occasional human review, and keep the itinerary trace and travel-time matrix as text.
8. Present interiors with scene swap. Give each place camera bounds and an arrival tile, and a fade on the door. Do not build roof fading or floor slicing now; both add per-building authoring and both have documented readability failures.
9. Budget for NPC legibility from the first playable: villager markers on the world map (what Stardew 1.6 did for players) or a notebook of people against time (Majora's Mask). The research shows this is the cost players name under scene swap.
10. Allow interiors to be any size. Record the footprint-to-interior ratio in the render output so a reviewer can see it, but do not fail on it.
11. Keep exterior zones as separate places joined by edge records with a direction and offset, even if there is only one zone at first. This keeps the Pokémon-style stitched exterior available later without re-authoring.
12. Use procedural generation to draft, not to ship: a treemap room split and a greedy furniture pass that honours door paths and wall adjacency, with the resulting room graph written into the place file for the validators to check. Edit the draft as ASCII.

## Open decisions

1. Whether the door record lives in the building file (natural for footprint door and stairs) or in a town-level file (natural for zone-to-zone edges), or both with a validator joining them.
2. Whether NPCs are drawn while between places. Under scene swap they vanish at a door and reappear at the arrival tile; the sources do not say how Stardew presents this and no better convention was found.
3. Whether to derive rooms by flood fill only for validation, or also to expose them to schedules ("be in the kitchen") as sub-places. The Sims and RimWorld evidence argues for validation only.
4. Whether the per-leg grid search has a cap at all. Stardew's cap silently nulls long paths; no cap risks a slow tick on a pathological place. A test asserting the longest in-place path is the middle option.
5. Whether to add a declared `floor` index per place now (needed for stacked renders and for a later LDtk `worldDepth` export) or to infer it from stair doors.
6. Which legibility feature ships first: world-map markers or a people-by-time notebook. They need different data (current position versus full schedule) and the notebook commits the schedule format to being displayable.
7. Whether the exterior starts as one place or several zones. The research supports either; the edge-record format is the part to settle now.
8. How much of the autotiler to build before the first renderer: Wang sets only, or Wang sets plus pattern rules for wall faces.

## Conclusion

The research narrows the design question more than expected. Every game examined, including the ones marketed as continuous worlds, keeps a graph of places and portals somewhere in its data or derives one at runtime, so the proposal's central choice turns out to be between an authored graph whose errors surface at load and a derived graph whose errors surface as a stuck NPC, with the continuous world as a presentation option on top of either. For an agent working without a screen, the authored graph is the one whose failures can be turned into tests, and the Stardew codebase already enumerates those failures as exceptions and troubleshooting entries.

The second shift is about where the effort goes. The data model and the ASCII format are the easy part; every roguelike format converges on the same grammar and the editor JSON is small enough to write directly. The real work is the validator list and the route table, because those are what Stardew's modding community spends its troubleshooting pages on, and the one unsolved cost of scene swap is that the player cannot see where anyone is. That last item is a design task, and it should be scheduled alongside the layout work rather than after it.
