# Author the living town as inspectable data

Every shipped living world and every reusable research system in these notes converges on the same shape, and that shape is plain data a coding agent can write and test: one authored base routine per character resolved once per day, a priority-ordered override layer for events, relationships stored as a ledger of named, timed modifiers, memories stored as a tagged event log, and dialogue picked by most-specific-match rules over a bag of facts. No shipped non-LLM game simulates the content of NPC-to-NPC conversation; "gossip" is always propagation of numbers or discrete facts with authored lines keyed to them. Needs-driven utility selection (The Sims lineage) is the established way to fill gaps between scheduled activities, and planners (GOAP, HTN) are reserved for dependency chaining rather than daily life. For your constraints (TypeScript, pnpm, Cloudflare, an agent with no screen) the stack that fits is a dependency-free simulation package with a fixed tick and one seeded RNG, tested in Node, rendered by a thin PixiJS or Excalibur layer, and deployed as static assets; the pattern is proven in TypeScript by hayao.js, OpenFrontIO and Clockwork Engine. LLM use belongs at authoring time (frontier model, schema-constrained, validated, human-read) and in bounded runtime scenes where the model returns text plus a closed set of flags, which is the shape of every LLM feature that survived contact with players (Where Winds Meet, PUBG Ally, Ubisoft Teammates). The experimental frontier is per-character fallible beliefs, story sifting, and emergent drama without authored exchanges; the research record shows those producing mundane output (Talk of the Town's gossip was about hair color) unless a human or the player's own situated interest curates them.

## Shipped games solve daily life with a schedule and an override stack

Stardew Valley, the vibe you named, is almost entirely authored data. Each NPC has one schedule file whose entries are slash-delimited points of `<time> [location] <tileX> <tileY> [facing] [animation] [dialogue]`, and the game picks exactly one entry per day from a fixed priority ladder: festival, then date, then rain (with a 50% `rain2` variant), then season plus weekday plus hearts, then weekday, then season, then a mandatory `spring` fallback whose removal "WILL BREAK THE GAME" ([Stardew Valley Wiki, Modding:Schedule data](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Modding:Schedule_data)). There is no needs model and no utility scoring. The authoring unit is a whole day for a whole condition, and an NPC cannot be diverted mid-day. The soap opera comes from **250 friendship points per heart**, daily decay (−2 for most villagers, −20 for a spouse), gift deltas (+80 loved, −40 hated, ×8 on birthdays), and dialogue commands like `$t` that plant a conversation topic and auto-create `_memory_` topics **1, 7, 14, 28, 56 and 104 days later** so a line can refer back to something that happened weeks ago ([Friendship](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Friendship); [Modding:Dialogue](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Modding:Dialogue)). The costs of this design are visible in the modding wiki: season × weekday × hearts × rain × marriage × festival is hand-written per path, `<dayOfMonth>` dialogue keys silently work only in year one, and a bad `GOTO` crashes the game ([Modding:Schedule data](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Modding:Schedule_data)).

Ultima VII, your other touchstone, took the opposite trade. `SCHEDULE.DAT` stores **4 bytes per entry**: 5 bits of activity type (32 types such as Sleep, Eat, Tend Shop, Blacksmith, Patrol, Preach, Waiter, Loiter), 3 bits selecting one of eight 3-hour slots, and a location; each of 256 NPCs has 1 to 8 entries ([Ultima Codex, SCHEDULE.DAT](https://www.wiki.ultimacodex.com/wiki/Ultima_VII_internal_formats_%E2%80%93_SCHEDULE.DAT)). The engine owns what each activity means, so authoring is cheap and every new activity costs engine code. Exceptions (intro scripts, quest states) live in usecode and "eggs" outside the schedule file, and Exult developers report that this override layer was the hard part to find and debug, along with the lack of a Z coordinate for multi-floor routines ([Exult forum](https://exult.sourceforge.io/forum/viewtopic.php?p=19195)). The Ultima Codex design essay distills the era's advice: at minimum move each NPC between work and home on a day/night cycle, build routines for classes of NPC and layer a few unique traits on top, keep NPCs findable about 14 of 24 hours, and avoid routines so specific they "would lead to very crowded cemeteries" ([Digital Lycaeum, NPC Schedules](https://lycaeum.ultimacodex.com/npc-schedules/)).

Bethesda's Radiant AI is the cautionary version of the override stack. What shipped in Oblivion and Skyrim is a cascading list of AI packages (sleep, eat, travel, sandbox) with hour-block schedules and conditions, evaluated top-down so "the first package that is found valid based on its time schedule and logical conditions is selected" ([UESP CK wiki, Package](https://ck.uesp.net/wiki/Package_(Form))). The needs-driven emergence shown at E3 2005 was damped on purpose: Emil Pagliarulo described skooma addicts murdering a quest-critical dealer, and the team reduced theft and destruction goals because they "broke stuff" ([GamesBeat, 2010](https://gamesbeat.com/dimming-the-radiant-ai-in-oblivion/)). Bruce Nesmith's later lessons are the ones to keep: "just because a response was reasonable in the real world, it doesn't mean it's good in a game", reactions were overused at first (dropping an item brought "half the town"), and the smoother Skyrim version was noticed less than the quirky Oblivion one ([GamesBeat, 2012](https://gamesbeat.com/bethesdas-nesmith-reflects-on-the-difficult-birth-of-skyrims-radiant-story-system/); [FRVR, 2025](https://frvr.com/blog/news/elder-scrolls-designer-explains-that-radiant-ai-is-an-enormous-investment-for-bethesda-but-every-improvement-actually-makes-it-less-noticeable-to-players/)). Modders note that package debugging is tedious enough to need dedicated tools and that the "Once Per Day" flag silently fails when a package has a schedule ([Beyond Skyrim, NPC Packages](https://wiki.beyondskyrim.org/wiki/Arcane_University:NPC_Packages)).

The large modern systems push intelligence into the environment. Warhorse's 2015 paper on Kingdom Come: Deliverance states the problem plainly: script codebases become unmanageable and the CPU budget was about **5 ms per frame for all NPCs**, which rules out planning for ambient behavior. Their answer was behavior objects: smart objects (chairs, beds), smart areas (pubs, fields, cities) in parent-child hierarchies, quest objects that change daily routines, and "situations" (short multi-NPC eye candy, cleaned up if any participant aborts), injected as behavior-tree subtrees under subsumption-style priorities (ambient, combat, situations). They measured under 1 ms average with 30 complex NPCs, and a scripter placed about 60 smart areas and 200 smart objects in two days ([Černý et al., arXiv 1508.00377](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1508.00377)). Hitman splits knowledge into public world state and private per-NPC knowledge, with periodic shared "services" that can go stale, and coordinates groups through "situations" without direct NPC-to-NPC messaging ([Game Developer, The AI of Hitman](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-ai-of-hitman-2016-)). Watch Dogs: Legion's Census is a relational database that generates schedules from a profile and propagates memories and affinity along family networks ([Ubisoft News, 2021](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/4po3S9Pwp1YcgBmGPmQxAh/watch-dogs-legion-the-tools-that-built-london)). Shadows of Doubt computes each citizen's schedule in a short calculation before each day starts, then allows deviation when events call for it ([ColePowered DevBlog #8](https://colepowered.com/?p=34142)), which is Stardew's whole-day authoring, computed.

The Sims is the needs-based alternative and its designers' own lessons cut against deep simulation. Objects "advertise" satisfaction of motives, the Sim scores advertisements against its current motive levels, and the stated benefit was adding new objects later without the Sims knowing about them ([Forbus course outline, The Sims under the hood](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~forbus/c95-gd/lectures/The_Sims_Under_the_Hood_files/outline.htm)). Matt Brown's AIIDE 2006 talk on The Sims 2 described the design as "Big A, little i": a detailed model of which urinal a Sim would pick did not reliably produce the behavior, a random number generator made players equally willing to invent stories, players forget quickly, and authoring tools mattered more than the AI ([kmjn.org notes](https://kmjn.org/notes/sims2_ai.html)). The recurring bug class across all of these is resource contention: Shenmue's whole population went to one convenience store for breakfast and got stuck, fixed with an occupancy cap ([Game Developer, Shenmue postmortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/yu-suzuki-recounts-the-making-of-i-shenmue-i-)), and KCD II "ran out of beds" and sends NPCs out of the city at night ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/kingdom-come-deliverance-2-brings-reactive-world-more-ethnicities/)).

| Game | Routine representation | Exceptions | Relationship model | Scale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stardew Valley | Per-NPC keyed day scripts, tile-level points | Most-specific key wins; no mid-day interruption | Single scalar per NPC (250/heart), decay, topics | ~30 villagers |
| Ultima VII | 4-byte (activity, 3-hour slot, location) entries | Usecode and eggs outside the file | None simulated | 256 slots |
| Oblivion/Skyrim | Ordered package list, hour blocks, conditions | First valid package wins; sandbox fallback | Disposition values | Hundreds |
| KCD / KCD II | Day cycles over smart objects and areas | Subsumption subbrains; situations | Per-NPC experiences, friendliness | ~600 active; ~2,400 |
| Hitman | FSM crowd plus 300+ behaviour-tree NPCs | Situations and services | Public vs private knowledge | 1,200 to 2,000+ crowd |
| The Sims 1-4 | Motives scored against object advertisements | Pushed interactions (fire, carpool) | Sims 2 memories; Sims 3 traits; Sims 4 emotions | Household |
| Dwarf Fortress / RimWorld / CK3 | Needs and jobs | Mental breaks, storyteller events | Per-pair ledger of named timed modifiers | Dozens to thousands |

## Research systems supply the social layer at a known authoring price

The shipped games above model relationships to the player and little else. The soap-opera layer (NPC-to-NPC desire, rejection, history-aware reactions) comes from a research lineage that has been rebuilt three times into reusable form. Comme il Faut, the engine under Prom Week, keeps a deliberately thin character model: immutable binary traits, temporary binary statuses (some directional, "angry at"), binary relationships (friends, dating, enemies) and directed scalar networks (buddy, romance, cool). A "social exchange" is one character's attempt to change social state with another; desirability is the sum of weights from authored influence rules, the responder's rules decide accept or reject, and authored "instantiations" retarget the same performance to any pair whose preconditions hold. Every event is logged in a Social Facts Database with timestamp, labels ("cruel", "embarrassing") and a realization string, so a later line can say "after you made scandalous remarks about my mother?" The price: **over 5,000 influence rules for 18 characters**, tamed by grouping rules into "microtheories" after a "big bag of rules" produced contradictions ([McCoy et al., CiF, IEEE TCIAIG 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)). Ensemble is its generalized successor, a standalone `ensemble.js` library with a JSON schema (categories, types, `directionType`, min/max, `duration` where 0 models an event rather than a state) and volition and trigger rule files whose weights sum ([Ensemble GitHub](https://github.com/ensemble-engine/ensemble); [Ensemble wiki, Schema](https://github.com/ensemble-engine/ensemble/wiki/Schema)).

Versu, built by Richard Evans and Emily Short, is the strongest precedent for content that is reusable across characters. The whole world state is sentences in an "exclusion logic" database (`brown.in!dining_room`, where `!` means the only value, so asserting a new location deletes the old one), which the authors chose because "nothing is hidden" and the inspector can explain why a precondition failed. Behavior comes from concurrent, role-agnostic "social practices" (dinner, flirtation, a politics argument) that offer affordances; agents pick actions by one-step lookahead against utility-weighted wants. They report a 20-minute episode taking about two months versus Façade's three person-years, and "more than 1000 parameterized actions, authored in one year" ([Evans & Short, IEEE TCIAIG 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)). Linden Lab cancelled it in 2014 and refused to sell it back ([Game Developer, The end of Versu](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/the-end-of-versu-emily-short-looks-back)), but Praxish (2023, vanilla JS, open source) reconstructs the logic layer and the practice JSON format (`id`, `roles`, `actions` with `conditions` and `outcomes`), and reports two example storyworlds in "only a few hundred lines" of JSON ([Dameris et al., Praxish, AIIDE 2023](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/27537/27310)). Versu's own pain points are the ones to expect: tuning "over 300 desires" was "difficult and unrewarding", concurrent practices made scoring and debugging hard, and agents shared one world model so misunderstandings "cannot be implemented fully" ([Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)).

Talk of the Town is the deepest published model of fallible knowledge. Each character holds a mental model of every person and place made of "belief facets" with an evidence list, a strength, provenance pointers and a predecessor chain; evidence comes in eleven types (observation, statement, eavesdropping, confabulation, lie, mutation, forgetting, and so on), and gossip exchanges beliefs about the people most salient to both conversants. The authors describe it as "not very computationally efficient" (each NPC holds 800 to 1,200 facets) and admit "it is not easy for us to articulate how this architecture could be utilized for games in mainstream genres" ([Ryan & Mateas, Game AI Pro 3 ch. 37](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter37_Simulating_Character_Knowledge_Phenomena_in_Talk_of_the_Town.pdf)). Emily Short's reading of the dissertation records the sobering result: characters "mostly talk about things like other characters' hair and eye color", and "knowledge simulation doesn't naturally produce narrative intrigue" ([Emily Short, 2019](https://emshort.blog/2019/05/28/curating-simulated-storyworlds-james-ryan-ch-6f/)). The drama in Bad News came from a human operator sifting the simulation live for an improv actor ([UChicago CCCT](https://ccct.uchicago.edu/events/trauma-and-the-aesthetics-of-life-bad-news/)). WAWLT, from the same school, made the pragmatic simplification you should copy: assume all characters know every event, but give each character only a handful of subjective "impressions" (up to three positive and three negative per relationship, each pointing at its cause), and let gossip copy impressions rather than facts ([Kreminski et al., WAWLT, FDG 2020](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/WAWLT_FDG2020.pdf)).

The content-side formalism practitioners converge on is the storylet: content plus prerequisites plus effects, "simple, atomic, robust, and recombinable" ([Emily Short, Storylets](https://emshort.blog/2019/11/29/storylets-you-want-them/)). Wildermyth's implementation over procedural heroes is the best shipped template: roles are cast in order with score thresholds, inputs query personality, relationships and "hooks", and outputs plant new hooks "to start follow-up stories", with the guideline that options should "differ in kind, not just magnitude" ([Wildermyth wiki, Story Inputs and Outputs](https://wildermyth.com/wiki/Story_Inputs_and_Outputs)). Salience-based selection, where the most specific matching piece wins with broader pieces as fallback, is Elan Ruskin's Left 4 Dead design and is also the default in Yarn Spinner 3 and in storylet-framework's `useSpecificity` ([Emily Short on Ruskin's GDC 2012 talk](https://emshort.blog/2012/03/16/gdc-2012-talk-on-dynamic-dialogue/); [Yarn Spinner saliency primer](https://docs.yarnspinner.dev/write-yarn-scripts/advanced-scripting/storylets-and-saliency-a-primer)). Its known weakness is pacing, because it "lacks an authored dramatic arc" ([Emily Short, Beyond Branching](https://emshort.blog/2016/04/12/beyond-branching-quality-based-and-salience-based-narrative-structures/)); the cheapest fix on record is Left 4 Dead's director loop (estimate intensity, Build Up, Sustain Peak, Peak Fade, Relax) rather than a planning drama manager ([Booth, The AI Systems of Left 4 Dead](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)).

Two negative results bound the experimental end. Chroniqueur (FDG 2026) simulated 61 years of a settlement into 228K events and tested story-sifting heuristics (improbability, Polti's dramatic situations) against 24 readers; "the results of this user evaluation do not show that our story heuristics have any significant relationship with reading enjoyment", affairs were "too prevalent ... in spite of multiple attempts to tone it down", and the authors conclude emergent narratives need readers already familiar with the world ([Lessard et al., Narrative Gold Digging](https://www.pcgworkshop.com/archive/lessard2026narrative.pdf)). And Façade, the one interactive drama that shipped, needed about 2,500 hand-written joint dialog behaviors across 27 beats, "~3 person years of just authoring", and still fell short on "clearly communicating the state of the social games to the player" ([Mateas & Stern, AIIDE 2005](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIIDE/2005/AIIDE05-016.pdf)). That legibility problem has a name, the Tale-Spin effect, for systems that "fail to represent internal system richness on their surfaces" ([Wardrip-Fruin, Grand Text Auto](https://grandtextauto.soe.ucsc.edu/2008/02/25/ep-56-re-reading-tale-spin)); Prom Week answered it by showing each character's ranked desires in a thought bubble, Versu with clickable emotion portraits, and Dwarf Fortress found a cosmetic trait "was judged a failure because players could not act on it" ([Aaron Reed on Dwarf Fortress](https://if50.substack.com/p/2006-dwarf-fortress)).

| System | Reusable artifact | Status |
|---|---|---|
| CiF / Ensemble | JSON schema of traits, statuses, networks; weighted volition and trigger rules; tagged event history | Open source (`ensemble.js`, BSD); thin docs |
| Versu / Praxish | Exclusion-logic fact DB; role-agnostic practices as JSON; utility wants | Praxish open source, research quality |
| Talk of the Town / Neighborly | Belief facets with evidence and provenance; "implant" trick; Neighborly ECS reconstruction | Research; author calls it inefficient |
| Kismet | Trait-to-tag proclivities, location roles with casting ranges, `heard` knowledge, patterns | Sketch, Prolog/ASP backed |
| Storylets (Wildermyth, Fallen London) | Content + prerequisites + effects, role casting, hooks | Shipped practice |
| Felt / Winnow | Declarative sifting patterns over an event log | Research; null result on absolute heuristics |
| Left 4 Dead director | Intensity estimate, four-phase pacing loop | Shipped practice |

## A pure TypeScript simulation core fits an agent that cannot see the screen

The engine question matters less than the architecture question. Three open TypeScript projects already implement what you need: hayao.js is "a headless-native simulation kernel" where rendering is a plugin that observes and cannot change results, randomness goes through a seeded RNG, and a canonical state hash supports replay checks ([hayao.js.org](https://hayao.js.org/)); OpenFrontIO runs its dependency-free core in a Web Worker separate from PixiJS, forbids wall-clock access, and gets replays from determinism ([OpenFrontIO architecture](https://www.mintlify.com/openfrontio/OpenFrontIO/development/architecture)); Clockwork Engine has a fixed-frame loop, seeded PRNG, recording and playback, and swappable PIXI or headless rendering ([clockwork-engine](https://ithub.global.ssl.fastly.net/hiddentao/clockwork-engine)). The determinism discipline underneath is Fiedler's fixed timestep ("the same inputs produce the same simulation results regardless of render framerate") ([Gaffer On Games](https://gafferongames.com/post/fix_your_timestep/)) and Factorio's per-tick CRC with tagged snapshots so the first divergent tick pinpoints the bug ([FFF #47](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-47)). With integer ticks (one per in-game minute is enough for Stardew-style 10-minute schedule granularity) and a single seeded RNG threaded through every system, the same package runs in Node for tests and later in a Cloudflare Worker or Durable Object without a rewrite.

For the renderer the choice is between a renderer-only library and a small engine. PixiJS v8 is the most mature piece of infrastructure here (WebGPU in core since 2024, v8.20.0 in summer 2026) but it is a renderer; tilemaps, camera and map loading are yours ([PixiJS v8 launch](https://pixijs.com/blog/pixi-v8-launches); [GameDev.net on 8.20.0](https://gamedev.net/news/5221-pixijs-v8200-released/)). Excalibur is the one TypeScript engine whose own release process is vitest unit tests plus Playwright visual tests with a Dev Container, and it ships first-party LDtk and Tiled loaders; its latest release is v0.32.0 (23 Dec 2025), it is still 0.x with breaking releases, and no 2026 release was found ([Excalibur v0.32.0](https://github.com/excaliburjs/Excalibur/releases/tag/v0.32.0); [LDtk plugin](https://excaliburjs.com/docs/ldtk-plugin); [Tiled plugin](https://excaliburjs.com/docs/tiled-plugin)). Phaser 4 is the largest ecosystem but v4.0.0 shipped 10 April 2026 with patch releases still landing (4.2.1 on 9 July 2026), 4.x tutorials and plugins are thinner than v3's, and the Phaser site blocked programmatic fetches during this research ([phaser.io/download/phaser4](https://phaser.io/download/phaser4); [phaser.io/download/stable](https://phaser.io/download/stable)). Kaplay's 4000 line has been in alpha since mid-2025 with API breaks in nearly every alpha, and the stable 3001 line is frozen ([Kaplay releases](https://github.com/kaplayjs/kaplay/releases)). melonJS is maintained but small ([I Programmer](https://www.i-programmer.info/news/167-javascript/18949-melonjs-browser-based-2d-game-engine.html)).

| Engine | Latest stable (date) | Headless/testing story | Map import | Fit for this project |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PixiJS | 8.20.0 (summer 2026) | None built in; pair with a pure sim core | Hand-written | Strong, if you own tilemap/camera |
| Excalibur | 0.32.0 (Dec 2025) | vitest + Playwright visual, Dev Container | First-party LDtk, Tiled | Strong; pin the version |
| Phaser | 4.2.1 (Jul 2026) | No documented headless mode | Tiled (4.x status unverified) | Large ecosystem, young v4 |
| Kaplay | 3001.0.19 (Jun 2025); 4000 alpha | None documented | ASCII levels only | Weak |
| Godot 4.7 | 4.7.2 (Aug 2026) | `--headless`, CLI export; gdUnit4 reports xvfb slowness | Tiled to `.tscn` | Possible but no TS, slower loop |
| Bevy 0.18 | Jan 2026 | Headless community pattern | Third party | Rust; migration per release |
| Ebitengine 2.10 | Sep 2026 | Virtualization mode for automated testing | Hand-written | Go; single wasm command |

Godot deserves a sentence because a code-only loop is real: `--headless` plus `--script`, `--import` then `--export-release`, and single-threaded web export is the default since 4.3 so no COOP/COEP headers are needed ([Godot CLI tutorial](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/editor/command_line_tutorial.html); [Godot web export docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/ja/4.5/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)). But the agent would hand-write `.tscn` text with no editor validation, and gdUnit4 users report headless input problems worked around with xvfb that made CI "very slow" ([gdUnit4Net discussion](https://github.com/godot-gdunit-labs/gdUnit4Net/discussions/350)). Given that you already live in TypeScript, the Godot route buys nothing you cannot get from a TS sim core and costs you the Node test loop.

Verification without a screen should be text first. The highest-value tests are ASCII or JSON dumps of the map, NPC positions and schedules from the pure simulation, asserted in vitest; screenshots are a secondary check. When you do screenshot, Chromium needs explicit SwiftShader flags (`--use-gl=angle --use-angle=swiftshader-webgl --enable-unsafe-swiftshader`) ([Chromium SwiftShader docs](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src.git/+/lkgr-ios-internal/docs/gpu/swiftshader.md)), and on Linux without a display WebGL can "silently degrade to a 2D fallback" so the screenshot succeeds and is wrong but plausible; check the renderer before capturing ([microlink, WebGL without a GPU](https://microlink.io/blog/webgl-without-a-gpu)). Baselines must be captured in the same headless environment used for comparison ([createit, headless Chrome WebGL](https://www.createit.com/?p=3319)), and a Phaser testing skill recommends exposing a `window.__TEST__` hook so Playwright can set deterministic state ([playwright-testing skill](https://skillselion.com/skills/chongdashu/phaserjs-oakwoods/playwright-testing)). Keep pixel-diff tests few and coarse.

Maps and art have agent-friendly paths. LDtk's JSON (schema 1.5.3) can be written or read directly, and its Super Simple Export emits per-level PNG, CSV and small JSON ([LDtk JSON docs](https://ldtk.io/json); [LDtk game-dev docs](https://ldtk.io/docs/game-dev/)); Tiled's CLI exporter needs an X server on Linux (`xvfb-run tiled --export-map`) ([Tiled export manual](https://doc.mapeditor.org/en/stable/manual/export/)), so the agent should write Tiled or LDtk JSON from code, or generate maps in the game's own format and skip both editors. Kenney's 30,000+ assets are CC0 ([Kenney license](https://github.com/ETdoFresh/kenney.nl); [Defold Kenney listing](https://defold.com/assets/kenney)); the Universal LPC character generator gives far more top-down RPG character variety but mixes CC-BY-SA, CC-BY, OGA-BY and GPL and exports a credits sheet you must ship ([LPC generator](https://github.com/LiberatedPixelCup/Universal-LPC-Spritesheet-Character-Generator)). The US Copyright Office's January 2025 report holds that purely AI-generated output is not copyrightable, though human arrangement or modification can be ([Sidley summary](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2025/02/07/u-s-copyright-office-issues-report-on-artificial-intelligence-and-copyrightability/)); treat generated sprites as gap-fillers you do not own.

Deployment is the easy part. A TS bundle is static assets on Cloudflare Pages with no new infrastructure; only Godot threaded exports and love.js require COOP/COEP headers ([Godot web export docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/ja/4.5/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html); [2dengine/love.js](https://github.com/2dengine/love.js)). For dialogue, ink is the only major language with an official, typed JS runtime and an in-process compiler (`new inkjs.Compiler(src).Compile()`), so agent-generated scenes can be compiled and played in a test without inklecate ([inkjs README](https://github.com/y-lohse/inkjs)); Yarn Spinner 3 has the nicest storylet features but no official JS runtime ([docs.yarnspinner.dev](https://docs.yarnspinner.dev/)), and storylet-framework's author says he is "unlikely to expand or maintain this library" ([storylet-framework README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wildwinter/storylet-framework/main/README.md)). JSON storylet decks you own are the easiest format for an agent to generate, schema-validate and unit test.

## Represent people with few traits, ledgers, tagged memories and keyed schedules

**Personality.** The shipped evidence favors few, legible, mechanically bound traits over many axes. Dwarf Fortress has roughly 50 facets on a 0 to 100 bell curve with a 78% neutral band and effects the wiki repeatedly marks "not entirely understood" ([DF Wiki, Personality trait](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Personality_trait)); Crusader Kings III capped personality traits at three after CK2 let characters "pile up traits with little effect" ([GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/articles/15647/)); RimWorld simplified characterization "so each trait affects gameplay" ([Game Developer, RimWorld](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-rimworld-i-fleshes-out-the-i-dwarf-fortress-i-formula)). Encode traits as tags that feed rules and utility weights (Kismet's `extrovert: +++(talking)` is the compact form), and give each character a long prose document besides, because the "interview-based" generative-agent result and Ubisoft's "thousands of words" per character both say that document is the highest-leverage authoring artifact ([arXiv 2411.10109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109v1); [GamesBeat on Teammates](https://gamesbeat.com/?p=310806)).

**Motivations.** Model needs as considerations in a utility system, used only to fill time the schedule leaves open or to pick among a schedule entry's options (which tavern, which bench). Dave Mark's Infinite Axis Utility System scores each action as the product of considerations normalized to [0,1] through response curves, so any zero vetoes an action; Guild Wars 2 built over a hundred reusable consideration patterns that designers combined "in a mere handful of minutes" ([Game AI Pro 3 ch. 13](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter13_Choosing_Effective_Utility-Based_Considerations.pdf)). Guard against oscillation from day one: commitment bonuses, cooldown and runtime curves, or The Sims Medieval rule of deciding only when the action queue is empty ([Game AI Pro ch. 9](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter09_An_Introduction_to_Utility_Theory.pdf)). Keep GOAP out of daily life: Orkin lists "no scheduling facility" among its limits ([Orkin 2005](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/orkin2005agent.pdf)), and Merrill's team dropped a planner as "too organic and mysterious for the designers' comfort" ([Game AI Pro ch. 10](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter10_Building_Utility_Decisions_into_Your_Existing_Behavior_Tree.pdf)). If you need dependency chains (fetch key, unlock shed, feed goats), HTN compound tasks in Humphreys' plain-text notation with a Method Traversal Record for explainable replans are the readable option ([Game AI Pro ch. 12](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter12_Exploring_HTN_Planners_through_Example.pdf)).

**Relationships.** Store per-pair, directed relationships as a ledger of named, timed modifiers that sum to a scalar, in the CK3 and RimWorld style (Friend +60, "Broke Truce −50 (3 years)", insult −15 for 20 days), with most entries decaying to zero by expiry ([CK3 Wiki, Opinion](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Opinion); [RimWorld Wiki, Social](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Social)). This makes "why does she dislike me" explainable in UI, which is the legibility fix the research strand keeps asking for. Add a small set of binary relationship labels (friends, dating, rivals, family) and family base opinions (parent/child +30, sibling +20 in RimWorld). Follow Versu's rules for change: long-term changes "require active choice from both characters", short-term changes "happen frequently", and "it should never be possible for an NPC unilaterally to change his or her relationship to the player" ([Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)).

**Knowledge and memory.** Use two stores. First, a world event log where every event carries a timestamp, participants, tags and a realization template (CiF's SFDB), which rules can query in "true now", "true in history" and "times true" modes ([CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)). Second, per-NPC private knowledge as a bounded list of "impressions" or facts with a confidence, a source, a timestamp and an expiry, which is Orkin's WorkingMemoryFact (16 attributes each with confidence 0 to 1, and his regret that stale facts lacked expiration times) and Hitman's public/private split ([Orkin 2005](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/orkin2005agent.pdf); [Game Developer, Hitman](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-ai-of-hitman-2016-)). Gossip is then a rule that copies an impression from one NPC to another when they are co-located and friendly, with the recipient recording "heard from X at Y", which is the Legion family-network propagation at village scale ([Ubisoft News, 2021](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/4po3S9Pwp1YcgBmGPmQxAh/watch-dogs-legion-the-tools-that-built-london)). Full belief facets with mutation and confabulation are the experimental option and the record says they produce mundane content for a lot of compute.

**Schedules.** Combine Stardew's key ladder with Ultima VII's activity types. A schedule entry should be `(time window, activity type, location or smart object, optional dialogue key)`, where activity type is a verb the engine knows (sleep, eat, tend shop, loiter, visit) rather than a tile, and the day's entry list is resolved once at dawn from conditions (season, weekday, weather, flags, relationship buckets) by most-specific-key-wins with a required fallback. Add Bethesda-style per-entry conditions and a sandbox fallback at the bottom of every list. Give every location a capacity and every NPC a fallback activity, because Shenmue and KCD II both broke on contention ([Game Developer, Shenmue](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/yu-suzuki-recounts-the-making-of-i-shenmue-i-); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/kingdom-come-deliverance-2-brings-reactive-world-more-ethnicities/)). Interruptions follow the ambient/latent model from ScriptEase: a game event preempts the ambient behavior with a higher-priority latent behavior, and the ambient behavior resumes when the latent one completes ([AAAI 2007 ScriptEase demo](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/18798/18798-52-22506-1-10-20210929.pdf)). Hold resume state in a goal stack of (goal, resume-info) frames so a villager interrupted on the way to the well goes back to the well.

**Places and objects.** Put affordances on the world, not in brains. The minimal smart-object record for a 2D town is id, position and slots (tile plus facing), affordances (verb, need deltas, duration, preconditions, effects), capacity and reservation, and an advertisement function of the querying NPC, which is what Unreal's Smart Objects package today as slots with claim and release ([UE Smart Objects](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/smart-objects-in-unreal-engine)) and what KCD's smart areas add in hierarchy (the pub defers to the city for "pray") ([arXiv 1508.00377](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1508.00377)). Adding a new object with affordances then adds behavior to every NPC without touching a brain.

**Dialogue and barks.** Select lines Valve-style: assemble a bag of facts (concept, speaker, listener, relationship bucket, location, time, recent events, impressions), score every rule by matched criteria with required criteria as hard filters, and pick the highest, with ties broken randomly; Mímir is a small Rust reimplementation that shows how little code this is ([Valve Developer Community, Response System](https://developer.valvesoftware.com/wiki/Response_System); [lukecarr/mimir](https://github.com/lukecarr/mimir)). Convert scalars into buckets (`opinion_bucket = hostile|cool|warm|friend`) before they enter rules, or you get threshold soup. Use ink (via inkjs) for authored scenes with choices, tunnels and state, and a JSON storylet deck for barks and ambient reactions, with the simulation as the fact source for both.

**Pathing and scale.** Grid A* with a Manhattan heuristic on a small town map is cheap per NPC; for popular destinations (tavern, well, church) precompute a Dijkstra map per destination so everyone heading there gets O(1) steering, and recompute when doors or capacities change ([Red Blob, A*](https://www.redblobgames.com/pathfinding/a-star/introduction.html); [Red Blob, Tower defense pathfinding](https://www.redblobgames.com/pathfinding/tower-defense/)). At Stardew scale (dozens of villagers the player can watch) you do not need the Ubisoft three-tier LOD (virtual NPCs updating every 5 to 15 s, bulk once per second, real every frame) ([GameRevolution on AC Origins](https://www.gamerevolution.com/news/386683-ubisoft-talks-about-building-assassins-creed-origins-world-through-ai)), but design the schedule layer so off-screen NPCs can advance by waypoint rather than by tile if you ever grow past a hundred.

## LLMs pay off at authoring time and inside bounded scenes

The always-on generative-agent pattern is the expensive one, and its cheap descendants reinvent scripts. Smallville ran 25 agents for two game days at a cost of "thousands of dollars in token credits" on gpt-3.5-turbo, "sequentially in roughly real-time game time", and its documented failures (retrieval misses, embellishment, world-knowledge leakage, agents "overly cooperative" from instruction tuning, picking a bar for lunch) are the ones shipped games still fight ([Park et al., arXiv 2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)). Affordable Generative Agents cut cost by a "Lifestyle Policy" that reuses routine plans instead of regenerating them ([arXiv 2402.02053](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02053v2)); Lyfe Agents claim 10 to 100× lower cost by choosing high-level options rarely and cheap actions within them ([arXiv 2310.02172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02172v1)). Both are scripted schedules by another name. AI Town, the TypeScript port, contributes the right skeleton for your case: a deterministic single-threaded engine owns state and processes queued inputs, and LLM calls run as asynchronous "agent operations" that never write game state directly and submit results through the same input queue a player uses ([AI Town ARCHITECTURE.md](https://github.com/a16z-infra/ai-town/blob/main/ARCHITECTURE.md)).

Every LLM feature that survived players has the same shape. PUBG Ally's "System 1" behavior tree runs movement and combat at tick rate and can preempt the "System 2" LLM, which chooses from a bounded tool interface; Krafton's lesson is that "language-model deliberation cannot keep pace with game ticks, so the fast control layer must run independently" ([alphaxiv 2609.29837](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.29837)). Where Winds Meet gives LLM chat to a subset of side NPCs whose output is "constrained to text and to a discrete set of possible in-game flags" (become Old Friend, turn hostile, give weekly gift); NPCs cannot create items or rewrite quests ([allthings.how](https://allthings.how/where-winds-meet-and-ai-how-the-mmos-chatbot-npcs-work/)), and players still talked a guard into believing he had fathered their child ([GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/?p=632199)). Ubisoft Teammates keeps "traditional action trees for core actions" and found its pure-LLM assistant hallucinating health packs that do not exist ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/?p=310806)). Arm's mobile reference design states the rule directly: "do not turn the game engine into an LLM runtime"; the model returns an in-character reply plus structured labels and "does not control game actions" ([Arm Community blog](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/first-contact-a-mobile-architecture-for-llm_2d00_powered-game-characters)). Vaudeville's pre-mortem is the dependency warning: an external API made "chat outcomes and playability ... not fully predictable", launch traffic produced a surprise bill, and the service went down during the game's biggest stream ([Game Developer, Vaudeville pre-mortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/press-release/vaudeville-a-pre-mortem)). A 130-participant randomized study found LLM NPCs raised cognitive load and lowered usability and trust without improving overall experience ([arXiv 2604.10107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10107)).

Cost is no longer the blocker for bounded use. At October 2026 list prices Claude Haiku 5.5 is $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 output with cache hits at $0.01 ([Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)); GPT-6 luna is $0.10 / $0.50 ([OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)); Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite is $0.10 / $0.40 ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). A turn with a 2,000-token cached character prompt, 300 fresh tokens and 100 output tokens is about **$0.0001 on a small model and about $0.002 on Sonnet 5.5**, so 50 turns per session costs half a cent to nine cents (arithmetic on list prices, not measurement). Krafton's field numbers show the other end: an Opus-class model cost $5 to $7 per 20-minute match in research replays versus $0.02 to $0.06 for Gemma 4 31B, which is why they shipped a 2B on-device model ([Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/26592/built-a-teammate-got-a-chat-partner-kraftons-first-scorecard-for-pubg-ally)). Their latency-matched retest (cloud preferred 52% to 31% once speed was equal) means on-device was a latency decision rather than a quality one; for text conversation in a Stardew-like, 1 to 3 s of cloud latency is tolerable.

For your "high-plot situations" the proven runtime pattern is Dramamancer's: an authored scene with a fixed opening, authored events each with a natural-language condition and an authored outcome, one "interpretation" call per player input that decides which conditions are now satisfied, and one "instantiation" call per NPC line constrained to NPCs and the latest input ([Dramamancer, arXiv 2601.18785](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18785v1)). Drama Llama's author study shows the limits: three of six authors called output clichéd, character development scored lowest, and trigger timing was inconsistent ([Drama Llama, arXiv 2501.09099](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09099v1)). A knowledge-gated pipeline for a mystery NPC cut critical hallucinations by 65% and "entirely prevented" premature disclosure at a usability cost ([arXiv 2609.23043](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23043)). One low-risk extension is inZOI's: at the end of each day the model "adjust[s] their personal schedule of activities based on their experiences" ([NVIDIA on inZOI](https://www.nvidia.com/en-gb/geforce/news/nvidia-ace-naraka-bladepoint-inzoi-launch-this-month/)); in your terms, a nightly call proposes deviations from an allowed list and code validates them against opening hours and capacity before they become tomorrow's schedule entry.

At authoring time the evidence is thinner but consistent. No published case study of a coding agent generating and testing NPC schedules for a shipped game was found. The adjacent findings: LLMs "alone are not able to generate functioning plans" without schemas and validators ([FHNW thesis](https://irf.fhnw.ch/handle/11654/52053)); every academic system that worked is a schema plus a model; and voice homogenization is real, with LLM-played tabletop characters leaning on stereotyped traits while human lines were "more concise and direct" ([arXiv 2503.20623](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.20623)), and few-shot example lines working better than banned-word lists ([roborhythms](https://www.roborhythms.com/?p=33697)). The Dramamancer authors' evaluation variables (style adherence, character distinctiveness, scene awareness, event detection accuracy, outcome realization) are a usable rubric for a reviewer pass ([arXiv 2601.18785](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18785v1)). Build the authoring pipeline and the runtime pipeline on the same schema: the character document the coding agent writes from is what a runtime model is grounded in, and the validators that reject a malformed generated schedule are the ones that reject a runtime proposal.

## Established practice versus experimental ideas

| Established (shipped in multiple games, or shipped once and reconstructed) | Experimental (research only, or shipped once with mixed results) |
|---|---|
| Per-character keyed day schedules with most-specific-key-wins and a mandatory fallback (Stardew, Bethesda) | Dynamic per-NPC schedule generation from profiles at town scale (Legion Census; Ubisoft reports tuning and tagging as ongoing costs) |
| Activity-type verbs the engine implements (Ultima VII, Bethesda procedures) | Nightly LLM-proposed schedule deviations (inZOI, one shipped experimental setting) |
| Priority preempt and resume for interruptions (ScriptEase, KCD subbrains, Hitman situations) | GOAP or planners for daily life (Orkin and Merrill both warn against it) |
| Needs-based utility over object advertisements, decide when idle (Sims, IAUS) | Appraisal emotion models (FAtiMA, OCC); the one CiF+FAtiMA combination was discontinued |
| Smart objects and areas with capacity and reservation (Sims, KCD, Unreal) | Exclusion-logic world databases with concurrent practices (Versu; Praxish is a research reconstruction) |
| Relationship ledgers of named timed modifiers (CK3, RimWorld) | Weighted-rule social exchanges with thousands of influence rules (Prom Week; Ensemble exists but is thinly documented) |
| Tagged event log that dialogue can reference (CiF SFDB, Stardew `$t` topics) | Per-character fallible beliefs with mutation and lies (Talk of the Town) |
| Most-specific-rule dialogue selection (Valve, L4D, Yarn 3 saliency) | Story sifting with absolute heuristics (Chroniqueur null result; Bad News needed a human) |
| Storylets with role casting and hooks (Wildermyth, Fallen London) | Planning drama managers (Façade; "largely denizens of research labs") |
| Deterministic fixed tick, seeded RNG, per-tick hashes, replay (Factorio, Fiedler) | Property-based testing of NPC schedules (no practitioner write-up found) |
| LLM returns text plus closed flags, engine owns state (Where Winds Meet, PUBG Ally, Teammates) | Always-on generative agents per NPC (Smallville; cost scales with NPC count × game time) |
| Authoring-time generation into a schema with validators and a human reader | Agent-authored schedules and event scripts as a repeatable pipeline (no published case study) |

## Recommendations

1. Build a dependency-free `sim` package in TypeScript with an integer tick (one per in-game minute), one seeded PRNG passed explicitly, no wall-clock or DOM access, a per-tick state hash and an input log that doubles as save and replay. Test it in Node with vitest. Treat hayao.js, OpenFrontIO and Clockwork Engine as reference designs rather than dependencies.
2. Render with PixiJS v8 behind a snapshot interface, or with Excalibur pinned to 0.32.x if you want LDtk/Tiled loaders and a Playwright visual suite out of the box. Decide this in the first week and do not revisit unless the renderer blocks you.
3. Write maps as LDtk or Tiled JSON from code (or in your own format) so no GUI or Xvfb is in the build. Start with Kenney CC0 tiles; add LPC characters only with the generated credits sheet shipped.
4. Make verification text-first: ASCII map dumps, per-NPC daily itineraries, a "where is everyone at 14:00" table, and invariants (every NPC reaches its arrive-by destination, no NPC in two places, sleep windows respected, no location over capacity). Add a handful of coarse Playwright screenshots gated on a WebGL-context assertion.
5. Schedule format: per-NPC lists of `(window, activity verb, place or smart object, dialogue key, conditions)` resolved once at dawn by most-specific-key-wins with a required fallback and a sandbox entry at the bottom. Activity verbs are engine code; places have capacity and reservation. Interruptions are a priority stack with resume.
6. Fill idle time with a small utility system: needs as [0,1] considerations with response curves, multiplicative combination, commitment bonus, decide only when the action queue is empty. Add an oscillation detector to the test suite.
7. Relationships: per-pair directed ledgers of named timed modifiers plus a few binary labels, bucketed before use in rules. Apply Versu's rules: both parties choose long-term changes, and NPCs never unilaterally change their relationship to the player.
8. Memory: a world event log with tags and realization templates, plus a bounded per-NPC impression list with source, confidence and expiry. Gossip copies impressions on co-location. Surface the reasons in UI (a thought bubble or journal) so players can act on state.
9. Dialogue: a Valve-style rule database over a fact bag for barks and reactions (JSON you own, schema-validated), and ink via inkjs for authored scenes. Compile and play every generated ink file in CI.
10. NPC-to-NPC drama: start with authored, role-agnostic exchanges (ask out, confide, snub, make up) that cast any eligible pair, with volition from a short list of weighted rules and outcomes that plant hooks for follow-up exchanges. Budget for a few dozen rules per exchange, not thousands, and throttle drama-generating exchanges with an L4D-style intensity loop.
11. LLM at runtime only inside authored scenes: one interpretation call per player input, one instantiation call per line, closed flag set validated by code, hard turn budget, small model (Haiku 5.5 class or a Flash-Lite class) with a cached character prompt. Never let a model write a relationship value directly.
12. LLM at authoring time: the coding agent generates schedules, barks and storylets into the schema, validators check structure against the world (places exist, hours open, flags defined, canon not contradicted), and a human reads for voice with the Dramamancer rubric. Keep per-character example lines as the style anchor.

## Open decisions

1. PixiJS plus your own tilemap layer, or Excalibur with its loaders and 0.x churn.
2. Tile-level schedule points (Stardew) versus activity verbs over places (Ultima VII) as the primary authoring unit; the recommendation above is verbs, but it changes how much engine code each new behavior costs.
3. Whether NPC-to-NPC drama ships in the first playable at all, or only after the schedule, relationship and dialogue layers are stable.
4. Omniscient event log with subjective impressions (WAWLT) versus any form of private fallible belief; this decides whether "she heard it from her sister" is a mechanic or a line of flavor.
5. Where the LLM boundary sits: zero runtime calls, bounded scenes only, or scenes plus a nightly schedule-deviation proposal.
6. Whether a drama throttle (intensity loop) exists from the start or is added once playtests show pacing problems.
7. Art licensing posture: CC0 only, or LPC with share-alike obligations, or generated sprites you do not own.
8. Whether the simulation will ever run server-side (Durable Object) for persistence or multiplayer, because that argues for keeping the sim package free of browser APIs from the first commit.

## Conclusion

The research answers your framing question more sharply than expected: the "living world" you want is already a solved data-modeling problem at the layer of schedules, relationships and keyed dialogue, and an open problem at the layer of unprompted drama. Shipped games got believability from a base routine, an override stack, capacity-limited places and a few legible numbers, and their developers' postmortems say perceived life beat simulated life every time. Research systems added the social exchange, the role-agnostic practice and the tagged event history, and their authors report that those are reusable, while fallible belief models and automatic sifting were not. Your constraint that behavior be carefully developed scripts written by a coding agent is the mainstream choice rather than a compromise.

The implication for build order is that the schedule and relationship layers should be exercised by text tests for weeks before any renderer polish or any LLM call, because every team in these notes named tooling, inspection and debugging as the real cost. A town whose state can be dumped as a table, replayed from a seed and explained line by line is one a coding agent can keep extending without a screen, and it is also the one a runtime model can be grounded in safely later.
