# Social simulation, interactive drama, and character modeling research (formal/non-LLM strand)

Research date: October 2026. Scope: academic and experimental systems that represent NPC personalities, motivations, relationships, knowledge and social dynamics, and that try to produce soap-opera-like emergent drama. LLM-driven agents are excluded except where they touch these formal models. Primary sources were read in full where noted (PDFs extracted); secondary sources are flagged.

Reading guide for the report writer: the most reusable concrete representations for a Stardew-like town with soap-opera characters are (1) CiF/Ensemble's schema + weighted influence rules + social-facts history, (2) Versu/Praxish's exclusion-logic database + concurrent social practices with roles, (3) Talk of the Town's belief-facet/evidence model for gossip and misremembering, (4) Kismet's tag-proclivity language with location roles, (5) Felt/Winnow sifting patterns for finding drama after the fact, and (6) Left 4 Dead's intensity-based pacing loop as the pacing template. Each is detailed below with the representations spelled out.

## Q1. Façade (Mateas & Stern): beats, the drama manager, ABL; what worked and what did not

### Takeaway
Façade decomposed a one-act drama into ~2,500 hand-written "joint dialog behaviors" (jdbs) in the reactive-planning language ABL, grouped into 27 beats sequenced by a tension-arc drama manager; it achieved strong local agency but weak global agency, and its authors blamed the per-beat authoring cost (about 3 person-years for 20 minutes of play) and the difficulty of communicating hidden social-game state to the player.

### Cited Findings
- Architecture: "characters written in the reactive-planning language ABL, a drama manager that sequences dramatic beats, a forward-chaining rule system for understanding and interpreting natural language and gestural input from the player, and an animation engine" — [Mateas & Stern, "Structuring Content in the Façade Interactive Drama Architecture", AIIDE 2005](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIIDE/2005/AIIDE05-016.pdf)
- Game-vs-story resolution: interactions are recast as "abstract social games" organized around a numeric score such as player affinity; in part one an "affinity game" (zero-sum, whose side is the player on), a "hot-button game" (incendiary topics progressing through tiers), and in part two a "therapy game" (self-realization counters); plus an overall "story tension level". The score is "not directly communicated to the player via numbers or sliders, but rather via enriched, theatrically dramatic performance." — [AIIDE 2005](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIIDE/2005/AIIDE05-016.pdf)
- Beat structure: a Façade beat has 10–100 jdbs; a jdb is "a tightly coordinated, dramatic exchange of 1 to 5 lines of dialog between Grace and Trip, typically lasting a few seconds", written in 40–200 lines of ABL. Beats consist of a canonical sequence of beat goals (transition-in, body goals, wait goal, default transition-out) plus handler meta-behaviors that react to NLP-interpreted discourse acts by adding/removing/reordering beat goals and interjecting beat mix-ins. — [AIIDE 2005](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIIDE/2005/AIIDE05-016.pdf)
- Volume: ~2,500 jdbs authored; ~66% in beat goals/mix-ins across 27 beats, of which ~15 are seen per run; ~33% in ~20 "global mix-in" progressions (three tiers each, on satellite topics like sex, divorce, the wedding photo, plus deflection/recovery mix-ins); long-term autonomous behaviors (fixing drinks, advice ball) are only ~1% of jdbs but "contribute a great deal to the appearance of intelligence". Any one run shows at most 25% of content. — [AIIDE 2005](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIIDE/2005/AIIDE05-016.pdf)
- Drama manager selection rule: each beat is annotated with "preconditions, weights, weight tests, priorities, priority tests, and story value effects – the overall tension level"; the sequencer picks "the unused beat whose preconditions are satisfied and whose story tension effects most closely match the near-term trajectory of an author-specified story tension arc (in Façade, an Aristotelian tension arc)". The DM is dormant except when a beat ends or aborts. — [AIIDE 2005](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIIDE/2005/AIIDE05-016.pdf)
- Coherent intermixing: global mix-ins are written to be causally independent of beats; beat goals interrupted before their "gist point" are repeated after the mix-in; each beat goal has a "reestablish" jdb; most jdbs have 3–5 tonal alternates for affinity/tension combinations and 2–4 dialog alternates per tone. Because tone always matches, "players often perceive them as causally related, even though they are not." — [AIIDE 2005](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIIDE/2005/AIIDE05-016.pdf)
- NLU: "millions of potential player inputs are mapped, using hundreds of authored forward-chaining NLU rules, into one or more of ~30 parameterized discourse acts"; NLU failures occur ~30% of the time. Local agency: ideal reactivity ~25% of the time, partial ~40%, shallow ~25%, none ~10%. — [AIIDE 2005](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIIDE/2005/AIIDE05-016.pdf)
- Stated failures: "~3 person years of just authoring"; "only 27 beats were created in the end, resulting is far lower global agency than we initially hoped for"; "Façade is not generating sentences – although it is generating sequences"; "always clearly communicating the state of the social games to the player" is a challenge Façade "falls short on"; the marriage-collapse domain "overly raised players' expectations of the characters' intelligence". With 27 beats "technically there are thousands of different beat sequences possible; however, since most beats are causally independent, the number of meaningfully different beat sequences are few." — [AIIDE 2005](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIIDE/2005/AIIDE05-016.pdf)
- Stated successes: integration of beat goal/mix-in, global mix-in and drama manager sequencers with NLP; "two tightly-coordinated non-player characters who can believably keep dramatic action happening" when the player stops interacting; self-absorbed characters can "believably ignore unrecognized or unhandleable player actions". Future direction named: higher-level authoring tools that compile abstract specs into jdbs. — [AIIDE 2005](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIIDE/2005/AIIDE05-016.pdf)
- Development history: work began 1998; released July 2005 as a free Windows download after five-plus self-funded years. — [Game Developer, "The story of Façade"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-story-of-facade-the-ai-powered-interactive-drama)
- Wardrip-Fruin (2008): Façade "doesn't always work, but more often than not it does" and demonstrated interactive drama could be made real. — [Grand Text Auto EP 8.5, March 17 2008](https://grandtextauto.soe.ucsc.edu/2008/03/17/ep-85-facade)
- Versu's critique (Evans & Short 2014): Façade's behaviors are hard-coded to specific characters ("there is no general making cocktails activity. Instead, there is the particular activity of Trip making cocktails"), so replacing a character would require rewriting most behaviors; and an NPC's decision to join a joint behavior "is not based on his individual personality or desires." — [Evans & Short, IEEE TCIAIG 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)
- Riedl & Bulitko place Façade in the middle of the "virtual character autonomy" axis: a drama manager provides coherence while characters determine how plot units are realized via "personality-driven decompositions from high-level plot units to executable behaviors." — [Riedl & Bulitko, AI Magazine 2013](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/riedl2013interactive.pdf)

### Inferences
- The two persistent lessons for a town sim are: content cost dominates when every behavior is bespoke to characters (argues for character-agnostic, role-based content as in Versu/CiF), and hidden social state must be surfaced somehow or players cannot act on it (argues for Versu-style transparent feedback, see Q3).
- Façade's "multiple fronts of progression, causally independent, occasionally interdependent" plus tone-matching is a cheap trick for making unrelated threads feel like one story; applicable to interleaving town subplots.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve "Writing Façade: A Case Study in Procedural Authorship" (2007) or the ABL language paper; the beat-language annotation format beyond the list above is not quoted.

## Q2. Prom Week and Comme il Faut (CiF); Ensemble

### Takeaway
CiF represents a social world as a thin character model (binary traits/statuses, binary relationships, directed scalar networks), a cultural knowledge base, and a social-facts database of labeled past events; behavior comes from "social exchanges" (initiator intent + responder accept/reject + authored "instantiations") whose desirability is computed by summing weights of thousands of authored influence rules grouped into "microtheories"; Prom Week needed over 5,000 such rules for 18 characters, and Ensemble is the generalized, JSON-schema-driven, open-source successor.

### Cited Findings
**Character and social-state representation (CiF, 2014)**
- "the representation of each character is thin. What makes characters rich and unique is their relational situation in the social world and their interconnected history." Four sets: traits, statuses, relationships, social networks. — [McCoy et al., "Social Story Worlds With Comme il Faut", IEEE TCIAIG 6(2), 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)
- Traits are immutable binary properties (brave, intelligent); statuses are temporary binary properties that "expire when the conditions that triggered them no longer hold" and can be directional ("angry at", "infatuated with"). Personality change over time is not modeled. — [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)
- Relationships are binary, nonexclusive, nonrestrictive pair states (housemates, rivals), paired with "bidirectional, scalar valued social networks" (e.g., respect 30 one way, 85 the other). — [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)
- Cultural Knowledge Base (CKB): items plus connection types (loves/hates/has) plus one universally agreed adjective per item (Mondays are "boring" even if Jon loves Mondays). — [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)
- Social Facts Database (SFDB): records every action with timestamp, a "performance realization string" (NLG-templated, e.g. "%i% made scandalous remarks about %rp% mother"), and author-defined labels ("risque", "cruel"). "CiF does not simulate hidden information: all events that occur are assumed to be immediately known about by all characters." Backstory is prepopulated as events with negative timestamps. — [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)
- Prom Week's concrete inventory: relationships (3): friends, dating, enemies; networks (3): buddy, romance, cool; statuses (34) e.g. popular, embarrassed, angry at, pities, cheater, heartbroken, has a crush on; traits (44) e.g. competitive, sex magnet, witty, attention hog, oblivious; SFDB labels (13): cool, lame, romantic, failed romance, gross, funny, bad ass, mean, nice, taboo, rude, embarrassing, misunderstood; CKB adjectives (10); CKB connection types (4): likes, dislikes, wants, has. "over 5000 influence rules were created to represent the social norms of Prom Week." — [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)

**Social exchanges, volition, response**
- A social exchange is "an attempt by one character to change the social state between him and another character"; each has one primary intent (e.g. become friends), preconditions (hard gates), an initiator influence rule set and a responder influence rule set (soft weights), and any number of instantiations (authored performances with their own preconditions and effects; at least one accept and one reject). — [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)
- Volition formation: "For each pair of characters, volition formation ranks all possible intents and exchanges based on a hand authored set of social influence rules. Each rule has a weight value"; weights from initiator IRSs and all microtheories are tallied; responder weights determine accept/reject. — [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)
- Prom Week UI shows the player "the top social exchanges that each character desires to play with each other character"; each character has a ranked list of desired exchanges (over 40 exist); determined by "over 5,000 social considerations". — [Prom Week dev blog](https://promweek.soe.ucsc.edu/?p=295); [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)
- Instantiation selection: if several instantiations' preconditions hold, "the most salient instantiation is selected. Salience value is generally correlated to the complexity, or strictness, of the instantiation precondition". Instantiations are "retargeted": any instantiation can play between any pair of characters allowed by its preconditions. — [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)
- Effects: instantiation effects adjust network values by fixed amounts, start/end relationships, bestow/remove statuses; "only characters directly involved in the social exchange are permitted to be directly affected"; third parties change via trigger rules. — [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)

**Rules**
- Predicates bind character variables, a predicate type (trait, relationship, status, network, SFDB, CKB), and an evaluation mode: "true now", "true in history", "times true" (e.g. "popular" = friends with ≥4 characters, written as one predicate instead of four). SFDB predicates cannot be "true now" by design. Example lookup template: `%SFDB_(cruel, i, r, 7)%` = a "cruel" event by initiator toward responder within 7 turns, realized as "after you made scandalous remarks about my mother?" — [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)
- Trigger rules: universal rules run after exchanges that change state, e.g. "if a character is 'dating' more than one person, give them the 'two-timer' status". — [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)
- Time-ordered rules: predicates get a time-order index, evaluated in ascending order in true-in-history mode; motivating example the "knight in shining armor" rule (B is mean to A, then C is mean to B → A wants to befriend C). — [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)
- Microtheories: rule sets "unified by a precondition" on one predicate (e.g. relationship(partner,x,y)) that apply to all exchanges sharing an intent; introduced because "the 'big bag of rules' approach" produced "redundant, overlapping, or contradictory rules", worse with multiple authors. "a majority of rule authoring in CiF involves these specific intents, or microtheories". Example interplay: the "feels superior to" microtheory lowers desire to befriend, but the "give advice" exchange's own IRS raises it. — [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)
- Worked example: Simon (oblivious, witty; has crush on Naomi; friends with Naomi; high romance toward her) asks Naomi (attractive, popular, low romance toward him) out; "has a crush on" microtheory raises "ask out"; "popular" microtheory makes her reject non-popular people who did embarrassing things recently; because they are friends, the instantiation chosen has her "kindly letting him down". — [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)
- Comparison claim vs The Sims 3: CiF rules have first-order-like complexity (can reference third parties: "x is cheating on y if x and y are dating and there is a character z also dating x"), and use backstory/play history; Sims 3 "can only reference the two characters in an interaction" and lacks history. — [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)

**Results and evaluation**
- Prom Week released Feb 14 2012; 13,003 play traces by May 17 2012 (5,425 in story levels). In Simon's final level, of 263 playthroughs "no two were exactly alike"; average node indegree ~1.11; 1-gram: 38 of 39 social moves used; 6-gram: 5,066 unique patterns. Oswald's story: 25 opening moves with average indegree 15.6, dropping to 2.36 after move 2, 1.27 after 3, 1.07 after 4. Only 11 of ~100 possible opening exchanges were ever chosen, interpreted as goal-directed play. Goal completion: 610 completions across 263 traces (2.32 per player, ~46% of possible). — [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)
- Other uses: Mismanor (first-person, added items and knowledge as first-class entities with statuses like "known by Violet" and classifier traits like "secret"), IMMERSE (DARPA, real-time with Kinect), SIREN (paired CiF with FAtiMA for emotion dynamics). Awards: IGF 2012 Technical Excellence finalist, IndieCade 2012 finalist. — [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)
- Versu's critique of Prom Week: "behavior is just a sequence of isolated and unrelated speech acts" with no continuity; a group deciding what to do next is one speech act in Prom Week but a multi-step practice in Versu. — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)
- Anthology authors' critique: CiF "was primarily employed for a single game development project, Prom Week, has no formal definition outside of its implementation, and lacks comprehensive documentation for use in external projects". — [Azad et al., "Anthology", AIIDE 2022](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/21967/21736)

**Ensemble (successor engine)**
- Ensemble is "A rules-based AI framework for social simulation ... an evolution of the Comme il Faut AI system"; a standalone `ensemble.js` library with an `ensemble` singleton API, a desktop authoring tool covering schema, trigger rules, volition rules, characters, history, actions; BSD-4 license; 351 commits, 77 issues, 66 stars at time of reading. Associated paper: Samuel, Reed, Maddaloni, Mateas, Wardrip-Fruin, "The Ensemble engine: Next-generation social physics", FDG 2015. — [Ensemble GitHub](https://github.com/ensemble-engine/ensemble); [Neighborly paper citing FDG 2015](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/Neighborly_CoG22.pdf)
- Schema file fields: `category` (lowercase name of a social phenomenon), `types` (array of instances, each a separately tracked variable), `isBoolean`, `directionType` ∈ {directed, undirected, reciprocal} (reciprocal: changing one side auto-changes the other), `minValue`/`maxValue` (clamped), `actionable` (whether characters can form volitions over it), `defaultValue`, `duration` (0 = recorded only on the timestep it occurs, modeling events rather than states). — [Ensemble wiki: Schema](https://github.com/ensemble-engine/ensemble/wiki/Schema)
- API: `addRules(data)` takes files with `fileName`, `type` ∈ {"volition","trigger"}, `rules`; "numerical `weight`(s) defined in the rule's `effect` will be summed with all other contributing rule weights"; bound effects look like `{ category: "attribute", type: "strength", first: "rival", operator: "+", value: 10, second: undefined }`; `calculateVolition(cast)` forms volitions of each character toward every character (including self) per intent, with dumps like `{ "category": "network", "type": "buddy", "intentType": true, "weight": 20 }`; actions form a graph per intent with `leadsTo` children and a `lineage` string such as `"RAISESTRENGTH-WORKOUT"`, scored by adding influence-rule weights while traversing; `addHistory` takes an array of timestep objects; `runTriggerRules(cast)` applies rules that conditionally change social state. — [Ensemble wiki: Ensemble API](https://github.com/ensemble-engine/ensemble/wiki/Ensemble-API)
- Neighborly authors describe Ensemble as a rational reconstruction of CiF that "removed the experience-specific constructs used in Prom Week, and gave users a general schema for creating social simulations with a small cast of characters." — [Johnson-Bey, Nelson, Mateas, "Neighborly", CoG 2022](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/Neighborly_CoG22.pdf)
- Praxish authors on the CiF line (CiF, Ensemble, Kismet): "a two-stage model of character decision-making. Characters ... first form high-level volitions toward one another (generally relationship status intents such as 'start dating' or 'become rivals') ... and then take actions that are specified to advance these volitions"; limitation: "characters cannot intend arbitrarily complicated sets of logical conditions". — [Dameris, Hernandez Roman, Kreminski, "Praxish", AIIDE 2023](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/27537/27310)

### Inferences
- Authoring load scales with the rule base, not the cast: 5,000+ weighted rules for 18 characters, 3 relationships, 3 networks, ~78 traits/statuses. Microtheories and trigger rules exist specifically to tame that base; a town-sim designer should plan the same two abstractions from day one.
- The SFDB-with-labels design (every event logged with tags, timestamps, and a first/second/third-person realization string) is the simplest proven way to get characters that "remember and refer to past events" in dialog.
- CiF's omniscience assumption (everyone knows everything) is the main thing a gossip-driven soap opera would have to replace; see Talk of the Town (Q4) for the alternative.

- I could not retrieve the Ensemble FDG 2015 paper text or a full JSON example of a volition/trigger rule from the repo (only API fragments). Table I of the CiF paper (example influence rules) did not survive PDF text extraction.

## Q3. Versu / Praxis (Richard Evans, Emily Short)

### Takeaway
Versu modeled the whole world as sentences in "exclusion logic" (a tree-structured modal logic where `!` enforces single-valued slots), coordinated autonomous utility-based agents through concurrent, role-agnostic "social practices" that offer affordances rather than commands, and reported roughly an order of magnitude faster content production than Façade; it was cancelled by Linden Lab in 2014 and never released, but Praxish (2023, open source) reconstructs the logic layer and practice format from the published documentation.

### Cited Findings
**History**
- Built by Emily Short, Richard Evans (AI lead for The Sims 3) and Graham Nelson (Inform); development began 2010; acquired 2012 by Linden Lab; cancelled 2014; "the framework has never been released, with Linden Lab declining to sell it back to its original creators or otherwise allow its continued development". Example games A Family Supper and Blood & Laurels were briefly on iPad and are now unplayable on modern iOS. — [Praxish, AIIDE 2023](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/27537/27310)
- Short (March 2014): Linden let her go in Feb 2014, refused to sell the codebase/IP ("a definite no"); Blood and Laurels and other completed apps would not be released. At Linden they added "a mechanism that detects when a scene has lost momentum and introduces a new event to restart it", and a new authoring toolset with story-flow visualization that increased content production speed "by a factor of at least ten"; one Blood and Laurels playthrough shows less than 10% of its text. — [Game Developer, "The end of Versu: Emily Short looks back"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/the-end-of-versu-emily-short-looks-back)

**Architecture (Evans & Short, IEEE TCIAIG 2014; manuscript received Oct 2012)**
- Strong-autonomy design: "Each character chooses his next action based on his own individual beliefs and desires. There is a centralized DM, but it is rare indeed for the DM to override the characters' autonomy"; the DM "typically operates at a higher level—by providing suggestions, or tweaking the desires of the participants." Two reasons: replayability (role/character permutations) and player control ("A simulated system has clear rules which the player can learn and internalize"). — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)
- Responses to Façade's three issues: text output instead of 3D ("adverbial modifiers are much cheaper: we can modify a verb by simply appending an adverb"); each character has unique text overrides (Brown swaggers, Lady Catherine hobbles); feedback via character portraits per Ekman emotional state, clickable to see who the emotion is directed at and the triggering event; a menu interface where "affordances provided by each social practice are displayed explicitly", grouped by practice. Guided by Wardrip-Fruin's "SimCity effect". — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)
- World representation: "the simulation state is entirely determined by a set of sentences in a modal logic ... There are no objects, or pointers"; advantages: visibility ("Bugs lurk in the darkness. In this architecture ... nothing is hidden"), debuggability (logical breakpoints on which practice made a fact true), serializability. Runtime inspector can "find out why an action's preconditions have failed" and "what is causing a fact to become true". — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)
- Exclusion logic literals: `brown.sex!male`, `brown.class!upper`, `brown.in!dining_room`, `brown.relationship.lucy.evaluation.attractive!40`, `process.dinner.dining_room.participant.brown`. `.` asserts a subtree; `!` asserts the only subtree, so inserting `A.at!Y` automatically removes `A.at!X` (no STRIPS delete lists). Deleting a prefix removes a whole subtree, giving data lifetimes (`mr_collins.beliefs.clergymen_should_marry` dies with the agent; `process.whist.data.whose_move!brown` dies with the game). Praxis is "strongly, but implicitly typed"; `A(agent).sex!G(gender)` is a typing judgment that an agent has exactly one sex. Tools: type checker, inspector, deterministic playback, stress-test tool running hundreds of AI-only games. — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)
- Social practice declaration example:
  ```
  process.greet.X(agent).Y(agent)
    action "Greet"
      preconditions X.in!L and Y.in!L
      postconditions text "[X] says 'Hi' to [Y obj]"
  end
  ```
  Instantiated by asserting `process.greet.jack.jill`. Action language supports disjunctive and negated preconditions, nested quantifiers, numeric expressions, domain axioms, conditional effects. — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)
- Constitutive view (after Rawls): "every affordance is contained within a practice and is only available if that practice is instantiated"; multiple practices run concurrently (eating, politics conversation, flirtation, reacting to spilled soup); an agent's options are the union of affordances of practices it is in. Practices store arbitrary data and are "significantly more powerful than a finite-state machine". — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)
- Norms: norm-violating actions get postconditions marking a violation; most agents have strong desires to respect norms (the DM lowers these for rakish Brown); when the player violates a norm "the practice which kept track of the norm spawns a subpractice" offering others options to disapprove, forgive, get angry, or evict. — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)
- Decision making: utility-based reactive selection with one-step lookahead on actual consequences ("they actually execute the results of the action ... Then, they evaluate this future world state with respect to their desires. Then, we undo"); contrasted with The Sims where estimated and actual consequences diverge (the bathroom loop). Utility = sum of satisfied wants; a want is any exclusion-logic sentence with a utility modifier, e.g. Brown wants `Other.sex!male and Other.class!upper and is_displeased_with.Other.brown` at +20 per instantiation, so annoying three upper-class men scores 3x. "as many personalities as there are sentences in a language." — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)
- Relationships via "role evaluation" (Sacks' membership categorization): multiple asymmetric evaluations per pair (well bred, proper, attractive, generous, good father, good husband), stored as `Agent.relationship.Evaluated.role.Role!Value!Explanation` so characters remember reasons ("Mr. Darcy is a bad suitor because he was rather rude at the dance"); acquired by hard-coding, by interpreting actions (interpretation is itself a chosen action, and interpretations are themselves evaluable), or by hearing and believing another's evaluation. Plus one symmetric public "relationship state" (friends, lovers, enemies). — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)
- Reactions: implemented as spawned short-lived practices that "destroy itself if anything more recent happens, to prevent agents reacting to 'old news'". Emotions: one Ekman-based state at a time, previous state remembered for mood switching; each emotion records target and cause. — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)
- Beliefs: shared world model for memory reasons; "misunderstandings, etc., cannot be implemented fully"; specific issues (is there a ghost; who knows Frank loves Lucy) stored as individual beliefs that gate actions. Beliefs are limited to two-place predicates over constants—no quantified beliefs ("the murderer is one of the guests") and no nested beliefs ("Mr. Quinn believes that Lucy believes ..."). — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)
- Character arcs: the most complex part of a character file; "internal high-level choices" (does Brown take another mistress or improve his poetry) with per-character epilogs; only the player can act against the simulation's preferred affordance, so player characters change and NPCs stay in supporting roles. — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)
- Core model and quips: practices communicate through relationship states, beliefs, emotions, evaluations; dialog is authored as "quips" (text template + effects), and a single quip is marked to do several things (Mr. Collins' remark communicates positive self-evaluation, admiration of Lady Catherine, and shifts him to "pleased"). Design rules: "Relationship state changes should require active choice from both characters"; "It should never be possible for an NPC unilaterally to change his or her relationship to the player"; "individual actions should ideally produce change to multiple qualities" because with few affordances different scoring formulas produce indistinguishable behavior. — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)
- Story manager: per-episode, "a high-level director who does not like to micromanage ... initiate practices, watch their progress, and insert occasional changes"; itself a practice and an agent with meta-level desires, so "the player can actually be the DM"; no tension-arc planning ("We do not do this"). — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)
- Content cost: Façade's 20-minute scenario took 3+ years; a comparable Versu 20-minute episode "takes about two months"; the 45–60-minute ghost story took six months; Versu had "more than 1000 parameterized actions, authored in one year" vs Façade's 30 discourse acts. With the Prompter play-script front end (Graham Nelson), "a substantially branching 20-min episode in less than a week". — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)
- Stated limitations: tuning "over 300 desires" is "a difficult and unrewarding problem" even with the inspector; concurrent practices make scoring/debugging hard; a crude "dominating practice" suppresses others when a body is found, but what they wanted was Heidegger's "public mood" that opens some practices and closes others; agents cannot have divergent understandings of a practice's state. — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)

**Praxish (reconstruction, 2023)**
- Praxish (Santa Clara University, vanilla JS, open source at github.com/mkremins/praxish) reconstructs the Praxis logic database, practice layer and character layer. Sentence grammar `X ::= S | S.X | S!X`; queries by unification (`Room.occupant.Person`); inserting `jukebox.playState!broken` wipes the playState subtree; deleting `practice.debate.ben.pattie` discards the whole debate. — [Praxish, AIIDE 2023](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/27537/27310)
- Practice JSON format: `id`, `roles` array, `actions` (each with `name`, `conditions`, `outcomes`), optional `name`, static `data`, `init` commands, `functions`. Example: `{ id: "greet", roles: ["Greeter","Greeted"], actions: [{ name: "[Actor]: Greet [Other]", conditions: ["eq Actor Greeter","eq Other Greeted"], outcomes: ["delete practice.greet.Actor.Other"] }] }`. Inserting `practice.greet.trip.gonzalo` spawns an instance; actions can spawn subpractices (a question spawns a practice offering the target ways to answer). Condition types: propositional, equality, negation (disjunction, math, universals deferred). — [Praxish, AIIDE 2023](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/27537/27310)
- Character goals: `{utility: 5, conditions: ["practice.bar.B.patron.kaguya.order!Drink", "kaguya.ship.Crush.evaluation.crush", "Crush.preferences.favoriteDrink!Drink"]}`; utility of an action = sum of goals it would satisfy, counted per instantiation; one-step lookahead; ties broken randomly. New feature: "practice-bound agents" (a jukebox, a drama manager) restricted to one practice. Two example storyworlds are "only a few hundred lines of practice and character definition JSON"; "multiple complex practices per day" once features solidified. — [Praxish, AIIDE 2023](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/27537/27310)
- Related lineage: Spirit AI's Character Engine (co-founded by Short) used Praxis-like practices and is now unavailable; MKULTRA (Horswill 2018) used an exclusion-logic database; City of Gangsters (Zubek et al. 2021) uses the logic language BotL for periodic queries that update relationship states "in a fashion similar to story sifting". — [Praxish, AIIDE 2023](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/27537/27310)
- Versu's own source docs cited by Praxish: Evans, "Praxis: A Logic-Based DSL for Modeling Social Practices" (slide deck, versublog 2014); Evans & Short, "The AI Architecture of Versu" (versublog 2014); Nelson, "Prompter: A Domain-Specific Language for Versu" (2014); Short, "How Versu works" (versu.com). — [Praxish, AIIDE 2023](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/27537/27310)

### Inferences
- For a Stardew-like town, Versu's two strongest ideas are: (a) content as reusable, role-agnostic practices ("dinner", "flirtation", "argument about the fence") that can be instantiated between any villagers and run concurrently in the same room; and (b) a fully inspectable fact database so designers can ask "who is in a room with someone they dislike?" without code.
- Versu's reported pain points predict a town-sim's: tuning hundreds of desires, deciding which concurrent practice should dominate when something big happens, and the absence of per-character beliefs (needed for gossip).

### Gaps
- I did not read the Praxis slide deck, "The AI Architecture of Versu", or Prompter docs; their exact file formats are not quoted here. No controlled evaluation of Versu exists (authors say so).

## Q4. Talk of the Town and Bad News (James Ryan et al.)

### Takeaway
Talk of the Town simulates a small American town from 1839 to 1979 (300–500 living NPCs with routines, businesses, families, Big Five personalities) and layers on the deepest published model of fallible character knowledge: per-character "mental models" of every person/place made of "belief facets" with evidence lists, strengths, provenance pointers, and mutation/forgetting; Bad News proved the output usable only with a human "wizard" sifting the simulation live for an improv actor, which motivated the whole story-sifting research line.

### Cited Findings
**World simulation**
- "Talk of the Town is an asymmetric multiplayer dwarflike ... that features character knowledge propagation as a core mechanic"; town simulated "from its founding in 1839 ... through the death of the central character in the summer of 1979"; layout, routines, and social/family networks "emerge bottom-up". NPCs "act out daily routines across day and night cycles by either going to work, going on errands, dropping by places of leisure, visiting friends and family, or staying at home"; they may start businesses, hire, build, marry, give birth; "NPCs decide what to do by utility-based action selection"; co-located characters interact "at probabilities that depend on their relationship and their personalities"; "From a simple affinity system, continued interaction may breed contempt, friendliness, or romantic feelings (these work unidirectionally and may be asymmetric)". 300–500 NPCs at gameplay time; a character's appearance is 24 inherited facial attributes. — [Ryan & Mateas, "Simulating Character Knowledge Phenomena in Talk of the Town", Game AI Pro 3 (2017)](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter37_Simulating_Character_Knowledge_Phenomena_in_Talk_of_the_Town.pdf)
- Neighborly's description: TotT simulates a town "for over 100 virtual years ... from small ghost towns to bustling social centers with more than 200 residents", using "a simple model of abstract social mechanics combined with pseudo-randomness and population statistics to produce engaging emergent narratives about star-crossed lovers, generational rivalries, family legacies, and romantic love triangles"; only one explicit "socialize" action, with the relationship model determining side effects; "only a certain percentage of days were modeled each year" with interpolation; relationships use "charge and spark" (friendship and romance); personality is Big Five; code at github.com/james-owen-ryan/talktown (MIT license, files include mind.py, personality.py, relationship.py, routine.py, whereabouts.py, business.py, life_event.py, drama.py). TotT "incorporates ad-hoc assumptions about Bad News' setting into the core simulation code"; replacing the Big-5 model "would require a complete rewrite of the relationship model, character routines, and event logic". — [Neighborly, CoG 2022](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/Neighborly_CoG22.pdf); [talktown repo](https://github.com/james-owen-ryan/talktown)
- Emily Short's reading of the dissertation: romance "is handled probabilistically, based on both characters' personalities, their ages, and how much time they spend together"; procedurally generated gravestones let characters update beliefs about past residents; Ryan "admits the content is not very dramatic. Characters mostly talk about things like other characters' hair and eye color." — [Emily Short, May 28 2019](https://emshort.blog/2019/05/28/curating-simulated-storyworlds-james-ryan-ch-6f/)

**Knowledge model (the reusable part)**
- Requirements: perceptible attributes (hair color; conditionally perceptible: workplace while working), a perceptibility radius (same location), a procedure for character salience, authored attribute saliences (hair color more salient than chin shape), and a hand-authored "belief mutation graph". — [Game AI Pro 3 ch. 37](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter37_Simulating_Character_Knowledge_Phenomena_in_Talk_of_the_Town.pdf)
- Structure: each character holds an ontology of interlinked mental models, one per person/place, each a list of belief facets. Facet types for people: status (alive/dead, departure year, marital status), age (birth year, death year, approximate age), name (first/middle/last, suffix, surname ethnicity, hyphenation), appearance (24 facial attributes), occupation (company→links to place model, title, shift, status), home (→place model), whereabouts (where on a given day/night→place). For places: employees/residents, apartment flag, block, address. — [ch. 37](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter37_Simulating_Character_Knowledge_Phenomena_in_Talk_of_the_Town.pdf)
- Facet fields: owner, subject, type, value, mental-model pointer, predecessor (previous belief, giving a perfect belief history NPCs cannot access), parents (the facets in other characters' minds that spawned it), evidence list, strength (sum of evidence strengths), accuracy flag. — [ch. 37](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter37_Simulating_Character_Knowledge_Phenomena_in_Talk_of_the_Town.pdf)
- Evidence typology (11 types, 5 categories): origination — reflection, observation, transference (copying beliefs between look-alikes), confabulation (value drawn from the town-wide distribution, e.g. 45% black hair → 45% chance), lie, implant; reinforcement — declaration ("the more a person retells some belief, the stronger that belief becomes ... an NPC who frequently tells the same lie might come to actually believe it"); deterioration — mutation; termination — forgetting; plus statement and eavesdropping as propagation. Each evidence piece has source, location, time, and strength (type-dependent, decays over time, and for statements scaled by the recipient's affinity for the source and the source's own belief strength). Recipients treat lies as statements but the system tracks the truth. — [ch. 37](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter37_Simulating_Character_Knowledge_Phenomena_in_Talk_of_the_Town.pdf)
- Salience computation: relationship of subject to observer (coworker > stranger), friendship, romantic feelings, subject's job level; for places, whether observer lives/works there. — [ch. 37](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter37_Simulating_Character_Knowledge_Phenomena_in_Talk_of_the_Town.pdf)
- Gossip/propagation: conversants score everyone either knows by summed salience to both, pick the n highest (n from relationship strength and both extroversion scores), then exchange attribute beliefs at probabilities set by attribute salience; characters can learn about people they never met, including the dead (parents telling children about relatives). — [ch. 37](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter37_Simulating_Character_Knowledge_Phenomena_in_Talk_of_the_Town.pdf)
- Fallibility: each timestep, transference/confabulation/mutation/forgetting fire probabilistically per mental model, modulated by an inherited floating-point memory attribute, facet type (whereabouts mutates more than first name), and belief strength; mutation uses the authored graph (e.g. brown→black 0.15, brown→gray 0.03, with illustrative probabilities for hair color and facial hair). — [ch. 37](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter37_Simulating_Character_Knowledge_Phenomena_in_Talk_of_the_Town.pdf)
- Belief revision: adopt the first supported belief; contradicting evidence is compared by strength; weaker contradictions are tracked as candidate beliefs that can later overtake ("Belief oscillation is possible, as such, but that is an appeal of the design"). — [ch. 37](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter37_Simulating_Character_Knowledge_Phenomena_in_Talk_of_the_Town.pdf)
- Efficiency trick: knowledge is not simulated during the 140-year generation; at the end, knowledge is "implanted" (family, friends, neighbors, coworkers always; others with probability 1 − 1/salience; per attribute with probability tied to attribute salience), then one week is simulated with full knowledge phenomena. Statistics: each NPC holds ~250–400 mental models (extroverts 500–600) and ~800–1200 belief facets; world generation takes minutes; between-turn knowledge simulation about a minute. — [ch. 37](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter37_Simulating_Character_Knowledge_Phenomena_in_Talk_of_the_Town.pdf)
- Stated limitations: "worries about the complexity of interaction between all these parameters"; "not very computationally efficient"; the only reported tuning case: in early Bad News performances characters "could not remember where their own parents lived", fixed by lowering the mutation rate for home addresses; "It is not easy for us to articulate how this architecture could be utilized for games in mainstream genres." Dialog was intended to be fully procedural with a dialog manager and an NLG pool of "nearly three million generable lines" of small talk. — [ch. 37](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter37_Simulating_Character_Knowledge_Phenomena_in_Talk_of_the_Town.pdf)

**Bad News (the live performance)**
- Bad News (Ryan, Samuel, Summerville, UCSC EIS) "combines Wizard of Oz techniques and live improvisational acting into an emotionally charged one-on-one experience"; the player, as a mortician's assistant, "navigates the richly simulated town to interact with its residents, who are each played live by a professional actor. An unseen operator listens in remotely to manage the unfolding experience via live coding and discreet communication with the actor." — [UChicago CCCT event page](https://ccct.uchicago.edu/events/trauma-and-the-aesthetics-of-life-bad-news/)
- The storyworld is generated before play; the player must find and notify the next of kin of an unidentified dead person; blends immersive theatre, tabletop roleplay, IF descriptions on a tablet, and improv; follows a "contract of care". — [Emily Short, May 28 2019](https://emshort.blog/2019/05/28/curating-simulated-storyworlds-james-ryan-ch-6f/)
- Performed "more than one hundred times" by 2017; "thousands and thousands of generated character beliefs" encountered, "generally ... well-formed and believable". Featured at Slamdance and IndieCade; covered by The Guardian and Rolling Stone. — [ch. 37](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter37_Simulating_Character_Knowledge_Phenomena_in_Talk_of_the_Town.pdf); [Neighborly](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/Neighborly_CoG22.pdf)
- "The original Talk of the Town was designed to be used with a human-in-the-loop operator in the Bad News live experience. The operator was then tasked with manually inspecting the simulation data and piecing together potential narratives." A later paper quantifies this: Samuel, Summerville, Ryan, England, "A Quantified Analysis of Bad News for Story Sifting Interfaces", ICIDS 2021; and the WizActor tool was tested by having a professional designer build a regency-era world in Kismet. — [Neighborly](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/Neighborly_CoG22.pdf)
- Primary paper: Samuel, Ryan, Summerville, Mateas, Wardrip-Fruin, "Bad News: An Experiment in Computationally Assisted Performance", ICIDS 2016, pp. 108–120 (cited in Neighborly and WAWLT). — [Neighborly](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/Neighborly_CoG22.pdf)

**Ryan's dissertation and successors**
- Curating Simulated Storyworlds (UCSC, 2018, CC BY) asks why The Sims and Dwarf Fortress succeed where Tale-Spin failed, and argues "a human being or a second AI system is needed to extract the good stories from the simulator and present them to the reader." — [eScholarship record](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/1340j5h2); [Emily Short, May 21 2019](https://emshort.blog/2019/05/21/curating-simulated-storyworlds-james-ryan/)
- Ryan's "pains of emergent narrative": boringness, granularity extremes, lack of modularity, lack of abstraction, modeling gaps, causality issues (fixes: contingent unlocking, causal bookkeeping); "pains of curation": missing/poor curation, poor presentation, failure to mount as a playable experience, aesthetic posturing. Ryan rejects drama managers on aesthetic grounds; distinguishes feedforward (sim generates material presented elsewhere) vs feedback (story told inside the sim, e.g. an NPC recounting events) architectures; proposes sifting patterns and sifting heuristics. — [Emily Short, May 23 2019](https://emshort.blog/2019/05/23/curating-simulated-storyworlds-james-ryan-2/)
- Hennepin (finer-grained TotT taking a day or two to run, with a Dwarf-Fortress-derived personality model "to provide a more explainable environment") and Sheldon County (a generated podcast from Hennepin output). Lesson named by Short: "Knowledge simulation doesn't naturally produce narrative intrigue." "Apophenia hacking": the curator may expand on but not contradict the simulation. — [Emily Short, May 28 2019](https://emshort.blog/2019/05/28/curating-simulated-storyworlds-james-ryan-ch-6f/); [Neighborly](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/Neighborly_CoG22.pdf)
- Neighborly (2022) reconstructs TotT as a Python ECS with YAML archetypes and plugins: characters have GameCharacter (name, age, location, life values, statuses), optional Personality and Routine components; 7-day/24-hour routines (to model weekends); occupations; "character values" (confidence, material things, knowledge, power) that steer which locations/activities characters frequent, as an alternative to Big Five; relationships as a directed network of friendship and romance scalars in [-50, 50] plus modifiers (Friend, Enemy, Coworker, Love Interest); life events via production rules with preconditions/post-effects that ask each involved character to accept; behavior trees for callbacks. Story sifting (Centrifuge) planned. "TotT-likes simulate the major skeleton of a character's life, leaving it up to users to fill in the gaps with their imagination"—apophenia "is the glue that makes this fidelity of simulation work". — [Neighborly, CoG 2022](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/Neighborly_CoG22.pdf)
- Related dissertation: Johnson-Bey (UCSC) on reusable tools for social-simulation-driven emergent storytelling, covering the design space, exemplar frameworks, sifting tools, and designer interviews. — [eScholarship record](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/4mr6c29j)

### Inferences
- TotT's model is the most complete published blueprint for a gossip system with provenance ("Gary Stuart told me last night at the 3rd Street Diner"), misremembering, and lies; but two findings are sobering for soap-opera goals: the gossip content was mundane (hair color), and the drama in Bad News came from a human sifting plus an actor.
- The knowledge-implant trick (simulate history without beliefs, then seed beliefs from relationships and salience) is the practical way to afford belief modeling at town scale.

- The primary EXAG 2015 paper ("Toward Characters Who Observe, Tell, Misremember, and Lie") and the ICIDS 2016 Bad News paper were unreachable (server errors); the dissertation PDF download returned empty. Ryan's narrativized traces and the TotT routine/social-network generation details ("A simple method for evolving large character social networks", 2016) were not read.

## Q5. Kismet, Ceptre, and other logic-based simulation languages (plus Anthology)

### Takeaway
Kismet (Summerville & Samuel, 2020) is a compact Prolog/ASP-backed DSL where traits are weighted "proclivities" toward action tags, actions carry tags/conditions/results/visibility/response slots, locations cast characters into roles, and patterns (including temporal ones) match social configurations; Ceptre (Martens, 2015) expresses social state as linear-logic resources consumed and produced by rules (four units of anger can be spent on a murder) with stages for interactivity; Anthology (2022) is a Sims-style motive-utility framework with JSON agents/locations/actions.

### Cited Findings
**Kismet**
- "Kismet is a light weight social simulation language/engine" (Python 3.7+, clingo answer-set solver, Tracery for text); explore via `kismet/kismet.ebnv` and a Jupyter playground; paper: Summerville & Samuel, "Kismet: a small social simulation language", Casual Creator Workshop at ICCC 2020. — [Kismet GitHub](https://github.com/adamsumm/Kismet); [Neighborly ref list](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/Neighborly_CoG22.pdf)
- Language doc: components are Characters, Traits and Statuses, Locations, Actions, Roles. Trait grammar: `trait = default? (trait|status) NAMES ( argument* ) (opposes NAMES)?: proclivity+.` with `proclivity = VALENCE (tag (if conditional)? )`. VALENCE is a run of `+`/`-`, `never`, or `always`; "The probability of an action is a softmax for the sum of all proclivities that could impact it", with a temperature parameter. Example: `extrovert opposes introvert: +++(talking).` implies `introvert: ---(talking).`; conditional proclivity `+++(kind if >Self likes <Other)`. Argument sigils: `>` initiator, `<` recipient, `^` subject, `*` a past action, `@` a location, e.g. `gossip_about(>Gossipper, <Gossipee, ^GossipedAbout)`. — [Kismet language doc (Google Doc)](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1mhbraeSE9MJOnuzJgVaQk6MYo_G9AP3MD-3GYY9pQKM/edit?usp=sharing)
- Actions: `action NAME(args) (costs INT)? (extends NAME)? "DESCRIPTION": location: ...; tags: ...; responds: ...; if conditional; results: ...; visibility; response: ...`. Example: `action bore_with_chitchat(>Talker, <Listener) extends chitchat(>Talker, <Listener): tags: boring; result: Listener likes Talker -= 1.` Location groups: `location (Talker, Listener) (Target)` = Talker and Listener co-located where Target is absent; `location [drinking](Talker, Listener)` = at a location tagged drinking. Tags are "soft-gating"; `if` is a hard gate (conjunction only; an "or" requires a duplicate action). Knowledge conditions: `did | received | saw | heard | knows` and negations; `visibility` scales the chance an action is observed (global default "likely ... 1% or so"); the only knowledge effect is `CHARACTER heard ACTION`. — [Kismet doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1mhbraeSE9MJOnuzJgVaQk6MYo_G9AP3MD-3GYY9pQKM/edit?usp=sharing)
- Responses: `responds: -(rude,violent)` marks slap as a negative response to rude/violent actions; `response: Asked + "Asked accepts..." (Asker and Asked date each other), - "Asked rejects Asker" (Asker is sad, ...)`, chosen by the target's proclivities toward the root action's tags; `0` allows an ignore option. — [Kismet doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1mhbraeSE9MJOnuzJgVaQk6MYo_G9AP3MD-3GYY9pQKM/edit?usp=sharing)
- Locations and roles: `location NAME: name "TEXT"; tags: ...; supports: [1] pastor, [3] deacons, [20:40] parishioners; initialization: cast [1] pastor; each_turn: cast [20-40] parishioners.` Roles have tags and an `if` (e.g. `role bartender(>Tender) extends job(>Tender): tags: drinking; if Tender's age > 18.`); each role creates a non-selectable `cast_ROLE` action; action args can require role enactment at the location (`argue_with_boss(>Worker:worker, <Boss:boss)`). — [Kismet doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1mhbraeSE9MJOnuzJgVaQk6MYo_G9AP3MD-3GYY9pQKM/edit?usp=sharing)
- Patterns: `pattern love_triangle(>Suitor1,>Suitor2,>Target): if Suitor1 loves Target, Suitor2 loves Target.` used as a condition in `bicker_with_romantic_rival`; experimental temporal patterns: `temporal pattern get_revenge(RevengeSeeker, Target): if RevengeSeeker received [mean](>Target,<RevengeSeeker) ...[5-10] RevengeSeeker did [mean](<RevengeSeeker, >Target).` Initialization sketches generate founding families and locations with owners (the doc's later sections are unfinished notes and ASP fragments). — [Kismet doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1mhbraeSE9MJOnuzJgVaQk6MYo_G9AP3MD-3GYY9pQKM/edit?usp=sharing)
- Anthology's characterization: Kismet is "a lightweight social simulation specification language for facilitating the creation of small-scale scenarios, such as tabletop role-playing games", accessible to lay people, but "currently supports only a simple 'proclivity' model that determines the locations that agents travel to". Neighborly calls Kismet "a spiritual successor to Talk of the Town". — [Anthology, AIIDE 2022](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/21967/21736); [Neighborly](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/Neighborly_CoG22.pdf)

**Ceptre**
- Ceptre is "a rule specification language ... intended to enable rapid prototyping for experimental game mechanics, especially in domains that depend on procedural generation and multi-agent simulation", based on linear logic, which models "state change and actions without the need for a frame rule". — [Martens, "Ceptre", AIIDE 2015](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/12784/12632)
- Story-world type header: `at character location : pred. likes character character : pred. anger character character : pred. has character object : pred. depressed character : pred. dead character : pred.` Rule: `do/compliment : $at C L * $at C' L * $likes C C' -o likes C' C.` (`$` keeps a resource). Murder consumes anger as a resource: `do/murder : anger C C' * anger C C' * anger C C' * anger C C' * $at C L * at C' L * $has C weapon -o !dead C'.` — "we are using an emotion not just as a precondition but as a resource, where if we have enough of it, we can exchange it for a drastic consequence." Grief: `do/grieve : $at C L * likes C C' * dead C' -o depressed C * depressed C.` Initial state is a multiset like `{ at romeo town, ..., anger montague capulet, ..., likes mercutio romeo, ..., has tybalt weapon }`. — [Ceptre 2015](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/12784/12632)
- Rule selection is nondeterministic (uniform random by default); `#interactive` stages replace randomness with user choice; stages run to quiescence (`qui * stage act -o stage react.`) so voluntary actions and involuntary reactions (formOpinion rules) can be separated; traces form causal DAGs usable for debugging, feedback-loop analysis, and a "Tamara"-style generative hypertext. Limitation: first-order rule schemas lose decidability, so "exhaustive scenario analysis" is out of scope; Praxish notes Ceptre has no built-in NPC decision-making among applicable rules and cannot be in multiple stages at once. — [Ceptre 2015](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/12784/12632); [Praxish 2023](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/27537/27310)
- Ceptre tutorial: a rule `a * b * c -o d * e` replaces matching state components with `d` and `e`. — [Ceptre tutorial](https://github.com/chrisamaphone/interactive-lp/blob/master/tutorial.md)

**Anthology (NCSU, Martens lab, 2022)**
- Motive-based utility (after Millington & Funge), five motives on a 1–5 scale with periodic decay (Physical, Emotional, Social, Financial, Accomplishment, loosely Maslow); decision: for each possible action, `u = utility(agent, action) / (action.time + travel_time)`, pick max, ties random. JSON agent: `{ name: "Norma", motive: {accomplishment: 2, social: 2, physical: 4, emotional: 3, financial: 5}, relationships: [{type: "friend", with: "Quentin", valence: 3}, {type: "student-of", with: "MathProf", valence: 1}], currentLocation: {...}, currentAction: "wait_action" }`; location: `{ name: "Dining Hall", xPos: 5, yPos: 5, tags: ["food"] }`; action: `{ name: "attend_class", requirements: [{reqType: "location", hasAllOf: ["classroom"]}, {reqType: "people", relationshipsPresent: ["student-of"], minNumPeople: 2}], effects: [{motive: "accomplishment", delta: 1}], time_min: 75 }`. Requirement types: people, locations (tag sets), motives (thresholds). "Scheduled actions" are multi-agent (instigator action `attempt_hug`, targets get `accept_hug`/`reject_hug`); hidden actions only occur as responses. — [Anthology, AIIDE 2022](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/21967/21736)
- Usability goal: a working sim with 2–5 agents and 5–10 actions in under an hour without reading source; scalability: turn times under 1 ms until ~5,000 agents. Limitations: all agents share motives/decay; joint actions are immediate only; effects land at the end of an action, producing "an agent about to complete their 8-hr workday was interrupted by another agent that yelled at them, and consequently wasn't paid". Finding from their survey (Azad & Martens 2021, "Little Computer People"): "existing social simulation systems tend to invent new project-specific terminology and do not build on existing conceptual frameworks ... despite implementing very similar sets of social concepts", and "do not see use outside of their original research team." — [Anthology, AIIDE 2022](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/21967/21736)
- Azad's 2024 NCSU dissertation: "Little Computer People: A Taxonomy and Sandbox for Social Simulation" (advisors Jhala and Martens). — [Math Genealogy record](https://mathgenealogy.com/id.php?id=339459)

**Other logic-based threads**
- Social planning (Chang & Soo, AIIDE 2008): characters as BDI-ish agents with belief-revision and motivation rules compiled to PDDL with derived predicates; a social plan motivates other agents' actions; Iago reproduced Othello with a 14-step plan; "motivation sensitivity positively corresponds to possibility of story emergence" but also "can degrade the story by making goals too easily achievable" (Iago just asks Othello to kill Desdemona). Thespian is noted as decision-theoretic with lookahead limited to 2. — [Chang & Soo, AIIDE 2008](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/18666/18444)
- City of Gangsters' BotL (Zubek, Horswill et al., AIIDE 2021): periodic logic queries update relationship states; no composable practices. — [Praxish 2023](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/27537/27310)

### Inferences
- Kismet is the closest thing to a ready-made grammar for a village: trait→tag proclivities, location roles with casting ranges ("cast [10-20] patrons each turn"), observation visibility, and gossip via `heard`. Its own doc acknowledges only one knowledge effect and no disjunction, so it is a sketch, not a product.
- Ceptre's "emotion as spendable resource" is a neat formalization of slow-burn drama (anger accumulates, then is cashed in), and its stage/quiescence split is a clean way to separate voluntary actions from reactions.

### Gaps
- The Kismet ICCC 2020 workshop paper itself was not found; the Google Doc is informal and partly unfinished. Ceptre's social-simulation follow-ups (e.g. Ceptre Editor 2019) not read. Azad & Martens' taxonomy content not retrieved.

## Q6. Storylets / quality-based narrative, sifting, Wildermyth, Caves of Qud, Dwarf Fortress

### Takeaway
Storylets (content + prerequisites + effects) are the content-side formalism most practitioners converge on; selection can be player-chosen (Fallen London), salience-based (Left 4 Dead dialogue, Firewatch), or drama-managed; Wildermyth implements storylets over procedural heroes via "aspects", "hooks", relationships and role-casting; Dwarf Fortress and Caves of Qud generate history as a record to be read rather than as live drama, and Qud explicitly generates events first and rationalizes causes afterward.

### Cited Findings
**Storylets and QBN**
- Short (Nov 29 2019): a storylet has content, prerequisites "that determine when the content can play", and effects on world state; storylets are "simple, atomic, robust, and recombinable"; the definition deliberately omits how much content a storylet holds and how it is selected (deferring to Kreminski's survey). Examples: Fallen London (unlocked by resource levels, skill, or grinds), StoryNexus, Threaded Conversation (Inform), Versu and Character Engine (conversation elements as storylets inside procedural flows), Varytale, Lily's Garden, Bee. Pitfall: "time caves" need a unique flag per step, "too much combinatorial explosion". — [Emily Short, "Storylets: You Want Them"](https://emshort.blog/2019/11/29/storylets-you-want-them/)
- Short (Apr 12 2016): QBN = Failbetter's term for storylets unlocked by numerical "qualities"; strengths: modular, suits episodic content and many authors, gated storylets handle multi-step problems, later stories can add callbacks; weaknesses: unrewarding until many storylets exist, large libraries overwhelm players, multi-storylet arcs need "a lot of manual bookkeeping". Salience-based narrative (Short's term) selects from a pool by best fit to world state (Elan Ruskin's Left 4 Dead dialogue, Firewatch, King of Chicago); strengths: broad defaults plus specific overrides, adding content has few side effects; weaknesses: can trigger badly paced outcomes "because it lacks an authored dramatic arc", weight tuning "is largely trial and error". Waypoint narrative (Glass): NPCs pathfind through topic nodes toward trigger topics. — [Emily Short, "Beyond Branching"](https://emshort.blog/2016/04/12/beyond-branching-quality-based-and-salience-based-narrative-structures/)
- Academic survey: Kreminski & Wardrip-Fruin, "Sketching a Map of the Storylets Design Space", ICIDS 2018 (not read; referenced by Short). — [Emily Short tag page](https://emshort.blog/tag/quality-based-narrative/)

**Story sifting (Felt, Winnow, WAWLT, Loose Ends, Chroniqueur)**
- Felt (Kreminski, Dickinson, Wardrip-Fruin, ICIDS 2019): a DataScript-based sifting and simulation engine; events are entity-attribute-value triples (eventType, actor, target, tag); patterns use `eventSequence` with `not-join` exclusions; example "violation of hospitality" (guest enters town, host shows hospitality, host later harms guest, guest never left in between). Characters can use sifting to reason about the world. — [Felt GitHub](https://github.com/mkremins/felt)
- Winnow (Kreminski, Dickinson, Mateas, AIIDE 2021): "a declarative domain-specific query language for story sifting"; patterns like `(pattern violationOfHospitality (event ?e1 where eventType: enterTown, actor: ?guest) ... (event ?e2 where ... actor: ?host, ?host.value: communalism) ... (unless-event ?eMid between ?e1 ?e3 where ...))`; compiles to Felt; supports incremental sifting (while the sim runs) and partial sifting ("identifying the beginnings of compelling event sequences that haven't yet been completed"). — [Winnow GitHub](https://github.com/mkremins/winnow)
- WAWLT (FDG 2020): storyworld in DataScript via Felt; entities: characters, projects, institutions, events, relationships, impressions. Relationship entity = one character's view of another with role info and a numeric "charge"; "impression" = an evaluation caused by a specific introspection event, with a pointer to its cause; charge = sum of impression scores; a relationship holds up to three positive and three negative impressions, stronger ones displace weaker, and "gossip actions" copy impressions between characters. Design choice: "we instead assume that all characters know about every event, but that most characters only care about events that are somehow directly relevant to them ... subjective impressions, which are fewer in number and more reliably narratively consequential than granular facts." Characters use different sifting patterns to frame the same events, producing conflicts. Author goals ("Cast suspicion on character", "Introduce false lead") are heuristics that rank hundreds of possible actions; backstory simulation with longer-timescale actions ("write paper together", "take leave to parent") precedes play. Introspection actions are first-class and shown to players "to mitigate the Tale-Spin effect". — [Kreminski et al., WAWLT, FDG 2020](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/WAWLT_FDG2020.pdf)
- Loose Ends (AIIDE 2022): mixed-initiative interface for managing several plot threads at once; suggestions are structured events; preliminary expert evaluation suggests reduced "directionlessness". "Select the Unexpected: A Statistical Heuristic for Story Sifting" (ICIDS 2022) ranks sifted results by improbability. — [AAAI OJS Loose Ends](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/21955); [Lessard et al. 2026](https://www.pcgworkshop.com/archive/lessard2026narrative.pdf)
- Chroniqueur (Lessard et al., FDG 2026): a neolithic-settlement social sim with "causal bookkeeping"; 61 in-game years produced 228K events in 27K causal chains; heuristics tested: Schmid's eventfulness criteria (relevance, improbability, irreversibility, non-iterativity, effect) and ten of Polti's 36 dramatic situations mapped to event types (e.g. "Murderous Adultery: a lethal 'Jealous Violence' event", "An enemy loved: a 'Fall in Love' event toward a person in an enemy clan"). Result with 24 readers: "the results of this user evaluation do not show that our story heuristics have any significant relationship with reading enjoyment"; the favorite story was the control ("Gossip": a chief confides an affair while receiving food). Lessons: improbable causal chains are often "irrelevant improbability" (a meteor death leading to someone becoming attracted to rich people); "world-improbability does not equate narrative-improbability"; dramatic-situation searches return what you asked for ("if you look for a 'love triangle' ... you will not be surprised to find one"); emergent narratives "should probably not be expected to be appreciated in abstraction of their idiosyncratic worlds" (readers need familiarity with the world, as Dwarf Fortress players have). Affairs were "too prevalent ... in spite of multiple attempts to tone it down". — [Lessard, Friedrich, Paré-Chouinard, Kosseim, "Narrative Gold Digging", FDG 2026](https://www.pcgworkshop.com/archive/lessard2026narrative.pdf)
- Centrifuge (Johnson-Bey & Mateas, 2021): a visual tool for authoring sifting patterns for character-based simulationist storyworlds. — [Neighborly ref list](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/Neighborly_CoG22.pdf)

**Wildermyth**
- Designer Nate Austin: the game "[alternates] layers of handcrafted and procedural content"; central narrative with fixed beginning/middle/end plus procedural events from combat and character personality. — [Wikipedia: Wildermyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildermyth)
- Hero History: generated at start with three lines (Origin, Anecdote, Motivation) that affect prime and personality stats and seed Hooks; "History lines leave behind a number of Hooks, which are more specific character aspects and often take the form of a personality flaw or quirk"; history lines cannot be queried but can apply queryable aspects. — [Wildermyth wiki: History](https://wildermyth.com/wiki/History)
- Story selection: roles are cast in order ("the most important role should come first"), roles can be required with score thresholds, tags allow improvisation. Inputs: company state (e.g. "Has the company fought gorgons 3 times already?"), legacy unlocks, present heroes (`party` target), all heroes (`notAlreadyMatchedAs party`), particular heroes selected "by personality, relationship, hook, other aspects, or primary stat", `overlandTile`/`site` location details, foe factions, persistent NPCs via `npcId`. Outputs: gear, body modifications, history lines, stat boosts, hooks/aspects (Inhabited, Gorgonized, Wildheart) "to start follow-up stories", relationship boosts or aspect changes, overland and combat effects, recruiting/sacrificing heroes. Guidelines: "Risky choices should feel risky, and no choice should be purely negative"; options should "differ in kind, not just magnitude". — [Wildermyth wiki: Story Inputs and Outputs](https://wildermyth.com/wiki/Story_Inputs_and_Outputs)
- Relationships are Friendship, Romance, Rivalry, driven by event scenes and affecting gameplay (e.g. a lover's vengeance buff); players report repeated vignettes across campaigns. — [GDC Vault: "Getting Players Emotionally Invested in Procedural Characters in Wildermyth"](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027614/Independent-Games-Summit-Session-Getting); [RPG Site review](https://rpgsite.net/review/12107-wildermyth-review)

**Dwarf Fortress**
- Before play the world history is simulated "one week at a time for centuries"; civilizations' decisions are "shaped by their leaders' personalities"; a world's history "can exceed a gigabyte of text"; Legends mode lets players cross-reference figures and events. Dwarf biographies list personality traits, likes/dislikes, memories; the Adams brothers found "descriptions work best when they are tied to game mechanics" (a cosmetic foot-tapping trait "was judged a failure because players could not act on it"); a 2018 memory system stores strong seasonal events short-term, lets dwarves relive them with emotional effects, and promotes survivors to permanent memory that can change personality. Boatmurdered: a save passed among ~40 players one year each. Adams (2021): "We aren't even halfway to 1.0 yet." — [Aaron Reed, "2006: Dwarf Fortress", Sept 23 2021](https://if50.substack.com/p/2006-dwarf-fortress)
- Adams says he could not name a game before 2006 that tried to build a history. — [Kill Screen interview](https://www.killscreen.com/road-two5six-tarn-and-zach-adams/)
- Neighborly: Hennepin borrowed the Dwarf Fortress personality model because it lets users "more easily infer character behavior from personality traits". — [Neighborly](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/Neighborly_CoG22.pdf)

**Caves of Qud**
- History is "a set of historical events and group relationships centered around a set of five randomly generated ancient rulers, dubbed Sultans", inspired by Dwarf Fortress and Epitaph. — [Wikipedia: Caves of Qud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caves_of_Qud)
- Grinblat's GDC 2018 talk "Procedurally Generating History in Caves of Qud" shows "how he avoided building a full historical simulation by generating historical events and rationalizing them after the fact" (paper: Grinblat & Bucklew, "Subverting Historical Cause & Effect: Generation of Mythic Biographies in Caves of Qud", 2017). — [GDC Vault listing](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024990/Procedurally-Generating-History-in-Caves); [Caves of Qud media list](https://wiki.cavesofqud.com/wiki/User:Librarianmage/Sandbox/Caves_of_Qud/Media)
- Bucklew: static areas inside procedural worlds "meant we had a static backbone to deliver some of the deep world building and lore"; procedural elements let the team play their own game without it being "a mere review of a bunch of hand-crafted content". — [Game Developer, March 10 2022](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/tapping-into-the-potential-of-procedural-generation-in-caves-of-qud)

### Inferences
- The common pattern across Wildermyth, Fallen London and CiF instantiations is "storylet with role slots + prerequisites over character aspects + effects that plant hooks for later storylets"; the hook mechanic is the cheapest documented way to get multi-episode arcs out of atomic content.
- Sifting research has strong tooling (Felt/Winnow/Centrifuge) but weak evidence that any absolute heuristic finds stories readers like; Chroniqueur's 2026 null result and Ryan's dissertation both point to human-in-the-loop or player-situated sifting ("events relevant to the player character") as the workable form.
- Qud's "generate the event, then rationalize the cause" is a deliberate inversion of simulation and is worth considering for backstory in a town sim where coherence matters more than causal fidelity.

### Gaps
- Wildermyth's GDC talk content (behind GDC Vault) and the Qud mythic-biographies paper were not retrieved; Tarn Adams's own writing on DF personality/values ("Simulation principles from Dwarf Fortress", Game AI Pro 2) not read. Kreminski's storylet survey and "Select the Unexpected" details are secondhand.

## Q7. Drama managers / experience managers and pacing of emergent drama

### Takeaway
The experience-manager literature frames the problem as computing trajectories through state space under player interference (intervention vs accommodation), while the only widely shipped "director" is Left 4 Dead's, which runs a four-phase Build Up / Sustain Peak / Peak Fade / Relax loop on an estimated per-player intensity value; Kreminski's co-creative systems replace the hidden manager with player-stated "author goals" that rank simulated actions.

### Cited Findings
- Bates (1992) proposed the drama manager: "an intelligent, omniscient, and disembodied agent that monitors the virtual world and intervenes to drive the narrative forward according to some model of quality of experience"; an experience manager generalizes it beyond drama; "the user should not be aware of the existence of the experience manager or its interventions." Three axes: authorial intent (CYOA/plot graphs → generative managers), virtual character autonomy (strong story ↔ strong autonomy/"emergent narrative" per Aylett 1999), player modeling. Plot graphs encode precedence constraints with manager actions like "hinting, causing certain plot points to occur, and temporarily denying certain plot points"; search by adversarial search (Weyhrauch 1997), RL (Roberts et al. 2006), case-based planning. The "boundary problem" (Magerko 2005): respond to deviations by intervention (jam the gun, redirect NPCs) or accommodation (re-plan, recast a killed NPC's role). Open questions include "How to encode human authorial intent and at what level of abstraction?"; systems "have required substantive knowledge engineering expertise" and are "largely denizens of research labs" except Façade. — [Riedl & Bulitko, "Interactive Narrative: An Intelligent Systems Approach", AI Magazine 34(1), 2013](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/riedl2013interactive.pdf)
- Left 4 Dead AI Director (Booth, GDC 2009): goals include "Promote Replayability" and "Generate Dramatic Game Pacing"; static placement "Kills cooperation and degenerates into a race"; "Structured Unpredictability" superposes wanderers (high freq), mobs (20–30 infected every 90–180 s, 75% from behind), specials, bosses (Tank/Witch/Nothing shuffled along the escape route) and weapon caches. Adaptive Dramatic Pacing: "Estimate the 'emotional intensity' of each Survivor; Track the max intensity of all 4 Survivors; If intensity is too high, remove major threats for awhile; Otherwise, create an interesting population of threats." Intensity rises with damage taken and incapacitation, decays toward zero over time but "Do NOT decay ... if there are Infected actively engaging". Phases: Build Up (full threat until peak threshold), Sustain Peak (3–5 s minimum), Peak Fade (minimal threats, wait for a natural break), Relax (30–45 s or until far enough toward the next safe room). Inspired by Counter-Strike's "spiky" pacing: "Constant, unchanging combat is fatiguing. Long periods of inactivity are boring." Boss encounters are exempt from pacing. — [Booth, "The AI Systems of Left 4 Dead", Valve 2009](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- Façade's beat sequencer uses an Aristotelian tension arc and story-value effects (see Q1). — [AIIDE 2005](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIIDE/2005/AIIDE05-016.pdf)
- Versu's story manager is reactive and handcrafted per episode, spawning practices and tweaking desires, "at the expense of emergence at the narrative level"; Linden-era Versu added a lost-momentum detector that injects a new event. — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf); [Game Developer 2014](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/the-end-of-versu-emily-short-looks-back)
- Ryan rejects drama managers aesthetically; Short agrees a manager "can narrow what the simulation actually shows". — [Emily Short, May 23 2019](https://emshort.blog/2019/05/23/curating-simulated-storyworlds-james-ryan-2/)
- WAWLT: author goals are heuristic functions scoring every possible action; offscreen characters act autonomously by weighted random choice among high-scoring actions "to help mitigate writer's block"; in early scenes players pick escalation goals and later reconciliation goals. — [WAWLT, FDG 2020](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/WAWLT_FDG2020.pdf)
- Salience-based selection (Short 2016) is the lightweight alternative to a manager; its known failure mode is bad pacing because it "lacks an authored dramatic arc". — [Emily Short 2016](https://emshort.blog/2016/04/12/beyond-branching-quality-based-and-salience-based-narrative-structures/)

### Inferences
- A town sim can borrow L4D's loop almost directly: define a per-household or per-player "social intensity" (recent conflicts, revelations), decay it, and throttle the spawning of drama-generating practices (affairs, feuds) into build-up/relax phases, exempting a few authored "boss" events.
- The literature's consistent warning is that heavy managers suppress emergence and require expert knowledge engineering; WAWLT-style explicit goals or L4D-style throttling are the low-authoring-cost options.

### Gaps
- No source quantified how any drama manager performed against a no-manager baseline for emergent social drama. Kreminski's "Why Are We Like This?" has no evaluation; Loose Ends' evaluation was preliminary.

## Q8. The Sims-like strand: needs/motives, smart objects, FAtiMA, PsychSim, Thespian, Big Five, OCC

### Takeaway
The Sims' needs plus object "advertisements" is the baseline motive model that Anthology, Versu's authors and TotT all build on or react to; academic agent architectures split between appraisal-based emotion (FAtiMA, OCC) and decision-theoretic theory-of-mind (PsychSim/Thespian), and OCEAN-to-OCC mappings exist in the NPC literature but are thin on evidence.

### Cited Findings
- The Sims advertisement model: objects "broadcast what they can offer"; a Sim lists every object's advertised offers and "weighs each advertised number against its current needs" using a multiplier based on current motive levels (a bed's energy score is damped when rested and boosted when exhausted); lineage traced to SimAnt pheromones and the SimAntics scripting system (Don Hopkins). Forbus's 2003 Northwestern course had students edit object behaviors and advertisements with the Edith tool. — [Community write-up of Sims advertisements](https://playbooks.com/skills/simhacker/moollm/advertisement); [Forbus CS370 homework](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~forbus/CS370-Spring03/Homework/homework_eight.htm)
- Evans (Sims 3 AI lead) on the Sims' planner: estimated consequences ("he will relieve his bladder motive") miss conditional effects, causing the occupied-bathroom retry loop; FEAR has the same split between planner effects and gameplay effects. Sims 3 social norms "can only reference the two characters in an interaction" (CiF authors). — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf); [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)
- Anthology formalizes the needs model as five Maslow-ish motives with decay and time/travel-discounted utility (see Q5). — [Anthology 2022](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/21967/21736)
- FAtiMA: an open-source toolkit for agents with socio-emotional skills aimed at researchers and game developers; emotions "result from a subjective appraisal of events according with OCC Theory"; two appraisal layers: a reactive layer of "emotional reaction rules consisting of an event that triggers the rule and values for the OCC appraisal variables" and a deliberative layer appraising events against goals (hope/fear); FAtiMA Modular (Dias, Mascarenhas, Paiva 2014) is a generic framework for comparing appraisal theories. CiF's SIREN project paired CiF with FAtiMA, "an agent architecture that treats emotions as valenced evaluations of the world which affect and are affected by goals". — [FAtiMA Modular chapter (Springer)](https://www.doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-12973-0_3); [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)
- PsychSim/Thespian: Thespian "uses PsychSim's POMDP based agents to control each virtual character, with the character's personality and motivations encoded as the agent's goals"; PsychSim agents select actions decision-theoretically using beliefs, goals and theory of mind, and "the architecture does not consider affects in the action selection process" (2018 critique). Chang & Soo note Thespian's "maximum lookahead is limited at 2, as opposed to Iago's 14-step plan", so Thespian stories "rely more on causal sequences than problem-solving ones". — [Pynadath/Marsella IVA 2008 paper](https://people.ict.usc.edu/~pynadath/Papers/iva08.pdf); [SciTePress 2018 paper](https://scitepress.org/PublishedPapers/2018/65957/pdf/index.html); [Chang & Soo 2008](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/18666/18444)
- Neighborly's comparison: "Prom Week and PsychSim do not model locations at all"; PsychSim allows adding characters at runtime but they occupy no location; Ensemble and Versu use fixed casts. — [Neighborly 2022](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/Neighborly_CoG22.pdf)
- Big Five / OCC in NPCs: Baffa, Sampaio, Feijó & Lana (SBGames 2017) give each NPC an OCEAN profile that modulates event interpretation and emotion computation, with state machines driving attack/evade/befriend reactions; GAMYGDALA (Popescu, Broekens, van Someren, IEEE TAC 2014) is a "black-box" OCC appraisal engine where developers define goals and annotate events against them. — [Baffa et al. 2017](https://www.sbgames.org/sbgames2017/papers/ComputacaoFull/175357.pdf); [GAMYGDALA](https://ii.tudelft.nl/~joostb/files/Popescu_Broekens_Someren_2013.pdf)
- Personality representations used by the sim systems above: Big Five scalars (Talk of the Town), Dwarf Fortress's trait model (Hennepin), binary traits with weighted rules (CiF; 44 traits in Prom Week), "character values" (Neighborly), arbitrary logical wants (Versu: "as many personalities as there are sentences"), tag proclivities (Kismet). Evans (2008) earlier proposed "Representing personality traits as conditionals". — [Neighborly 2022](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/Neighborly_CoG22.pdf); [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf); [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)
- Versu's emotion model is deliberately minimal: one Ekman state at a time with target and cause recorded; the CiF model has no emotion component beyond statuses (angry at, heartbroken). — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf); [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf)

### Inferences
- The field's working consensus for playable social sims is not OCC-style appraisal but coarse, legible state: a few scalar networks plus labeled statuses and remembered causes. Appraisal architectures (FAtiMA, EMA) were used for pedagogical agents, and the one documented attempt to combine them with CiF (SIREN) was discontinued.
- "Traits must be tied to mechanics" (Dwarf Fortress) and "actions should change multiple qualities" (Versu) are the two personality-design rules that recur.

### Gaps
- I did not read the FAtiMA, PsychSim or Thespian primary papers; the quotes above come from search summaries of the linked PDFs and should be verified. No source gave evidence that Big Five scores produce recognizably different NPC behavior in a social sim.

## Q9. Recurring lessons: authoring bottleneck, combinatorics, legibility, the Tale-Spin effect, making emergent drama visible

### Takeaway
Every system above reports the same four problems: authoring cost grows with rules/behaviors not cast size; tuning weights is unrewarding; players cannot act on simulation state they cannot see (the Tale-Spin effect); and emergent output is mostly boring until something (a human, a sifter, a player's situated interest, or apophenia) curates it.

### Cited Findings
- Tale-Spin effect (Wardrip-Fruin): "works that fail to represent internal system richness on their surfaces, in an inversion of the Eliza effect"; Tale-Spin's characters plan and pursue goals but "little of this is visible to audiences in the resulting story, and little can even be deduced through repeated interaction"; the SimCity effect is the positive case where the interface teaches the player the model. — [Grand Text Auto EP 5.6, Feb 25 2008](https://grandtextauto.soe.ucsc.edu/2008/02/25/ep-56-re-reading-tale-spin); [Companion to Digital Literary Studies ch. 8](https://companions.digitalhumanities.org/DLS/content/9781405148641_chapter_8.html)
- Façade: communicating hidden social-game state was a stated failure; Versu responded with portraits, clickable emotion explanations, practice-grouped menus, and the SimCity-effect principle; Prom Week shows each character's ranked desires in a thought bubble; WAWLT exposes introspection actions "to mitigate the Tale-Spin effect"; Dwarf Fortress found cosmetic traits fail when players cannot act on them. — [AIIDE 2005](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIIDE/2005/AIIDE05-016.pdf); [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf); [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf); [WAWLT 2020](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/WAWLT_FDG2020.pdf); [if50 Dwarf Fortress](https://if50.substack.com/p/2006-dwarf-fortress)
- Authoring bottleneck numbers: Façade 27 beats / ~2,500 jdbs / 3 person-years; Prom Week 5,000+ influence rules; Versu 1,000+ actions in a year, 300+ desires, 2 months per 20-minute episode (under a week with Prompter); Praxish "multiple complex practices per day" once the framework stabilized; Anthology target of a 2–5 agent sim in under an hour. — [AIIDE 2005](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIIDE/2005/AIIDE05-016.pdf); [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf); [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf); [Praxish 2023](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/27537/27310); [Anthology 2022](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/21967/21736)
- Combinatorial explosion is the explicit motivation for CiF ("allowing for branching structures at discrete player choice points otherwise creates an exponentially increasing authorial burden"), Façade ("avoid the combinatorial explosion that occurs with complex causal event chains"), Riedl & Bulitko (branching graphs "quickly becomes intractable"), and Short's time-cave warning. — [CiF 2014](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf); [AIIDE 2005](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIIDE/2005/AIIDE05-016.pdf); [Riedl & Bulitko 2013](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/riedl2013interactive.pdf); [Short 2019](https://emshort.blog/2019/11/29/storylets-you-want-them/)
- Tuning: Versu's desire tuning is "difficult and unrewarding"; TotT worries about parameter interaction; Short says salience weight tuning "is largely trial and error"; Chroniqueur could not tone down affairs. — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf); [TotT ch. 37](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter37_Simulating_Character_Knowledge_Phenomena_in_Talk_of_the_Town.pdf); [Short 2016](https://emshort.blog/2016/04/12/beyond-branching-quality-based-and-salience-based-narrative-structures/); [Lessard 2026](https://www.pcgworkshop.com/archive/lessard2026narrative.pdf)
- Reuse failure: social-sim systems "invent new project-specific terminology" and "do not see use outside of their original research team"; TotT, CiF and Versu each needed a "rational reconstruction" (Neighborly, Ensemble, Praxish) to become reusable. — [Anthology 2022](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/21967/21736); [Neighborly 2022](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/Neighborly_CoG22.pdf); [Praxish 2023](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/27537/27310)
- Boringness and curation: Ryan's "overgenerate and curate"; Neighborly: "Simply running a social simulation and reporting everything that happens often results in 'boring' narratives: too many facts just told one after another"; TotT gossip was about hair color; Chroniqueur's null result on absolute heuristics and its suggestion of situated sifting around a protagonist; readers "need to be familiar with their source simulated worlds". — [Emily Short 2019](https://emshort.blog/2019/05/23/curating-simulated-storyworlds-james-ryan-2/); [Neighborly 2022](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/Neighborly_CoG22.pdf); [Lessard 2026](https://www.pcgworkshop.com/archive/lessard2026narrative.pdf)
- Apophenia as a design resource: low-fidelity life-event skeletons let players "piece together more elaborate stories than the simulation actually models"; WAWLT's editable transcript supports "extrapolative narrativization". — [Neighborly 2022](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/Neighborly_CoG22.pdf); [WAWLT 2020](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/WAWLT_FDG2020.pdf)
- Continuity and meaning: Versu argues isolated speech acts (Sims, Prom Week) lack continuity, which practices supply; Façade's tone-matching makes independent threads feel causally linked; Ryan's "causal bookkeeping" and Chroniqueur's causal chains make emergent events traceable; L4D's action transitions carry a human-readable "reason" string for debugging. — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf); [AIIDE 2005](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIIDE/2005/AIIDE05-016.pdf); [Lessard 2026](https://www.pcgworkshop.com/archive/lessard2026narrative.pdf); [Booth 2009](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- Relationship-change design rules (Versu): long-term relationship changes should be "strongly marked", require "active choice from both characters", and short-term changes should "happen frequently" with chances to back down or persist. — [Evans & Short 2014](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/evans2014versu.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a Stardew-like world the field's evidence suggests: keep the state model small and inspectable (CiF/Ensemble schema or an exclusion-logic database), reuse content across characters via roles, log every event with tags and provenance, give characters subjective impressions rather than full epistemic models unless gossip mechanics are core, surface reasons in the UI, and plan a sifting layer (even a simple Winnow-style pattern set anchored on the player's acquaintances) plus an L4D-style pacing throttle rather than a planning drama manager.
- Nothing in this literature demonstrates "soap opera" drama arising unprompted from a needs/relationship simulation; every showcased drama (Prom Week, Versu episodes, Bad News, WAWLT) had authored exchanges/practices or a human in the loop.

### Gaps
- No controlled study compares legibility interventions; the Tale-Spin/SimCity framing is critical theory, not measured. No source quantifies how many practices/exchanges a town-scale game would need; the closest datapoints are Prom Week (18 characters, 40+ exchanges, 5,000+ rules) and Versu (1,000+ actions for a handful of episodes).
