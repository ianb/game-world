# NPC decision-making architectures and narrative/dialogue authoring tools

Researched 2026-10-22. Scope: (a) NPC behavior architectures as practiced and taught by game AI practitioners, (b) dialogue/quest/narrative authoring tools and formats, with attention to JS/TS runtimes and suitability for authoring by an AI coding agent. Full game engines and LLM-driven NPCs are out of scope (covered by other researchers).

Sourcing note: several primary pages were behind anti-bot protection and could not be fetched directly (Valve Developer Community wiki, Creation Kit wiki at ck.uesp.net, GDC Vault was in maintenance). Where a finding relies on a search-engine snippet of such a page rather than a direct read, it is flagged "(snippet)". The free Game AI Pro chapters and Orkin's 2005 paper were downloaded and read in full.

## Utility AI: Infinite Axis Utility System, response curves, considerations, Sims-style needs, pitfalls

### Takeaway
Utility AI scores every available action from normalized [0,1] "considerations" passed through designer-tunable response curves and combined (multiplied in Dave Mark's IAUS, averaged in Graham's simpler model); it is the standard architecture for needs-driven, non-combat, "pick something sensible" behavior (The Sims lineage), and its known failure mode, oscillation between similarly scored actions, is handled with commitment bonuses, cooldown/runtime considerations, weights, or deciding only when the action queue is empty.

### Cited Findings
- Dave Mark's Infinite Axis Utility System (IAUS) is "a standalone, utility-only AI architecture" providing brains for "NPCs, squads, armies, and even inanimate objects"; inception 2012, briefly presented at GDC AI Summit 2013 ("Architecture Tricks: Managing Behaviors in Time, Space, and Depth"), covered at length at GDC 2015 with Mike Lewis of ArenaNet ("Building a Better Centaur: AI at Massive Scale", Guild Wars 2: Heart of Thorns). It is data-driven via a database-oriented design tool; a time-lapse showed designers building unique NPC AI packages in about 7 minutes; available as C++ or C# black box — [gameai.com IAUS page](https://gameai.com/iaus.php) (page copyright 2021)
- IAUS architecture as used in Guild Wars 2: Heart of Thorns (Mike Lewis, Game AI Pro 3, ch. 13, 2017): each possible action maps to a "decision score evaluator" (DSE); each think cycle every DSE is scored and the best-scoring option wins. A DSE's score is the product of its considerations; each consideration is a raw input normalized to [0,1] via "bookends" (e.g. 0 m to 100 m distance; above max scores 1), then remapped through a response curve. Any consideration scoring zero disqualifies the decision and the evaluator early-outs, so cheaper considerations are ordered first — [Game AI Pro 3 ch.13 PDF](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter13_Choosing_Effective_Utility-Based_Considerations.pdf)
- Concrete curves from Heart of Thorns: influence-map danger filter y = 1 − (x − 1)^4 (amplifies moderate danger, squelches trivial hazards); "runtime" consideration y = 1 − x^6 (drops a decision to zero after it has run a while, preventing endless evading); "cooldown" consideration y = x^5 (near-zero until the cooldown nearly ends, then spikes). Most DSEs start with two binary "switch" considerations (not mid-action, not rooted). Over one hundred premade consideration/behavior patterns were built and designers mixed them into new creatures "in a mere handful of minutes" — [Game AI Pro 3 ch.13](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter13_Choosing_Effective_Utility-Based_Considerations.pdf)
- Oscillation ("ping-pong") between two similarly scored decisions, per Lewis: mitigations are a "commitment bonus" (small boost to the last-chosen decision), per-decision weights so totals can be 3 or 4 instead of 1, runtime/cooldown considerations; all of these only move where oscillation lands. The simplest real fix is adding another consideration to one decision; otherwise reshape the curves. A slider-based tool for experimenting with considerations is recommended over solving analytically. Sequences can be built by cooldowns plus slightly descending weights — [Game AI Pro 3 ch.13](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter13_Choosing_Effective_Utility-Based_Considerations.pdf)
- Lewis's guidance for choosing considerations: ask "why would I do (or not do) this action?", then find data that proxies those motivations; prefer cheap proxy variables (a constant time instead of modeling invisibility duration), and let players narrate away rare mismatches; endpoints of curves decide whether an extreme input invalidates or merely deprioritizes a decision — [Game AI Pro 3 ch.13](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter13_Choosing_Effective_Utility-Based_Considerations.pdf)
- David "Rez" Graham's introduction (Game AI Pro 1, ch. 9, 2013): "every possible action is scored at once and one of the top scoring actions is chosen"; scores should be normalized 0–1 for consistency; expected utility EU = Σ D_i P_i (desire times probability); "decision factors" are each normalized and can be averaged, multiplied, min/max'd, and weighted (weights "emulate personality"); he recommends a drag-and-drop tool where designers wire factors into decisions. Worked example: ant colony Expand = avg(crowdedness 0.9, health 0.92) = 0.91 vs Breed = avg(health, nursery 0.15) = 0.535 — [Game AI Pro ch.9 PDF](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter09_An_Introduction_to_Utility_Theory.pdf)
- Graham on inertia: deciding every frame with two actions at 0.5 produces frantic oscillation; fixes are (1) weight the action currently in progress, (2) cooldowns with very high weight for staying, decaying over time, (3) only decide when the current action finishes. "On The Sims Medieval, a Sim would only attempt to make a decision when their interaction queue was empty. Once they chose an action, they would commit to performing that action." His demo chooses among scored actions with a weighted random rather than a strict argmax — [Game AI Pro ch.9](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter09_An_Introduction_to_Utility_Theory.pdf)
- Merrill on normalization: response curves "allow the curve to be broken up into segments that can be further fine-tuned [Mark 10]"; they are "often the target of on-the-fly tuning", so build them as reloadable data; also convert inputs to a common unit (health gained vs health lost, with exponent e.g. pow(HealthLost,1.2)) before combining — [Game AI Pro ch.10 PDF](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter10_Building_Utility_Decisions_into_Your_Existing_Behavior_Tree.pdf)
- The Sims: each Sim has motive meters ranging −100 to 100; each interaction gets a weighted score from current motive levels, personality traits and proximity; objects "advertise" their ability to satisfy needs ("intelligence sits partly in the environment"); the AI chooses randomly among the top-scoring options rather than always the best; in The Sims 3 the environment itself has motives such as a 50/50 gender balance or 80% employment and adjusts background characters toward them — [Game Industry Library summary of GMTK "The Genius AI Behind The Sims"](https://gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/gmtk-the-genius-ai-behind-the-sims). Motive list in The Sims 1: hunger, hygiene, fun, energy, bladder, social, comfort, room (snippet) — [GMTK transcript on Amara](https://amara.org/subtitles/cVoJS4OdmVql/en/1/download/The%20Genius%20AI%20Behind%20The%20Sims.en.txt)
- The 2003 affordance-theory paper names The Sims (2002) as an affordance-based system in which "a Sims modeler programs the environment, not the agents" (snippet) — [Affordance Theory for Improving the Rapid Generation... (BRIMS 2003)](https://www.seas.upenn.edu/~barryg/BRIMS2003_AffordanceTheory.doc)
- Wikipedia's utility-system article records that behavior trees "require the designer to specify priorities in sequence, whereas utility systems let behaviors sort themselves out by priority based on the scores", and that the IAUS is a stand-alone data-driven architecture needing little programming once wired to a game's inputs/outputs (snippet) — [Wikipedia: Utility system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility_system)
- Example of how a commercial utility tool exposes curves: Utility Intelligence (Unity) response curves have Curve Type, Slope, Exponent, XShift, YShift parameters plus presets (snippet) — [Utility Intelligence docs](https://uintel-go.utilityworlds.com/Documentation/IntelligenceEditor/ConsiderationTab/)

### Inferences
- Sims-style needs map directly onto IAUS: each need level is one consideration axis (bookended −100..100 → 0..1) with a curve that rises steeply as the need becomes urgent; an object's "advertisement" is the per-action consideration set. The Sims 1 design of advertising from objects and scoring in the Sim is the same split IAUS formalizes as DSE + considerations.
- For an everyday-life NPC the practical authoring unit is a reusable "consideration pattern" (Lewis's 100+ premade patterns), which is text/data and therefore well suited to being generated and tuned by a coding agent, provided there is a harness to replay scenarios and catch oscillation.
- Multiplicative combination (IAUS) gives veto semantics for free (any zero kills the action); averaging (Graham) does not, so averaging needs explicit validity checks.

### Gaps
- Dave Mark's own slides for the 2013/2015 IAUS talks are on GDC Vault, which was in maintenance during research; the "compensation factor" formula he uses for multiplying many considerations (to counter score collapse with many axes) could not be confirmed from a primary source.
- No primary (Maxis) description of The Sims' advertisement curves or the exact scoring formula was found; the details above come from a journalist's analysis.

## GOAP (F.E.A.R., Orkin) and HTN (Killzone/Horizon, Transformers): what they buy for everyday-life NPCs vs combat, and their cost

### Takeaway
GOAP (backward A* over a small symbolic state) buys emergent re-planning and reusable modular actions at the cost of CPU, opaque behavior for designers, and difficulty authoring fixed sequences; HTN (forward decomposition over ordered methods) keeps designer-readable priority order, supports partial plans and cheap re-planning, and is the planner Guerrilla pairs with utility scoring in Horizon. Both earn their keep where plans must chain dependencies (get key, open door, use object); for schedule-driven daily life most studios reach for packages/BTs/utility and reserve planners for reactive problem-solving.

### Cited Findings
- Orkin (Monolith, 2005), "Agent Architecture Considerations for Real-Time Planning in Games": the F.E.A.R. architecture is derived from MIT Media Lab's C4 (sensors → working memory → decision → blackboard → subsystems) with C4's "Action Tuples" replaced by a real-time planner. "Only one goal may be active at a time"; when sensors detect significant change the agent re-evaluates goal relevance and the planner finds the action sequence for the most relevant goal; actions activate by setting blackboard members (e.g. GotoTarget sets a destination; navigation responds next update) — [Orkin 2005 PDF](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/orkin2005agent.pdf)
- World state is "a fixed sized array of key-value pairs" indexed by enumerated symbols (values: int, float, bool, handle, enum, or symbol reference). Appendix A lists the entire symbol set, 17 symbols: AnimPlayed, AtNode, AtNodeType, AtTargetPos, DisturbanceExists, Idling, PositionIsValid, RidingVehicle, ReactedToWorldStateEvent, TargetIsAimingAtMe, TargetIsDead, TargetIsFlushedOut, TargetIsSuppressed, TraversedLink, UsingObject, WeaponArmed, WeaponLoaded. Appendix B lists ~40 actions (Attack, AttackFromNode, AttackMelee, DodgeRoll, DrawWeapon, EscapeDanger, FlushOutWithGrenade, GotoNode, GotoTarget, InspectDisturbance, LookAtDisturbance, Reload, SuppressionFire, TraverseBlockedDoor, UseSmartObjectNode, ...). Preconditions are a conjunction only; each symbol appears once per action; "no means of describing who a symbol refers to", solved by an "agent centric" representation (TargetIsDead rather than per-enemy health) with subsystems (target selection) handling the details — [Orkin 2005](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/orkin2005agent.pdf)
- Planning is A* over world states; heuristic = number of unsatisfied goal symbols; actions hashed by effect symbol so candidates are found instantly; "context preconditions" (arbitrary code querying working memory) prune further; action costs steer style: giving generic Attack cost 5.0 vs default 1.0 makes GotoNode+AttackFromCover (2.0) win. Designer-placed objects can declare dependencies, so plans chain: GotoNode(TableNode), UseObject(Table), GotoNode(NodeCover78), AttackFromCover() — [Orkin 2005](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/orkin2005agent.pdf)
- Sensors and working memory: expensive checks (raycasts, pathfinding, tactical-node sorting) run in sensors distributed over frames; SensorNodeCombat polls 3 times per second; limiting to one sensor per agent update caused "noticeably delayed reactions", fixed by having sensors return true only when they did significant work. All knowledge is a WorkingMemoryFact: a record of 16 attributes (position, direction, stimulus type, object handle, desire, update time...), each attribute carrying a confidence 0.0–1.0 (visual stimulation, proximity, degree of desire). Ten fact types (Character, Object, Disturbance, Task, PathInfo, Desire...). Centralized memory gives "persistent context that is often lost in FSM or scripted systems" (an NPC interrupted on a ladder remembers it). Garbage collection of stale facts was ad hoc; Orkin suggests expiration times on all facts — [Orkin 2005](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/orkin2005agent.pdf)
- Emergence and limits: NPCs outside a grenade's blast radius turned to watch it land because LookAtDisturbance was the next-best action to clear DisturbanceExists, "not intentionally implemented". Future-work list: no scheduling facility (ordering must be forced through more restrictive preconditions, making actions less generic), no compound/hierarchical actions for designer-mandated sequences, no squad planning — [Orkin 2005](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/orkin2005agent.pdf)
- A practitioner's reason for abandoning a STRIPS planner (Bill Merrill, Game AI Pro ch.10): "In the case of the planner, it was too organic and mysterious for the designers' comfort. Additionally, emergent behavior is still not a desirable feature most of the time. Planners also make it difficult to string together specific sequences of actions in a defined order when needed." FSMs were dropped for lack of modularity and messy transition logic — [Game AI Pro ch.10](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter10_Building_Utility_Decisions_into_Your_Existing_Behavior_Tree.pdf)
- HTN as shipped in Transformers: Fall of Cybertron (Troy Humphreys, Game AI Pro 1 ch. 12, 2013): a total-order forward decomposition planner. World state is `vector<byte>` indexed by an enum (WsEnemyRange ∈ {MeleeRange, ViewRange, OutOfRange}, WsHealth, WsIsTired); it "only needs to represent the problem space that our planner needs". Primitive task = Preconditions + Operator + Effects, e.g. `Primitive Task [SprintToEnemy] Preconditions [WsHasEnemy == true] Operator [NavigateTo(EnemyLoc, Speed_Fast)] Effects [WsLocation = EnemyLoc, WsIsTired = true]`. Compound task = ordered methods, each `[conditions] Subtasks [...]`; method order is priority. Sensors are "time-sliced" and write the world state — [Game AI Pro ch.12 PDF](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter12_Exploring_HTN_Planners_through_Example.pdf)
- HTN re-planning pitfalls and fixes: a world-state change mid-plan can cause a re-plan that aborts a still-valid lower-priority tail (RecoveryRoar) because effects already applied (AttackedRecently = true) make the planner fall through to a lower method. Rather than adding A*/Dijkstra costs ("tuning these costs can be pretty tricky"), the shipped fix is a Method Traversal Record (MTR): the list of method indices chosen per compound task, compared lexicographically so a new plan replaces the running one only if it is higher priority. Partial plans: because HTN plans forward from the current state it can stop after a few steps, whereas "GOAP and STRIPS planner variants ... use a backward search ... the planner has to complete the entire search in order to know what first step to take" — [Game AI Pro ch.12](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter12_Exploring_HTN_Planners_through_Example.pdf)
- Guerrilla's Horizon Zero Dawn AI talk description (page dated 17 Oct 2017): the AI uses "a combination of HTN planning and utility based decision making" plus systems to coordinate groups of agents and navigation for agents of different sizes in a changing environment; the move from level-based tactical shooters (Killzone) to open-world required new systems — [Guerrilla: The AI of Horizon Zero Dawn](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/the-ai-of-horizon-zero-dawn). Guerrilla's site also lists a November 2024 talk "HTN Planning in Decima" on the planner driving NPC high-level decisions (snippet) — [Guerrilla reading list](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read)
- Warhorse (Kingdom Come: Deliverance II) gave a GDC Game AI Summit talk "Combining GOAP and modular behavior trees" for NPC behavior (listing only, snippet) — [GDC Vault listing](https://gdcvault.com/play/1035576/Game-AI-Summit-Combining-GOAP)
- Orkin's own summary of the trade: planning "offers several benefits over ... scripts or finite state machines ... better equipped to handle unexpected situations ... modular ... re-use, sharing, and maintenance" but "at the cost of CPU cycles", requiring distributed processing and caching that "affect the agent architecture as a whole" — [Orkin 2005](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/orkin2005agent.pdf)
- A 2019 French academic analysis found that F.E.A.R.'s action costs have little impact in practice (snippet) — [Cazenave et al., RJCIA 2019](https://www.lamsade.dauphine.fr/~cazenave/papers/rjcia2019.pdf)

### Inferences
- For everyday-life NPCs, the valuable planner feature is dependency chaining through objects (fetch key → unlock door → cook), which Orkin shows via designer-authored object dependencies and which an HTN expresses as compound tasks with methods. Combat is where GOAP's reactive re-planning shines; daily routines are mostly fixed sequences, which is exactly what Orkin lists as awkward in GOAP ("no scheduling facility") and what HTN methods express naturally.
- The HTN world state (a byte vector of abstracted properties) and the GOAP symbol array are both tiny, deliberately abstracted blackboards. Any planner for a living world needs the same discipline: a handful of agent-centric symbols, with sensors (not the planner) doing expensive queries.
- An HTN domain in the Humphreys notation is plain text and is a good target for an AI coding agent; MTR-based priority gives deterministic, explainable replan decisions, which matters for testing.

### Gaps
- The actual slides for "Three States and a Plan" (GDC 2006), Guerrilla's 2018 "Beyond Killzone" and 2024 "HTN Planning in Decima" talks, and Warhorse's KCD2 GOAP+BT talk were not readable (GDC Vault down; Guerrilla PDF not fetched), so Horizon's specific HTN/utility split and KCD2's GOAP usage for daily-life NPCs are unconfirmed.
- Sergio Osimo's open-source HTN planner "informed by" Guerrilla's was mentioned only in a LinkedIn snippet; the repository was not located.

## Behavior trees: strengths, "sprawl", and hybrid BT+utility selectors

### Takeaway
Behavior trees are the industry default because designers can read and debug them, but priority is static and baked into the tree, so context-dependent priorities force subtree duplication (sprawl); the standard fix (Merrill, Game AI Pro 2013; Opsive and others ship it) is a utility selector node that scores children with normalized utilities, optionally with decorators reshaping utility as it propagates up and with evaluation separated from execution.

### Cited Findings
- Champandard & Dunstan, "The Behavior Tree Starter Kit" (Game AI Pro 1 ch. 6): covers sequences (AND), selectors (OR), active selectors, parallels with explicit success/failure policies, decorators (repeat, invert), monitors (continuously checking assumptions), filters (preconditions as a sequence prefix), and an event-driven traversal with a SUSPENDED status that "scales up significantly better on modern hardware"; two schools on interrupting running behaviors (noninterruptible flag vs. immediate abort) — [Game AI Pro ch.6 PDF](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter06_The_Behavior_Tree_Starter_Kit.pdf)
- Merrill: BTs "strike a strong overall balance across ease of implementation, ease of visualization, and adoptability", used by Bungie (Halo) and Crytek (Crysis 2); "designers can effectively visualize what's happening, in real-time". The limitation: "In a standard behavior tree, priority is static. It is baked right into the tree ... The same behavior may require different relative priorities, depending on the context. Behavior tree authors often deal with this conundrum by duplicating sections of the tree at different branches ... this still becomes inefficient, verbose, and potentially fragile." Example: Seek Medic duplicated under Combat and Relaxed with different conditions — [Game AI Pro ch.10](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter10_Building_Utility_Decisions_into_Your_Existing_Behavior_Tree.pdf)
- Merrill's hybrid: a UtilitySelector composite scores valid children and picks the best; decorators can transform a child's utility as it propagates (e.g. cubing Reload's utility to delay urgency, Fig. 10.4/10.5); his tree separates Evaluate() from execution so "the utility selector is able to evaluate its children prior to calculating utility, meaning it must update utility only for valid children", costly utility work can be cached in Evaluate(), the tree can be re-evaluated in parallel with execution and "only interject if the results vary from the presently executing plan", and whole-tree evaluation allows limited look-ahead validation of a plan — [Game AI Pro ch.10](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter10_Building_Utility_Decisions_into_Your_Existing_Behavior_Tree.pdf)
- Opsive's Behavior Designer Pro documents a Utility Selector "based on the implementation suggested by the Game AI Pro book"; it selects the highest-utility child and re-evaluates its children every tick (snippet) — [Opsive Utility Selector docs](https://opsive.com/support/documentation/behavior-designer-pro/concepts/tasks/composite/utility-selector/)
- Blackboards as practiced in Unreal: `UBlackboardData` is a data asset defining keys, with a Parent blackboard whose keys are inherited/overridable; `UBlackboardComponent` stores live values in one memory block with per-key offsets and tracks observers notified on key change; key types (`UBlackboardKeyType`) define value size and supported decorator test operations; decorators such as `UBTDecorator_BlackboardBase` select a key and receive `OnBlackboardKeyValueChange` (snippet of API docs) — [UE 4.27 UBlackboardComponent](https://docs.unrealengine.com/4.27/en-US/API/Runtime/AIModule/BehaviorTree/UBlackboardComponent/), [UBlackboardData](https://docs.unrealengine.com/4.27/en-US/API/Runtime/AIModule/BehaviorTree/UBlackboardData/index.html)
- Orkin's F.E.A.R. blackboard/working-memory split is the same idea in a planner: actions write intent to the blackboard, subsystems (navigation, animation) read it at their own rate — [Orkin 2005](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/orkin2005agent.pdf)
- Warhorse's KCD2 combines GOAP with "modular behavior trees" (listing only) — [GDC Vault listing](https://gdcvault.com/play/1035576/Game-AI-Summit-Combining-GOAP)

### Inferences
- A practical living-world stack that practitioners converge on: a utility (or package) layer chooses *what* to do, a BT or HTN expands *how* (the fixed sequence of steps), and a blackboard carries state between them. The utility-selector-in-a-BT pattern is the minimal version of this.
- BT "sprawl" matters most for daily-life NPCs because the same behavior (eat, sleep, chat) must appear under many contexts; a utility selector over a flat list of behaviors avoids the duplication.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study of BT size/sprawl in shipped games was found; the evidence is practitioner testimony.

## Schedule-driven NPCs: daily schedule with interruptions and reactions (packages, goal stacks, time-of-day, sensors, broken plans)

### Takeaway
The dominant production pattern is a prioritized list of time-windowed "packages"/schedule entries evaluated top-down with conditions (Bethesda), or a per-character keyed schedule table resolved once per day (Stardew Valley), layered under a higher-priority reaction layer that preempts the routine and then resumes it; sensors/stimuli write to a shared memory with confidence and expiry, and "plan broken" is handled by re-evaluation (goal relevance in GOAP, MTR-priority replanning in HTN, or falling to the next package).

### Cited Findings
- Bethesda packages: "AI-Packages (or NPC-Packages) control the behaviour of the actors in Skyrim. They specify where an NPCs works/eats/sleeps, how he behaves in combat or when he discovers a dead body"; the "Once Per Day" flag only works when a package has no start time or duration; debugging packages is "cumbersome" (helper mods: More Informative Console, Package Debug Helper) — [Beyond Skyrim wiki: NPC Packages](https://wiki.beyondskyrim.org/wiki/Arcane_University:NPC_Packages)
- Creation Kit wiki (snippets; pages blocked to direct fetch): "Procedures take inputs or parameters that further define behavior", e.g. a Travel procedure takes the destination; the Bethesda packages tutorial "recommends a sandbox package as a fallback at the bottom of an NPC's package list" and "covers creating a simple daily schedule for an NPC" — [CK wiki: Procedure](https://ck.uesp.net/wiki/Procedure), [Bethesda Tutorial Packages](https://ck.uesp.net/wiki/Bethesda_Tutorial_Packages). A modder reports a sandbox package template contains three procedures (Travel, Unlock Doors, Sandbox) and that selecting a procedure in the tree view shows its description (snippet) — [Nexus forum thread](https://forums.nexusmods.com/topic/5494762-le-ai-packages-and-procedure-comprehension)
- The AI Overhaul mod author describes Skyrim's packages as the "Radiant AI" system with a "procedure tree" for complex behavior, and the mod works by adjusting package data, AI overrides and idle markers; modders push packages via quest aliases or Papyrus scripts when the static package list is too rigid; packages with conditions "often fail in Special Edition" per forum reports (snippets) — [How AI Overhaul works (Nexus article)](https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/articles/2467), [Nexus thread on NPC mods](https://forums.nexusmods.com/topic/880695-a-much-better-more-npcs-mod)
- Stardew Valley schedule data (a concrete, text, per-NPC format): one file per character in `Content/Characters/schedules`; the game picks one schedule key per day in a fixed precedence (GreenRain special; married NPCs use only `marriage_*` keys; otherwise `<festival>_<day>`, `<season>_<day>`, `<day>_<hearts>`, `<day>`, `bus`, `rain2` (50%)/`rain`, `<season>_<dayOfWeek>_<hearts>`, `<season>_<dayOfWeek>`, `<dayOfWeek>_<hearts>`, `<dayOfWeek>`, `<season>`, `spring_<dayOfWeek>`, `spring` (mandatory fallback), `default`). Heart-gated keys apply if any player has at least that many hearts; the highest requirement wins. A value is a `/`-separated list of points `<time> [location] <tileX> <tileY> [facing 0-3] [animation] [dialogue]`; time is 24h without colon (1000 = 10:00), a leading `a` means "arrive by"; `bed` is a special location; animation names containing `sleep` put the NPC to sleep. Initial commands: `GOTO <key>`, `NOT friendship <npc> <hearts> ...`, `MAIL <letterId>/<cmdIfNot>/<cmdIf>`. Example: `"900 SeedShop 39 5 0/1030 ScienceHouse 5 19 1/2200 SeedShop 1 9 3 abigail_sleep"`; `"NOT friendship Sebastian 6/GOTO 11"` — [Stardew Valley Wiki: Modding:Schedule data](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Modding:Schedule_data)
- Assassin's Creed Origins (Ubisoft, May 2018): every NPC "is active at all times and follows a schedule covering sleep, eating, work, and travel", animals too; the "meta AI" has a tracking system for world objects, a spawning system for their visual representation, and a behavior system assigning goals; liveliness comes from conflicting needs (a hungry hippo drawn toward a guard; tired guards easier to sneak past) — [GameRevolution summary of Ubisoft blog](https://www.gamerevolution.com/news/386683-ubisoft-talks-about-building-assassins-creed-origins-world-through-ai)
- Ambient vs latent behaviors (University of Alberta ScriptEase, AAAI 2007): "An ambient behavior is spontaneously initiated by an NPC (proactive) or performed in response to a collaborative behavior initiated by another NPC (reactive). A game event may interrupt the NPC during an ambient behavior, triggering a latent behavior. When the latent behavior completes, the NPC resumes the interrupted ambient behavior." Ambient collaborative behaviors have higher priority than ambient independent ones and the independent behavior resumes afterwards. A guard pattern: Patrol, Rest, Check chest, Converse-Talk/Listen on a topic with eye-contact matching; at end of shift goes to the tavern; flees if the item is stolen. The paper notes Oblivion's Radiant AI still fails to react to theft, spellcasting or nearby attacks (citing Spronck 2006) — [AAAI 2007 demo paper PDF](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/18798/18798-52-22506-1-10-20210929.pdf)
- A scheduler where "higher-priority schedules automatically interrupt lower-priority ones when awareness changes", e.g. dropping wander for combat on threat detection (Facepunch s&box docs, snippet) — [s&box docs via Algolia](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/facepunch/sandbox)
- "What to do when the plan is broken" in GOAP: a blocked door invalidates GotoTarget; the NPC's goal set is re-evaluated, TraverseLink becomes relevant, TraverseBlockedDoor's context-effect records the door as blocked in working memory, the path planner then routes around and the NPC dives through a window; "the biggest benefit comes from the ability to re-plan" — [Orkin 2005](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/orkin2005agent.pdf)
- In HTN: re-plan on world-state change, but only replace the running plan if the new MTR is higher priority; keep plans partial so little is wasted when the world changes ("There isn't much point to planning too far into the future since there is a good chance the world state could change") — [Game AI Pro ch.12](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter12_Exploring_HTN_Planners_through_Example.pdf)
- Sensory/stimulus model (F.E.A.R.): SensorSeeEnemy dormant until a visual stimulus arrives then raycasts; SensorHearDisturbance creates one fact per disturbance for the planner to investigate; facts carry stimulus confidence and update time; stimulus-type and "Disturbance" facts drive InspectDisturbance/LookAtDisturbance/ReactToDanger/EscapeDanger, all of which clear DisturbanceExists — [Orkin 2005](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/orkin2005agent.pdf)
- Coordination via "situations" in Hitman: Absolution: an NPC needing coordination (e.g. stopping a trespasser) subscribes to a situation object which keeps the subscriber list, assigns roles ("leader"/"other") and updates each NPC's knowledge (its situation, who leads, how aggressively to react) (secondary source) — [AAAI paper citing IO Interactive 2012](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/12715/12715-52-16232-1-2-20201228.pdf)
- Kingdom Come: Deliverance II NPC scale: "quadruples the number of NPCs on the map to nearly 2400 ... concentrates around half of that into a single city"; "new techniques for the level of detail of the AI simulation had to be introduced, while making the scripting interface as oblivious to the LODs as possible"; takeaways: thousands of complex NPCs at 60 FPS; plan limits early; thread usage; move standardized logic into code (GDC 2026 session abstract, Petr Smrcek, snippet) — [GDC schedule listing](https://schedule.gdconf.com/session/supporting-thousands-of-npcs-in-kingdom-come-deliverance-kingdom-come-deliverance-ii/915120)

### Inferences
- Across Bethesda, Stardew, Ubisoft and the academic ScriptEase model the same three layers appear: (1) a routine layer keyed by time/day/conditions, resolved by first-match precedence; (2) a reaction/latent layer that preempts by priority; (3) a resume step. For an agent-authored world the Stardew key precedence plus Bethesda-style conditions is a compact, text-first data model; the Facepunch/ScriptEase "priority preempts, then resume" is the runtime rule.
- "Goal stack" behavior is best modeled as a stack of (goal, resume-info) frames: Orkin's point that working memory keeps the interrupted context (ladder) is what makes resumption cheap.
- A tick-based world clock with schedule entries at coarse times (Stardew uses 10-minute granularity in its examples, e.g. 1030) keeps the routine layer trivially deterministic.

### Gaps
- The Creation Kit wiki's Package page (schedule tab fields month/day/date/hour/duration, package stack evaluation order and re-evaluation frequency, "Must Complete"/"Interrupt Override" flags) was blocked by anti-bot protection on ck.uesp.net and cs.uesp.net, so these specifics are not confirmed here.
- No primary source was found describing Bethesda's internal package evaluation frequency or Ubisoft's schedule data format.

## Smart objects / affordances and why they reduce NPC-brain complexity

### Takeaway
Smart objects move "how to use me" (animation, positioning, preconditions/effects, slots, reservation) into the object, so the NPC brain only needs to pick an affordance by need; this is how The Sims scales to any object set, how F.E.A.R. chained object dependencies into plans, and how Unreal's Smart Objects (slots + claim/release + per-slot StateTree) package it today.

### Cited Findings
- "A Sims modeler programs the environment, not the agents"; behavior emerges from what objects offer rather than from modeled inner states (BRIMS 2003, snippet) — [Affordance Theory paper](https://www.seas.upenn.edu/~barryg/BRIMS2003_AffordanceTheory.doc)
- Objects in The Sims "advertise their ability to satisfy specific needs, so intelligence sits partly in the environment rather than only in the characters" — [Game Industry Library summary of GMTK](https://gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/gmtk-the-genius-ai-behind-the-sims)
- Industry smart objects vs academic: "A typical smart object is a graphical entity in the game world that is accompanied by a character animation, and it also handles positioning"; Kallmann's original smart objects had multiple interacting parts and could run code to change their own state; a planning formulation gives each object states plus affordances with pre- and post-conditions (snippets) — [Using Behavior Objects to Manage Complexity in Virtual Worlds (arXiv 1508.00377)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.00377), [Domain Authoring Assistant for Intelligent Virtual Agents (arXiv 1904.03266)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.03266)
- F.E.A.R. has a UseSmartObjectNode action and designer-placed "invisible game objects" with dependencies on other objects, e.g. "an NPC needs to flip the table over before taking cover behind it"; the planner chains the extra actions, with "no limit to the number of dependencies" — [Orkin 2005](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/orkin2005agent.pdf)
- Unreal Engine 5.8 docs: "Smart Objects represent a set of activities in the level that can be used through a reservation system" — [UE Smart Objects landing page](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/smart-objects-in-unreal-engine). API snippets: the SmartObjectSubsystem holds registered instances and provides spatial queries and reservations; gameplay tags can be set on instances or individual slots (`get_slot_tags`); a claim is made from a query result and the handle binds object, slot and user id; change reasons include ON_CLAIMED/ON_RELEASED; Mass AI has a task that claims a smart object from search results and releases it when done; with Gameplay Interactions each slot runs its own StateTree — [SmartObjectSubsystem Python API](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/python-api/class/SmartObjectSubsystem), [FSmartObjectRuntimeSlot](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Plugins/SmartObjectsModule/FSmartObjectRuntimeSlot), [SmartObjectChangeReason](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/python-api/class/SmartObjectChangeReason)
- Planning-based smart objects "does not fit well to the domain of applications with highly dynamic v-worlds as computer games are" (IVE 2006, snippet) — [IVE overall 2006 PDF](https://artemis.ms.mff.cuni.cz/main/papers/IVE-overall-2006.pdf)
- An ITU prototype extends smart objects with territories and an "Affordance Tree" for social simulation (snippet) — [ITU publication page](https://pure.itu.dk/en/publications/a-prototype-using-territories-and-an-affordance-tree-for-social-s/)

### Inferences
- The minimum useful smart-object record for a 2D town is: id, position/slots (tile + facing), affordances (verb → need deltas, duration, preconditions, effects), capacity/reservation, and an "advertisement" function of the querying NPC. This is exactly the data an IAUS consideration set consumes and an HTN primitive operator targets.
- Smart objects also make content agent-authorable: adding a new object with its affordances adds behavior to every NPC without touching any brain.

### Gaps
- The UE Smart Objects overview page is JavaScript-rendered and returned no text; slot definitions, tag queries and the full claim → use → release lifecycle are described only from API reference snippets.
- No primary Guerrilla or Ubisoft document on their smart-object/affordance systems was retrievable.

## Social/relationship state and how dialogue systems query it (Valve response rules, Crusader Kings opinions, Prom Week/CiF, storylet specificity)

### Takeaway
Two complementary data models dominate: (1) a *fact/criteria query* model (Valve's Response System, re-implemented in Mímir) where dialogue rules match a bag of key/value facts and the most specific matching rule wins, and (2) a *scalar-with-modifier-list* relationship model (Crusader Kings: per-pair opinion = sum of categorized, timed, decaying modifiers), with Prom Week's Comme il Faut showing the richer end (thousands of weighted social considerations ranking social exchanges). Storylet engines (Yarn Spinner saliency, storylet-framework specificity) adopt Valve's "most specific wins" for barks.

### Cited Findings
- Valve Response System parts: Concepts, Criteria, Rules, Response Groups. The game gathers a set of criteria (facts) about the NPC and world, with the concept treated as a high-priority criterion; "The Response system checks each rule against the set, give it a numeric score based on the rule's set of criteria. Then the system picks one rule with the highest score"; a rule's total is the sum of its criteria scores; a failing criterion contributes nothing, a matching one adds its weight; "if at least one criterion is marked as required and isn't satisfied, the rule will never be chosen. Most criteria are set to be required, but criteria that aren't required simply boost the rule's score"; `matchonce` deactivates a rule after first match; Left 4 Dead introduced a rewritten version with different optimizations not fully documented; source is public via the Alien Swarm SDK (snippets; page blocked by Anubis anti-bot) — [Valve Developer Community: Response System](https://developer.valvesoftware.com/wiki/Response_System)
- Emily Short on Elan Ruskin's GDC 2012 talk ("Rule Databases for Contextual Dialog and Game Logic"): dialogue snippets are matched to a continually changing world state in Left 4 Dead 2 and other Valve games; "like Inform, it prioritizes rules and applies the most specific one it can find, using less-specific ones as fall-backs"; design requirements were that writers can author large amounts of content easily, responses fit many situations, and dialogue can be interrupted sensibly (a character under attack breaks off); characters "don't plan or pursue goals through dialogue but give a strong impression of situational awareness"; the post links Ruskin's slides on his own site — [Emily Short, 16 Mar 2012](https://emshort.blog/2012/03/16/gdc-2012-talk-on-dynamic-dialogue/)
- Mímir (Rust, "heavily inspired ... by Elan Ruskin's amazing session from GDC 2012"): `Query<FactKey, FactType>` is a map of facts; `Rule` is a map from fact key to an evaluator (e.g. `FloatEvaluator::EqualTo(5.)`, `gte(2.)`) plus an outcome; a `Ruleset` evaluates all rules and returns the one with the most requirements satisfied (most specific); ties are broken at random. Example: rule {enemies_killed == 5} vs rule {enemies_killed == 5, doors_opened >= 2}; a query with both facts returns the second — [lukecarr/mimir README](https://github.com/lukecarr/mimir)
- Ian Thomas's storylet-framework offers `useSpecificity=true`: "the more clauses in the condition, the more relevant the storylet probably is", used "for example, bark implementations"; priorities still take precedence, specificity sorts within a priority — [storylet-framework README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wildwinter/storylet-framework/main/README.md)
- Yarn Spinner 3 saliency: the default "best" strategy "selects the available node with the most specific conditions"; strategies: Best, Best Least Recently Viewed, Random Best Least Recently Viewed (recommended default), Random; all filter out failing conditions first; custom strategies allowed — [Yarn Spinner storylets and saliency primer](https://docs.yarnspinner.dev/write-yarn-scripts/advanced-scripting/storylets-and-saliency-a-primer), [Yarn Spinner 3 beta blog (snippet)](https://www.yarnspinner.dev/blog/yarn-spinner-3-beta-1)
- Crusader Kings III opinion model: every character holds an opinion of every other character; categories: General, Attraction (adults only, age caps), Vassal, Child (<16), Courtier, Same Dynasty, Tyranny, Faith Hostility. Composition is a list of named modifiers with values and optional durations, e.g. Diplomacy +1 per point, "Attacked An Ally −25 (3 years)", "Broke Truce −50 (3 years)", Friend +60, Best Friend +120, Rival −60, Nemesis −120, Faith hostility Astray −10/Hostile −20/Evil −30 (Pluralist halves, Fundamentalist doubles). "Most opinions decay toward 0. Some grow over time, and some stay constant." Tyranny decays monthly (−0.25 default, −0.15 Soon Forgiven, −0.05 Venial) and is capped at 1000 — [CK3 wiki: Opinion](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Opinion)
- CK3 modifier stacking quirk reported by a modder: stacking the same opinion modifier "is intended" but "the duration of the entire stack is reset to another 5 years (which is not intended)", and there is no per-entry timer (snippet) — [Paradox forum post](https://admin-forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/modding-quick-questions.589686/post-25700986). CK2 mod example of decaying modifiers with value+duration: "Child: −100 (100 years) with decay", dynasty −40 (30 years), courtier −1 (2 years) (snippet) — [Steam Workshop changelog](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/1866148157?l=danish)
- Prom Week / Comme il Faut: "over 5,000 social considerations" (facts that shape how characters feel, e.g. shyness lowers outgoing acts, past meanness lowers niceness), combined rather than any single one deciding; "social exchange" = an action one character takes toward another with the intent of changing their relationship plus the responder's response; over 40 exchange types; each character keeps a ranked list of desired exchanges (volition); the responder accepts or rejects; third-party relationships affect outcomes (Simon disbelieves Cassandra's rumor because he is closer friends with Naomi); 20+ templated dialogue scenes per exchange type render the result — [Prom Week dev blog](https://promweek.soe.ucsc.edu/?p=295)
- ink's own state model is deliberately small: "ink does not provide a full world-modelling system ... there are no 'objects', no concepts of 'containment'"; instead `LIST` variables act as enums/flags/sets (`LIST kettleState = cold, boiling, recently_boiled`), reusable across variables, with set operations (containment `?`, `^` intersection, inversion, "full" list), used for knowledge tracking and simple state machines — [Writing with ink, Part 5](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/inkle/ink/master/Documentation/WritingWithInk.md)

### Inferences
- A sound combined model for a living world: relationship state as per-pair categorized modifier lists with (value, startTick, duration, decay) in the CK style; a per-NPC memory of event facts with confidence and expiry (Orkin's WorkingMemoryFact); and dialogue/bark selection as a Valve-style query where the facts bag is assembled from (concept, speaker, listener, relationship bucket, location, time, recent events, memory) and rules are scored by matched criteria with required criteria as hard filters. Each piece is plain data and readily generated by a coding agent.
- Converting scalar affinity into bucketed facts (e.g. `opinion_bucket = hostile|cool|warm|friend`) is what makes rule specificity meaningful; raw scalars in rules lead to brittle threshold soup.

### Gaps
- Ruskin's slides (criteria syntax, "memory" writes from responses, L4D scale numbers like rule and line counts) were not retrievable; the Valve wiki page could not be read directly.
- The CK3 script schema for `opinion_modifiers` (fields for decay/months/stacking) was not found on an accessible page; only observed behavior is cited.
- Prom Week's internal representation (networks for buddy/romance/cool, statuses, social facts database) is known from the CiF papers, which were not fetched; only the dev-blog summary is cited.

## Pathfinding/movement for tile/town worlds, off-screen NPC simulation (LOD/bubble), time systems and determinism

### Takeaway
For tile worlds, grid A* (Manhattan heuristic on 4-way grids) is the default and Dijkstra maps/flow fields are the right tool when many agents share goals; shipped open worlds simulate off-screen NPCs with tiered LOD (Ubisoft: virtual/bulk/real with 5–15 s / 1 s / per-frame updates; AC Unity: 10,000 crowd NPCs with 40 full AIs; KCD2: ~2,400 NPCs with AI simulation LOD hidden from the scripting interface); determinism requires a fixed timestep and seeded RNG, verified in practice by per-tick checksums and replay (Factorio).

### Cited Findings
- Red Blob "Introduction to A*" (2014, last modified 29 Sep 2026): algorithms see only a graph of nodes/edges, so grids, rooms/doorways, etc. are interchangeable; BFS (equal costs), Dijkstra (movement costs, priority queue), Greedy Best-First (fast, suboptimal), A* (both; optimal if heuristic never overestimates); Manhattan heuristic on 4-way grids; early exit when the goal is *removed* from the queue; "Reducing graph size is the best performance step"; BFS/Dijkstra for paths to many locations, A* for one goal — [Red Blob Games: A* introduction](https://www.redblobgames.com/pathfinding/a-star/introduction.html)
- Red Blob tower-defense pathfinding (2014, updated to 2020): "all sources, one destination" is solved by one breadth-first/Dijkstra pass from the goal producing a distance field ("Dijkstra Map") and a flow field (came_from pointers / negative gradient); cost depends on map size rather than unit count, stays valid when units are pushed or spawned, but no early exit and wasteful for a single path; varying costs turns it into Dijkstra — [Red Blob Games: Tower defense pathfinding](https://www.redblobgames.com/pathfinding/tower-defense/)
- Assassin's Creed Origins NPC LOD: Virtual (far, not rendered, "needs still update goals every 5 to 15 seconds"), Bulk (closer, physically present but not fully rendered, updated once per second), Real (within 80 yards, fully rendered, every frame); unlike earlier games, NPCs "exist and simulate even outside the player's immediate proximity" — [GameRevolution, 5 May 2018](https://www.gamerevolution.com/news/386683-ubisoft-talks-about-building-assassins-creed-origins-world-through-ai)
- Assassin's Creed Unity (GDC 2015, "Massive Crowd on Assassin's Creed Unity: AI Recycling"): pooling swapped low-detail NPCs for high-detail ones unnoticed; about 10,000 crowd NPCs on screen with only 40 full AIs and 120 high-resolution models (listing snippet) — [GDC Vault listing](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022141/Massive-Crowd-on-Assassin-s). Hitman: Absolution reached 1,200-character crowds at 30 fps on 2012 consoles (GDC 2012 listing snippet) — [GDC Vault: Crowds in Hitman](https://gdcvault.com/play/1016443/Crowds-in-Hitman)
- Kingdom Come: Deliverance II: ~2,400 NPCs, half in one city, AI simulation LOD with the scripting interface "as oblivious to the LODs as possible", 60 FPS (GDC 2026 abstract snippet) — [GDC schedule](https://schedule.gdconf.com/session/supporting-thousands-of-npcs-in-kingdom-come-deliverance-kingdom-come-deliverance-ii/915120)
- Academic framing: "simulation LOD reduces the quality of the simulation at unseen places" (Brom, Šerý, Poch 2007) and "few places in the artificial world are important at a given time" with three open problems: finding important places, simplifying elsewhere, and blending between the two (IVE 2006) (snippets) — [Springer chapter](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/simulation-level-of-detail-for-virtual-humans/2882154), [IVE LOD 2006 PDF](https://artemis.ms.mff.cuni.cz/main/papers/IVE-LOD-2006.pdf)
- Glenn Fiedler, "Fix Your Timestep!" (10 June 2004): variable dt makes results depend on framerate (springs blow up, tunneling); use a fixed dt consumed from an accumulator, interpolate rendering with alpha = accumulator/dt, cap steps to avoid the spiral of death; with fixed dt "the same inputs produce the same simulation results regardless of render framerate", the basis of deterministic lockstep — [Gaffer On Games](https://gafferongames.com/post/fix_your_timestep/)
- Factorio: multiplayer is deterministic lockstep, only inputs are sent, every client simulates each tick identically and any divergence is a desync (FFF #188); desync hunting computes a CRC of the whole map every tick, saves tagged human-readable snapshots, and a replay checks the same checksums so the first divergent tick pinpoints the bug, usually "one variable" (FFF #47); a cheaper per-tick heuristic checksum (player state, entity counts) runs during play (FFF #55) (snippets) — [FFF #47](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-47), [FFF #55](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-55), [FFF #188](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-188)
- A roguelike developer's note: "if the simulation pulled even one value from somewhere it was not handed, a system clock, a random source the engine seeded for me" reproducibility breaks, and headless tests depend on that purity (snippet) — [solodevstack blog](https://solodevstack.com/blog/deterministic-simulation-architecture-roguelike)
- Stardew's schedule times are coarse (e.g. 900, 1030, 2200) and "An entry never starts before the previous entry's time" — [Stardew Valley Wiki](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Modding:Schedule_data)

### Inferences
- For a tile town with dozens to hundreds of NPCs, per-NPC A* on a small grid is cheap; Dijkstra maps per popular destination (tavern, well, workplaces) give O(1) steering for everyone heading there and are easy to cache per time-of-day.
- The Ubisoft three-tier model maps cleanly onto a discrete-tick simulation: "real" NPCs tick every tick, "bulk" every N ticks with pathing only, "virtual" update only their schedule/needs state and teleport along schedule waypoints. Blending (the IVE "third open problem") is the main engineering risk.
- Discrete ticks plus seeded RNG plus Factorio-style per-tick state hashing is the practical determinism toolkit and is directly testable in CI.

### Gaps
- No source quantified acceptable update intervals for off-screen *social* simulation (relationships, rumors); the Ubisoft numbers concern needs/goal updates.
- Crowd avoidance (RVO/ORCA) and navmesh-vs-grid tradeoffs in shipped town games were not researched in depth (Amit Patel's map-representations page returned 503).

## Authoring tools and formats: Ink/inkjs, Yarn Spinner, Twine/Twee, articy:draft, Dialogic, storylet libraries; text-first/git-friendliness; TypeScript runtimes; agent-authored pipelines

### Takeaway
Ink is the only major dialogue language with a mature, officially maintained JavaScript/TypeScript runtime *and* in-JS compiler (inkjs), which makes it the natural target for agent-authored content that must be validated programmatically; Yarn Spinner 3 adds storylet/saliency features but has no official JS runtime (community yarn-bound/bondage.js only); Twee 3 and Dialogic 2 timelines are plain text; articy:draft is a GUI/database product with JSON/XML exports and Perforce/SVN-backed multi-user partitions; JSON storylet decks (storylet-framework, SimpleQBN) are the most agent-friendly format for bark/encounter content.

### Cited Findings
**Ink (inkle)**

**Storylet libraries and the storylet model**
- Emily Short's definition (29 Nov 2019): a storylet is "a piece of content" plus "prerequisites" (when it can play) plus "effects" on world state; the definition "doesn't say how much content is inside a given storylet ... or how it's selected"; storylets can reproduce gauntlet, branch-and-bottleneck, sorting-hat, loop-and-grow and (with difficulty) time-cave structures; tools mentioned: Tiny QBN, StoryNexus, Varytale, Versu, Character Engine, Threaded Conversation; Max Kreminski's survey covers StoryAssembler, StoryNexus, King of Chicago, Reigns, Ice-Bound — [Storylets: You Want Them](https://emshort.blog/2019/11/29/storylets-you-want-them/), [Kreminski on storylets](https://emshort.blog/2019/01/06/kreminski-on-storylets/)
- storylet-framework (Ian Thomas; JS ESM + minified browser build, Python, C#, C++; MIT): storylets in JSON(C) with `id`, `condition` (expression over a context object, via his expression-parser: `and/or/not`, comparisons, function calls), `priority` (number or expression; higher = nearer top; equal priority shuffled), `redraw` ("always" | "never" | N draws), `content` (opaque app data), `outcomes` (named sets of context updates, `"default"` applied on `play()`, e.g. `"someCounter":"someCounter+1"`); packets with `context` declarations and `defaults` nest arbitrarily; `useSpecificity`; `draw()` accepts a filter; the author now points to Storylet Studio (storylets.dev) and is "unlikely to expand or maintain this library" — [storylet-framework README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wildwinter/storylet-framework/main/README.md)
- SimpleQBN: "a generic JavaScript library for creating quality-based narrative works" for Node and web, with a `State` class (`add/update/has/remove`) (snippet) — [videlais: Working with SimpleQBN](https://videlais.com/tag/qbn/). `@scio/storyletry`: TypeScript implementation of Emily Short's model, v0.1.0, Unlicense (snippet) — [npm @scio/storyletry](https://www.npmjs.org/package/@scio/storyletry)
- Mímir (Rust) is a "contextual query engine ... for video games with dynamic events (e.g. dialogue, animations) driven by their current world's state" — [lukecarr/mimir](https://github.com/lukecarr/mimir)

### Inferences
- Git-friendliness ranking (text-first, diffable, no GUI required): ink (.ink) ≈ Yarn (.yarn) ≈ Twee (.twee) ≈ JSON storylets > Dialogic 2 text timelines (.dtl is text but Godot-resource-flavored) >> articy (database project; the *exports* are JSON/XML, the source of truth is the tool plus Perforce/SVN partitions).
- TypeScript runtime availability: ink via inkjs (official, typed, with compiler) is the only first-class option; Yarn needs community yarn-bound (Yarn 2 spec, not 3, so no node groups/saliency/once/detour); Twee compiles to HTML for a story format (SugarCube/Harlowe) and is awkward to embed as a dialogue engine in a non-Twine game; storylet-framework/SimpleQBN are JS-native.
- For an AI coding agent writing content: ink's weave syntax is compact but has subtle gotchas (glue, lookahead with EXTERNAL functions, visit-count semantics), so a compile-and-play harness is essential; JSON storylet decks with explicit `condition`/`outcomes` are the easiest for an agent to generate, validate with a schema, and unit test; Yarn 3 node groups + saliency are the closest thing to a built-in bark/storylet engine but lack a JS runtime.
- A reasonable hybrid for a TS living-world game: ink for authored scenes (state, choices, tunnels/threads), a JSON storylet deck with Valve-style specificity for barks and ambient reactions, and the game's own TS simulation as the fact source for both (ink EXTERNAL functions and observers; storylet `context`).

## Testing simulation-heavy games: deterministic seeds, headless runs, property/snapshot tests, how studios test NPC schedules

### Takeaway
Practitioner evidence centers on determinism-first engineering (fixed timestep, single seeded RNG, no wall-clock), input recording/replay (Retro City Rampage), per-tick state checksums with tagged snapshots to localize divergence (Factorio), and planner-driven automated playthroughs (Rebellion, GDC 2021); no public account of how a studio specifically regression-tests NPC daily schedules was found.

### Cited Findings
- Rebellion (GDC 2021 AI Summit, Bram Ridder): an automated testing system "that plays entire games start to finish", combining a numeric domain-independent AI planner with behavior trees, demonstrated on Zombie Army 4, Sniper Elite and Evil Genius 2 (listing snippet) — [GDC Vault listing](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027537/AI-Summit-Automated-Game-Testing)
- Vblank (GDC 2015, Brian Provinciano), "Automated Testing and Instant Replays in Retro City Rampage": building a deterministic engine with input recording and playback (snippet) — [GDC news](https://gdconf.com/news/learn_how_to_build_a_game_that)
- Factorio's per-tick CRC of the whole map, tagged human-readable snapshots and replay comparison find the first divergent tick; divergence is usually one variable (snippets) — [FFF #47](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-47), [FFF #55](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-55)
- Fixed timestep makes "the same inputs produce the same simulation results regardless of render framerate" — [Fix Your Timestep](https://gafferongames.com/post/fix_your_timestep/)
- Deterministic simulation testing in general: intercept all nondeterminism (clock, scheduling, network, disk) and derive everything from one seed so a run replays identically given the seed (snippets) — [DZone: deterministic simulation testing](https://dzone.com/articles/deterministic-simulation-testing), [detsim (Go, virtual-time kernel)](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/arshnah/detsim)
- A 2026 journal paper proposes a scenario-driven black-box framework for checking whether NPC behavior is "stable and deterministic" across interaction sequences; of six VR test scenarios four met all criteria and two were partial; it notes existing game regression frameworks "focus on functional regressions rather than behavioral stability across sequences of interaction" (snippet) — [INTENSIF journal article](https://ojs.unpkediri.ac.id/index.php/intensif/article/view/28379)
- Determinism pitfall from a replay-system forum thread: random NPC target selection breaks replays unless the randomness is seeded and recorded (snippet) — [gamedev.tv forum](https://community.gamedev.tv/t/random-behavior-in-deterministic-replay-systems/43121)
- Merrill's evaluation/execution split allows evaluating a whole BT "independently of an agent's behavior execution" and validating "an entire plan ... to the end" before committing, which is a form of look-ahead check — [Game AI Pro ch.10](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter10_Building_Utility_Decisions_into_Your_Existing_Behavior_Tree.pdf)
- HTN's MTR makes plan-priority decisions explicit and comparable, and partial plans keep planner work bounded — [Game AI Pro ch.12](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter12_Exploring_HTN_Planners_through_Example.pdf)
- Dialogic runs gdUnit4 unit tests on every push/PR (tool-level testing practice) — [Dialogic README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dialogic-godot/dialogic/main/README.md)

### Inferences
- A workable test strategy for a living world in TS: (1) fixed tick + single seeded PRNG threaded through every system (Fiedler, Factorio, solodevstack); (2) headless "day runs" that hash world state per tick and snapshot it at fixed hours, diffing against golden snapshots (Factorio FFF #47 pattern); (3) property tests on schedules (every NPC reaches its `a`-time destination, no NPC is in two places, sleep windows respected) derived from the Stardew-style schedule table; (4) scenario tests that inject stimuli (theft, fire) and assert the latent behavior preempts and the ambient behavior resumes (ScriptEase model); (5) utility oscillation detectors that flag action flip-flops within N ticks (Lewis/Graham).
- None of these are described as studio practice for schedules specifically; they are compositions of documented techniques.

### Gaps
- No primary source describes how Bethesda, Warhorse, Ubisoft or Maxis regression-test NPC schedules or social simulation; the GDC 2026 KCD2 talk abstract mentions "plan limits early" but not testing.
- Property-based testing of game simulations has no located practitioner write-up; the evidence here is generic deterministic-simulation-testing material from the database world.

## How the architectures are combined in practice (FSMs, blackboards, hybrids)

### Takeaway
Shipped systems are hybrids: a planner or utility layer picks goals, BTs/HTN methods/packages expand them into steps, FSMs survive inside animation/locomotion subsystems, and a blackboard/working memory with sensors is the shared substrate; practitioners pick by authoring cost and designer legibility rather than by expressive power.

### Cited Findings
- F.E.A.R.: planner + blackboard + subsystems (navigation, animation) each updating "at some constant rate" and reading instructions from the blackboard; sensors feed working memory; this replaced C4 action tuples and earlier FSM/scripted systems — [Orkin 2005](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/orkin2005agent.pdf)
- Horizon Zero Dawn: HTN planning combined with utility-based decision making plus group coordination systems — [Guerrilla](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/the-ai-of-horizon-zero-dawn)
- Guild Wars 2: Heart of Thorns: pure utility (IAUS) with ~100 reusable consideration patterns and an influence map as a consideration input — [Game AI Pro 3 ch.13](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter13_Choosing_Effective_Utility-Based_Considerations.pdf)
- Kingdom Come: Deliverance II: GOAP combined with modular behavior trees (listing) — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1035576/Game-AI-Summit-Combining-GOAP)
- Merrill's team: BTs as primary decision mechanism with utility selectors, after abandoning a STRIPS planner and an FSM — [Game AI Pro ch.10](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter10_Building_Utility_Decisions_into_Your_Existing_Behavior_Tree.pdf)
- The Sims lineage: needs-based utility over object advertisements; The Sims Medieval decides only when the interaction queue is empty — [Game AI Pro ch.9](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter09_An_Introduction_to_Utility_Theory.pdf), [GMTK summary](https://gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/gmtk-the-genius-ai-behind-the-sims)
- Bethesda: ordered package list with conditions and schedules, sandbox fallback, procedure trees, idle markers — [Beyond Skyrim wiki](https://wiki.beyondskyrim.org/wiki/Arcane_University:NPC_Packages), [CK wiki tutorial (snippet)](https://ck.uesp.net/wiki/Bethesda_Tutorial_Packages)
- Ubisoft (Origins): schedules + needs at three simulation LODs under a meta-AI of tracking/spawning/behavior systems — [GameRevolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/news/386683-ubisoft-talks-about-building-assassins-creed-origins-world-through-ai)

### Inferences
- Authoring cost, roughly from practitioner testimony: FSM (cheap to start, messy to grow), BT (cheap, legible, sprawls), utility (cheap per behavior once the tool exists, tuning cost in curves), HTN (moderate; domain is readable text; needs MTR/partial-plan machinery), GOAP (highest engineering cost, lowest designer legibility, best emergent problem-solving). For a text/data-first world authored largely by a coding agent, utility + HTN-style compound tasks + Bethesda/Stardew-style schedules + JSON storylets keep everything in diffable text with small runtimes.

### Gaps
- No single source compares authoring cost quantitatively across architectures; the ranking above is inferred.
