# LLM-driven NPCs and living worlds in games (state of the art as of October 2026)

Scope: runtime use of LLMs as NPC brains or dialogue, authoring-time use of LLMs/coding agents to write NPC scripts, and the cost/latency/consistency lessons from research systems, open-source projects, middleware, and shipped games. Researched 2026-10-22. Where a source is vendor-published or self-reported, that is flagged.

## 1. Generative Agents / "Smallville" (Park et al., 2023) and its follow-ups

### Takeaway
The Smallville architecture (memory stream + retrieval scored by recency/importance/relevance, periodic reflection, hierarchical day plans) produced the most believable agents in its own evaluation, but it ran 25 agents for two game days at a cost of "thousands of dollars" on gpt-3.5-turbo, ran sequentially at roughly real time, and its documented failures (retrieval misses, embellishment, world-knowledge leakage, over-politeness from instruction tuning) are exactly the ones later shipped games still fight. Follow-up work cut cost 10-100x mostly by replacing per-step LLM calls with cheaper policies and summaries.

### Cited Findings
- 25 agents in a Sims-like sandbox; agents store experiences in natural language, synthesize reflections, and retrieve them to plan; ablations show observation, planning and reflection "each contribute critically to the believability of agent behavior." A single seeded intention (a Valentine's party) propagated as invitations over two days. — [arXiv 2304.03442 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442)
- Implementation uses gpt-3.5-turbo. Retrieval score = min-max-normalized recency + importance + relevance, all weights 1. Recency is exponential decay with factor 0.995 per game hour since last retrieval. Importance is an LLM-rated 1-10 score at memory creation (e.g., 2 for cleaning the room, 8 for asking a crush out). Relevance is cosine similarity of embeddings. — [arXiv 2304.03442 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)
- Reflection triggers when summed importance of recent events exceeds 150, which happened "roughly two or three times a day"; the model reviews the 100 most recent memories, generates 3 salient questions, retrieves evidence, and extracts 5 insights with citations. — [arXiv 2304.03442 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)
- Planning hierarchy: a 5-8 chunk day outline, decomposed into hour-long actions, then 5-15 minute actions. At each step the model decides whether an observation warrants a reaction; if so, the plan is regenerated from that point; if the reaction involves another agent, dialogue is generated until one agent ends it. — [arXiv 2304.03442 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)
- Believability (TrueSkill mu, 100 human evaluators): full architecture 29.89; no reflection 26.88; no reflection or planning 25.64; human crowdworker roleplay 22.95; no memory/planning/reflection 21.21. Full architecture vs prior-work condition d = 8.16. — [arXiv 2304.03442 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)
- Two-game-day end-to-end run: knowledge of Sam's candidacy spread from 4% to 32% of agents; knowledge of Isabella's party from 4% to 52%; relationship network density 0.167 to 0.74; 5 of 12 invited agents attended the party; 1.3% (6 of 453) awareness responses were hallucinated. — [arXiv 2304.03442 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)
- Documented failure modes: retrieval failure (Rajiv "hadn't followed the election closely" despite hearing about it); incomplete recall (Tom unsure the party existed); embellishment (Isabella invented an announcement Sam never planned); world-knowledge leakage (Yuriko describing "Adam Smith" as the 18th-century economist); overly formal dialogue (Mei's greetings to her husband); instruction tuning made agents "overly cooperative," Isabella "rarely said no" to ideas outside her interests; spatial-norm errors (agents not realizing places close after hours, choosing a bar for lunch). — [arXiv 2304.03442 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)
- Cost and runtime: the study was "costing thousands of dollars in token credits and taking multiple days to complete" for 25 agents over two game days; "Our current architecture runs sequentially in roughly real-time game time"; proposed mitigations are parallelizing agents, batching dialogue as one joint prompt, and caching periodic summaries. Building the architecture took "roughly a year." Agents "may be vulnerable to prompt hacking, memory hacking" and hallucination. — [arXiv 2304.03442 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)
- Independent confirmation of the cost: Affordable Generative Agents (Feb 2024) cites the Stanford Town study as "incurring costs of thousands of dollars"; its fix splits behavior into a "Lifestyle Policy" for agent-environment interactions (reusing routine plans instead of regenerating them) and a "Social Memory" for inter-agent interactions, claiming the same believability at significantly reduced consumption. — [arXiv 2402.02053](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02053v2)
- Lyfe Agents (Oct 2023) reports "computational cost 10-100 times lower than existing alternatives" via an option-action framework (high-level options chosen rarely, cheap actions within them), asynchronous self-monitoring, and Summarize-and-Forget memory; demonstrated in a 3D environment where agents collaboratively solve a murder mystery. — [arXiv 2310.02172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02172v1)
- Community fork of the official repo adds cost tracking and a spending cap; sample config caps a test run at $10 using gpt-3.5-turbo-0125 pricing. — [drudilorenzo/generative_agents](https://github.com/drudilorenzo/generative_agents)
- The 2024 follow-up "Generative Agent Simulations of 1,000 People" (arXiv 2411.10109, submitted 2024-11-15; Park, Bernstein et al.) builds agents from two-hour qualitative interviews with 1,052 real people; agents reproduce participants' General Social Survey answers at 85% of the accuracy with which people reproduce their own answers two weeks later, predict personality traits and experimental outcomes comparably to humans, and interview-based agents reduce accuracy gaps across racial and ideological groups versus demographic-only agents. — [arXiv 2411.10109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109v1)

### Inferences
- The 1,000-people paper is about social-science fidelity, not games; its relevance to a Stardew-like is the authoring lesson that a rich interview-style character document beats a demographic sketch for consistent behavior.
- The failure modes that still matter most for a scripted-world game are "overly cooperative" and "embellishment." A town NPC that agrees with everything and invents events is corrosive to a world whose charm is stable, authored relationships.
- The cheap follow-ups (Lifestyle Policy, option-action) are, in effect, re-inventing scripted schedules. That is a point in favor of scripts as the default and the LLM as the exception.

### Gaps
- Neither the original paper nor the follow-ups publish per-agent-per-game-hour token counts, so a precise 2026 cost rerun cannot be derived from them; see Section 6 for a worked estimate from current prices.
- No quantitative believability comparison exists between Affordable Generative Agents / Lyfe Agents and the original beyond the authors' own claims.

## 2. AI Town (a16z-infra, Convex) and other open-source LLM towns

### Takeaway
AI Town is a TypeScript port of the Smallville idea whose main engineering contribution is the separation of a deterministic, single-threaded game engine (60 ticks/s batched into 1 step/s inside Convex) from asynchronous "agent operations" that make LLM calls and feed results back as queued inputs. Its memory model is simpler than Smallville's (conversation summaries embedded, top-3 retrieved). Nobody has published running costs; the project defaults to local Ollama (llama3) and has not had a named successor.

### Cited Findings
- Stack: "Game engine, database, and vector search: Convex." Default LLM is local Ollama (`llama3` chat, `mxbai-embed-large` embeddings); Together.ai, OpenAI, and any OpenAI-compatible endpoint (Anthropic, Groq, Azure named) are supported. Changing the embedding model requires wiping data. `NUM_MEMORIES_TO_SEARCH` in `constants.ts` can be set to 1 if prompts slow things down. Simulation pauses after 5 minutes idle. 10.7k stars, 1.2k forks, 1,147 commits, 68 open issues at time of fetch. — [a16z-infra/ai-town README](https://github.com/a16z-infra/ai-town)
- Engine loop: `tick(now)` runs at 60 ticks/s for smooth movement, but ticks are batched into a step that runs "only 1 time per second"; a step loads world state, applies queued inputs, runs ticks, writes a diff. Inputs go into an `inputs` table with a monotonic number; the engine is single-threaded per world; a generation number cancels stale scheduled runs. Input latency is about one round trip plus half a step, "roughly 1.5s by default"; a 250 ms step lowers latency but raises function calls and DB bandwidth. Active state must stay "less than a few dozen kilobytes." — [ARCHITECTURE.md](https://github.com/a16z-infra/ai-town/blob/main/ARCHITECTURE.md)
- LLM calls are kept out of the engine: `Agent.tick` makes game-state decisions; when an LLM is needed the agent calls `startOperation` with an `internalAction`, which reads via `internalQuery`, never writes game state directly, and submits results as inputs; an `inProgressOperation` record enforces one operation per agent at a time. — [ARCHITECTURE.md](https://github.com/a16z-infra/ai-town/blob/main/ARCHITECTURE.md)
- Conversations have exactly two members with states `invited`, `walkingOver`, `participating`; after each conversation "GPT summarizes its message history," the summary is embedded into Convex vector search, and on a new conversation the agent retrieves "the three most similar memories." Embeddings are cached by text hash. No reflection or importance scoring is described. — [ARCHITECTURE.md](https://github.com/a16z-infra/ai-town/blob/main/ARCHITECTURE.md)
- Convex staff said AI Town hosting "should fit within a free Starter plan or at least a basic Pro plan," and the hosted demo had not exceeded the Pro plan's function-call allotment; no LLM spend figure given. — [Convex Discord thread](https://discord-questions.convex.dev/m/1140994230022582312)
- Post-launch: over 5k stars, walkable characters, a map editor, and community clones "AI Silicon Valley, Cat Town, or ... Zaranova." — [Convex: AI Town v2](https://stack.convex.dev/ai-town-v2)
- Origins: Yoko Li (a16z), Martin Casado (a16z), James Cowling (Convex CTO) discuss the project as growing from a companion-app prototype, built on the Generative Agents approach. — [a16z podcast listing](https://www.globalplayer.com/podcasts/episodes/7DrmcjJ)
- Other open-source towns: Agent Town (zoan37) is a browser demo inspired by Generative Agents using window.ai for bring-your-own-model; OSGA (ElliottDyson) is a community fork of Generative Agents targeting open-source LLMs; AgentArena (JustInternetAI) is "a Godot-native framework for LLM-driven NPCs with tools, memory, and goal-oriented behavior in small simulation scenes," focused on local models; a Godot LLM Framework addon (MIT) targets dialogue and tool use with Claude support first. — [zoan37/agent-town](https://github.com/zoan37/agent-town); [ElliottDyson/OSGA](https://github.com/ElliottDyson/OSGA); [JustInternetAI/AgentArena](https://github.com/JustInternetAI/AgentArena); [Godot LLM Framework asset](https://godotengine.org/asset-library/asset/edit/13585)

### Inferences
- The AI Town pattern (deterministic engine owns state; LLM runs as an asynchronous operation that can only submit inputs through the same queue a player uses) is the right skeleton for a Stardew-like where scripts own most behavior. It makes the LLM a slow, optional client of the simulation rather than its heart.
- AI Town's 1 step/s with a 1.5 s input latency is fine for conversation but not for action feedback; a Stardew-like should keep movement and interactions in the engine and give the LLM only conversational and planning hooks.

### Gaps
- No published measurements of AI Town's LLM spend per agent-hour on any provider.
- No evidence of a maintained Unity or Phaser port of the full Smallville loop; the Godot projects are frameworks, not Smallville replicas.
- No activity-date information for ai-town in 2025-2026 beyond third-party star counts; treat it as a reference implementation rather than an active platform.

## 3. Project Sid (Altera, 2024) and large-scale multi-agent experiments

### Takeaway
Project Sid's PIANO architecture runs ~10 concurrent modules per agent (some LLM, some small non-LLM nets) around a shared agent state with a single "Cognitive Controller" bottleneck so that speech and action stay coherent. Its headline civilizational results (roles, taxes, religion spread) come from 30-500 agent runs; 1,000+ agent runs exceeded the Minecraft server. The failures it names (hallucinated states poisoning later prompts, "saying but not doing," repetitive loops, unresponsiveness at scale) are the drift problems any living-world designer needs to budget for.

### Cited Findings
- Abstract: PIANO ("Parallel Information Aggregation via Neural Orchestration") lets agents interact in real time "while maintaining coherence across multiple output streams"; demonstrated with "10 - 1000+ AI agents" in Minecraft; agents "autonomously develop specialized roles," adhere to and change collective rules, and engage in "cultural and religious transmission"; results called "preliminary." Submitted 2024-10-31, 14 authors. — [arXiv 2411.00114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00114)
- Architecture: "10 distinct modules running concurrently" (memory, action awareness, goal generation, social awareness, talking, skill execution, cognition, planning, motor execution, speech), stateless functions reading/writing a shared Agent State at different speeds; reflex modules use "small, fast non-LLM neural networks." The Cognitive Controller makes high-level decisions broadcast to other modules; the bottleneck exists because "Actions from multiple output streams must therefore be bidirectionally influential." — [arXiv 2411.00114 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00114v1)
- Results: single agents acquired "an average of 17 unique items after 30 minutes," top performers 30-40; a 49-agent, 4-hour run saturated at ~320 (about a third of all) Minecraft items. 30 agents, 20 minutes: roles like farmers, miners, guards, blacksmiths emerged; without the social module roles did not persist. 29 agents with a constitution: deposits fell "from 20% to 9%" after the tax rate was amended to 5-10%; frozen-constitution runs showed constant rates. 500 agents, 9,000 s: Pastafarianism spread from 20 priests and "steadily increased" without saturating after two hours; rural areas produced fewer memes than towns. — [arXiv 2411.00114 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00114v1)
- Scale limit: 1000+ agent runs "exceeded the computational constraints of our Minecraft server," so detailed analysis uses a single 500-agent simulation. — [arXiv 2411.00114 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00114v1)
- Failures: an agent claiming to eat "a bagel" while not eating, and because false output feeds future prompts, small hallucination rates can "poison downstream agent behavior"; Abby's chat says "Sure thing!" while her function call chooses "explore," so Bob "mines with an imaginary pickaxe"; agents "become stuck in repetitive patterns of actions," groups "loop"; agents became "sporadically unresponsive" at high counts; no vision/spatial reasoning, weak innate drives, no de novo innovation. — [arXiv 2411.00114 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00114v1)
- Models: GPT-4o was the "latest base LM" that enabled item progression (older models could not); an "old snapshot" of Claude 3.5 Sonnet appears in the model comparison; GPT-4o used for role inference. No cost figures in the paper. — [arXiv 2411.00114 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00114v1)
- Press coverage (MIT Technology Review, Nov 2024) is the most detailed independent write-up; Altera raised over $11M from a16z and Eric Schmidt's fund; one critique questions how autonomous the agents really are. — [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2024/11/27/1107377/a-minecraft-town-of-ai-characters-made-friends-invented-jobs-and-spread-religion/)

### Inferences
- The PIANO "one controller decides; many modules execute" shape is the same lesson as Krafton's PUBG Ally two-layer design (Section 4): a slow LLM deliberator must not be the thing that owns per-tick state.
- For a town of ~30 NPCs, Sid's 30-agent specialization result suggests emergent roles are achievable, but the same runs show roles evaporate without an explicit social-memory module. Authored relationships are cheaper and more stable than emergent ones.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed replication of Project Sid, no cost disclosure, and no 2025-2026 status update from Altera was found; the company's post-Sid direction is unconfirmed.

## 4. Commercial middleware and shipped games

### Takeaway
By October 2026 the middleware market has split: Inworld has repositioned to voice/TTS and an "Agent Runtime" for enterprise (retiring its Character Studio); Convai sells a per-interaction hosted NPC stack with no named shipped titles; NVIDIA ACE is the only route that has shipped on-device NPC brains in commercial games (inZOI Smart Zoi, NARAKA AI teammate, PUBG Ally beta). Shipped LLM games are mostly single-mechanic conversation games with Mixed Steam reviews (Suck Up!, Vaudeville) or side features bolted onto a large authored game (Where Winds Meet). Every successful case constrains the LLM to text plus a small set of flags.

### Cited Findings
Middleware:
- Inworld: retired Character Studio; developers use the Inworld Portal and Agent Runtime SDKs (Unreal shipped Oct 2025, Unity early access); acquired Ultravox 2026-09-30; launched "Inference" 2026-10-14. Pricing: On-Demand pay-as-you-go, Creator $25/mo to Growth $1,500/mo, Enterprise custom; Realtime TTS-2 $25 per million characters On-Demand, $12.50 Growth, Enterprise quoted inconsistently at $5 or $10. Vendor-reported TTS-2 first audio 100 ms P90 before network. Named game: Latitude's Voyage uses Inworld for ~97% of its TTS volume. Critique: games are one market among several; studio-era projects must be rebuilt; cloud costs grow with player count. — [tech-insider comparison, Oct 2026](https://tech-insider.org/inworld-ai-vs-convai-vs-nvidia-ace-2026/)
- Inworld STT about $0.15/hour; LLM inference billed separately; 2026 guides describe Inworld as "repositioned as B2B AI infrastructure." — [Cinevva guide, July 2026](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/ai-npcs-dialogue)
- Inworld announced price cuts across TTS, STT and inference in June 2026 (press release; details not retrieved, page returned 403). — [Business Wire via Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/business-wire/20260610968386/inworld-cuts-prices-to-take-down-the-biggest-wall-in-consumer-ai-cost)

- NVIDIA ACE: no per-conversation fee for on-device use; production cloud NIM needs an NVIDIA AI Enterprise subscription; Game Agent SDK in beta and open source, requires Ampere or newer; PUBG Ally requires 8 GB VRAM; Total War: PHARAOH's advisor reportedly used ~6 GB VRAM; Sept 2026 added Nemotron Speech 3.5 Streaming and Qwen3 TTS fine-tuning; Nemotron 3.5 Bolt E4B announced for 2026-10-23. Lesson: "Language-model deliberation cannot keep pace with game ticks, so the fast control layer must run independently"; offline evaluations diverged from player preferences. - PUBG Ally numbers (Krafton report via Inven): 1,046 paid participants, 38,956 sessions over 28 days in a Korean PC bang; on-device model swapped six times; the LM is 4-bit quantized at 1.6-2.1 GB, 8 GB VRAM recommended; NPS rose from ~+8 early to ~+36 in the final phase; perception split tool 50.0%, companion 31.5%, teammate 18.5%; gameplay scores all below 3/5 (combat 2.58, judgment 2.88, response speed 2.93, command execution 2.99). Median API cost per match in research replays: Claude Opus 4.8 $5.14-$7.10, Claude Sonnet 4.6 $3.70-$5.17, Gemma 4 31B $0.02-$0.06, on-device $0. Runtime is a 2B on-device model (ASR + SLM + TTS). — [TalkEsport](https://www.talkesport.com/news/pubg-ally-claude-ai-cost-per-match/); [NVIDIA developer blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/?p=119043)

## 5. Hybrid architectures (LLM proposes, rules dispose; dialogue-only; constrained choice; director; generated plans; grounding; caching; small local models)

### Takeaway
The consistent 2025-2026 recommendation, from academic frameworks and from the two largest shipped deployments (Teammates, PUBG Ally), is a two-layer design: a deterministic behavior tree / state machine owns actions and world facts at tick rate, and the LLM is an event-driven advisor that returns text plus structured labels drawn from a closed set, validated before anything touches game state. Storylet systems (Drama Llama, Dramamancer) show how to let the LLM judge authored natural-language preconditions rather than author the plot.

### Cited Findings
- Behavior-tree + LLM: PUBG Ally's System 1 (behavior tree at tick rate) continues and can preempt while System 2 (LLM) deliberates; the LLM chooses from available high-level actions through a bounded tool interface. No single-layer ablation was run. — [alphaxiv 2609.29837](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.29837)
- Teammates: squadmates use "traditional action trees for core actions" with the LLM adding context; the pure-LLM assistant hallucinated nonexistent health packs. — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/?p=310806)
- Arm "First Contact" (2026-08-05): Gemma 3 1B fine-tuned with LoRA on ~19,000 synthetic single-turn interactions, int8 quantized (4.86 GB to 1.26 GB, sentiment accuracy 94% to 89%); each turn the LLM gets a compact snapshot (NPC sentiment, previous topic, player message), conversations are stateless; the LLM returns an in-character reply plus structured labels (hostile/not hostile; alignment low/medium/high) and "does not control game actions"; an appraisal layer maps labels to signals and lightweight state machines update sentiment and select actions. Pixel 9 latency: STT 1,512 ms to first text, LLM 813 ms to first token at 18.3 tok/s, TTS 154 ms; speech end to first spoken word 967 ms. Key lesson: "do not turn the game engine into an LLM runtime." — [Arm Community blog](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/first-contact-a-mobile-architecture-for-llm_2d00_powered-game-characters)
- Aalto thesis "The mind and the body": LLM as high-level "mind" for strategic and emotional decisions, FSM and behavior tree for runtime control, implemented in a survival builder (Echoshell). — [Aalto](https://aaltodoc.aalto.fi/items/307c8b44-d9bd-4b26-831c-ca69ddc808c6)
- HeRoN (Cimino, Deufemia, Selice, 2026): RL policy plus an LLM strategy generator with a reviewer component that checks proposals against environment constraints; up to 81% task-success improvement over RL baselines with fewer constraint-violating actions. — [IRIS Unisa](https://www.iris.unisa.it/handle/11386/4948713)
- LLM reasoner + classical planner (Puerta-Merino, Sabater-Mir, Jan 2025): the LLM decides in unanticipated situations, a planner produces executable plans. — [arXiv 2501.10106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10106v1)
- FSE 2025 industry paper: LLMs and agentic AI used to build complex behavior trees, aimed at both technical and non-technical developers. — [FSE 2025](https://conf.researchr.org/details/fse-2025/fse-2025-industry-papers/25/Enhancing-Game-AI-Behaviors-with-Large-Language-Models-and-Agentic-AI)
- "Agents Play Thousands of 3D Video Games" (Mar 2025): LLMs generate behavior-tree DSL representations with small neural nets for learned parts, as a middle ground between rule-based BTs and end-to-end policies. — [arXiv 2503.13356](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.13356)
- Storylets with LLM-judged triggers (Drama Llama, Jan 2025, Kreminski group): authors write setting, characters, and "triggers" with a natural-language condition, a sequence of stage-direction actions, and a type (Basic/Ending); after every message a minimal LLM drama manager answers YES/NO per active trigger, in author order, stopping at the first that fires; real-time generation within ~3 s per step; six-author study: structural flexibility strongest, originality and character development weakest; 3 of 6 called output clichéd; trigger timing inconsistent ("did not work as expected" for one author); output quality tracked author effort on triggers; sequential checking limits trigger count. — [arXiv 2501.09099](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09099v1)
- Dramamancer (UIST Adjunct 2025; design paper Jan 2026): author-defined schema of style, characters, scenes (fixed opening line, events, transitions); each event has a condition and an outcome; two single-call modules: instantiation (next line) and interpretation (which event conditions are satisfied after each player input); prompt constraints restrict generated lines to NPCs, work satisfied outcomes in "organically," and require responsiveness to the latest input. Author-side evaluation variables: style adherence, character distinctiveness, scene awareness, event detection accuracy, outcome realization. — [arXiv 2601.18785](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18785v1); [DBLP listing](https://dblp1.uni-trier.de/pid/21/10890.html)
- Kreminski and Wang (June 2025): GPT-4-tier models produce causally sound stories at small scale, but character intentionality and dramatic conflict remain hard. — [arXiv 2506.10161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10161?context=cs)
- Knowledge-gated mystery dialogue (Rahmati and Zhao, Sept 2026): a Structured Knowledge Tree with a tri-agent pipeline (retrieval, dialogue generation, response verification); the suspect can reveal only what the current narrative state allows; critical hallucinations reduced 64.78%, premature disclosure "entirely prevented," at the cost of usability trade-offs around forced reveals. — [arXiv 2609.23043](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23043)
- Grounding and canon: a vendor engineering post recommends retrieving lore at inference time from a versioned canon database scoped per character rather than fine-tuning on lore (which freezes a stale snapshot); explicit `knows_about`/`does_not_know` lists; structured output for anything that changes game state so quest acceptance is handled by game logic, not free text; three memory tiers (last 5-10 turns verbatim, a significant-events log, world state from game data). System-prompt-only characters "start drifting around turns 10-15," grounding plus memory reaches 50+ turns. — [Respan](https://respan.ai/resources/npc-dialogue-consistency)
- Guardrail design (Ixie Gaming): "agentic NPCs should never speak or act directly from the model layer"; constraints as "narrative collision walls" (forbidden actions, immutable beliefs, tone rules); memory as curated state that decays and resets at milestones; fairness-critical systems (difficulty, rewards, progression gates) stay deterministic; use cached/authored barks by default and reserve agentic mode for key moments; every decision gets a seed, input log and replay; regression harnesses test "variance bounds, not intelligence"; telemetry on guardrail firing rate, decision entropy, mid-conversation abandons. — [Ixie Gaming](https://www.ixiegaming.com/agentic-npcs-without-breaking-balance/)
- Dialogue-surface-only pattern recommended by a 2026 practitioner guide: "a deterministic state machine holds facts; the model only phrases them"; Qwen3 4B Instruct (Q4_K_M) and Llama 3.2 3B hit 80-130 tok/s with sub-150 ms first-token latency on an 8 GB GPU; 8B-12B models reserved for an offline "game master" layer that writes quests and story beats. — [bestllmfor local LLM for game dev](https://bestllmfor.com/verticals/local-llm-for-game-dev/)
- Local model catalog estimates (Oct 2026): SmolLM2 1.7B ~120 tok/s at 1.2 GB; SmolLM3 3B ~70 tok/s at 2 GB; Mistral 7B ~35 tok/s at 5 GB; 8 GB VRAM tier recommends Qwen 3.5 9B or Granite 4.2 8B; a used RTX 3060 12 GB is ~€250. Speeds are the site's estimates with no stated method. — [bestllmfor](https://bestllmfor.com/verticals/local-llm-for-game-dev/)
- Fixed-persona SLMs (Nov 2025): small models fine-tuned per persona with runtime-swappable memory modules, evaluated on DistilGPT-2, TinyLlama-1.1B, Mistral-7B on consumer hardware; argues cloud LLMs are impractical for many simultaneous local characters. — [arXiv 2511.10277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10277)
- Phi-3.5-mini QLoRA adapters for per-character personality swapping in ~2.1 GB VRAM. — [GWU thesis](https://scholarspace.library.gwu.edu/concern/gw_etds/8k71nj015)
- Gemma 3 1B: ~529 MB, up to 2,585 tok/s prefill on mobile (Google AI Edge, 2025). — [Google developers blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/gemma-3-on-mobile-and-web-with-google-ai-edge/)
- Deflanderization (CPDC 2025, Qwen3-14B with LoRA SFT on the GPU track): a prompting method that "quietly limits over-the-top role-play" so NPCs complete tasks; suppressing excess role-play improved operational accuracy; the persona-vs-task trade-off is "only partly resolved." — [arXiv 2510.13586 via paperium](https://paperium.net/article/en/243/deflanderization-for-game-dialogue-balancing-character-authenticity-with-taskexecution-in-llm-based)
- Player-study evidence for hybrids: randomized study (N=130, "Campus Culture Week" prototype) found LLM-NPCs raised cognitive load (p < .001), raised perceived autonomy, lowered usability and trust, and did not improve overall experience (p = .195), with the largest load increases in content creation and relationship-building tasks. — [arXiv 2604.10107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10107)
- Cinevva's "patterns that work": tight system prompt, short replies, small set of allowed actions or flags; hand-authored plot-critical content with generation for barks, small talk, reactions; limit what the character knows so it cannot leak the ending; "consider whether a branching dialogue tree would do the job before adding a live model call." — [Cinevva guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/ai-npcs-dialogue)
- Caching reality check: Death by AI could not cache because every round was fresh; Generative Agents proposed caching periodic summaries and batching dialogue into one joint prompt. — [Inworld case study](https://inworld.ai/blog/how-inworld-helped-the-ai-game-death-by-ai-with-20-million-players-reach-profitability); [arXiv 2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)

### Inferences
- For a Stardew-like with authored schedules, the cleanest hybrid is: scripts drive schedules, goals, relationships; an LLM "interpretation" call (Dramamancer-style) judges whether an authored natural-language condition has been met by free-text player input; an LLM "instantiation" call phrases a reply from facts the script supplies; the only state changes allowed are flags from a closed enum, validated by code. This is also exactly Where Winds Meet's shipped shape.
- A "director" LLM that picks which authored storylet to fire next is low-risk (it chooses among valid options) and cheap (one small call per scene boundary, not per tick). An LLM that invents the storylet is where cliché, drift and hallucination enter.
- Small local models (1B-4B) are good enough for sentiment labels and short in-character lines when fine-tuned; they are not good enough for plot judgment. A reasonable split is local for barks/labels, cloud Haiku/Flash-Lite class for interpretation, and a frontier model only at authoring time.

### Gaps
- No controlled study compares "LLM picks from authored options" against "LLM generates freely" on player-perceived quality; the evidence is design argument plus shipped practice.
- No published per-NPC latency numbers for an LLM-judged storylet system beyond Drama Llama's ~3 s per step.

- A Smallville-style always-on simulation (every NPC planning and reacting each 5-15 game minutes) is still the expensive pattern; the cost scales with NPC count × game time, not with player attention, which is the opposite of what a single-player indie wants. - Authoring-time generation is where frontier models belong: generating a whole NPC's year of schedules and dialogue once at $0.05 per call is trivially affordable, and batch pricing halves it.

## 7. Authoring-time use: LLM/agent-generated schedules, dialogue, storylets and event scripts

### Takeaway
There is little published, verifiable practice on using coding agents to write NPC schedules and event scripts that are then checked in, but the adjacent evidence is consistent: LLMs are weak at generating valid plans alone and need schemas plus validators; the best-documented authoring systems (Drama Llama, Dramamancer, Ubisoft's "fences," Meaning Machine's "Authored AI") keep the human on structure and let the model fill surface; evaluation needs author-side metrics (style adherence, character distinctiveness, event detection accuracy) and a human reader, because nothing automated catches voice homogenization.

### Cited Findings
- Ubisoft's Ghostwriter has drafted ambient NPC barks internally since 2023 (secondary source). — [Pragmatic Coders](https://www.pragmaticcoders.com/blog/ai-agents-in-game-development)
- Teammates' narrative director: building personalities and the world required "thousands of words of text," a different workflow from "we write dialogue, we record and this is it"; the team is still balancing how much narrative text guides characters without adding latency or hallucinations. — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/?p=310806); [Ubisoft News](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/3mWlITIuWuu0MoVuR6o8ps)
- Drama Llama's authoring loop is iterative: set up the world, run autonomous simulations, adjust the setting, then add and refine triggers; authors averaged 3-4 triggers per story; maintaining trigger consistency "consumed effort that could have gone to other qualities." — [arXiv 2501.09099](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09099v1)
- Dramamancer's author-side evaluation variables: style adherence, character distinctiveness, scene awareness, event detection accuracy, outcome realization. — [arXiv 2601.18785](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18785v1)
- A backstory-generation paper uses a three-agent Notary / Judge / Stress-Tester pipeline and separates consistency from playability ("Ludic Potential"). — [arXiv 2509.04239](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.04239)
- Ashby et al.: match player input to a knowledge graph, build quests from graph paths and grammars, then condition a fine-tuned GPT-2 on the quest to generate titles and dialogue (pipeline generates the task and its presentation separately). — [arXiv 2402.14879](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.14879v1)
- Practical indie pipeline shape: output as JSON nodes, CSV sheets, or Ren'Py blocks "their engine can parse tonight"; barks as short speakable units tagged with speaker ID and emotion flag. — [sorceress.games](https://sorceress.games/blog/script-an-ai-dialogue-generator-game-npc-pipeline-2026)
- Planning: a 2025 thesis found "LLMs alone are not able to generate functioning plans, but they can be used as an instrument that supports planning if it is expanded with other instruments"; a 2025 schema-based scheduler supplies dates, tasks, constraints, rules and example templates for the model to follow. — [FHNW thesis](https://irf.fhnw.ch/handle/11654/52053); [Corewell Health abstract](https://scholarlyworks.corewellhealth.org/pathology_laboratory_medicine_confabstract/104)
- Voice homogenization evidence: a 2025 linguistic analysis of LLM-run tabletop RPG sessions found model-played characters leaned on stereotyped traits while human lines were more concise and direct; "Generating Video Game Scripts with Style" (NLP4ConvAI 2023) motivates a weighted style memory because several writers contribute to one character and machine scripts "still trail human writing"; a community guide reports chatbots drifting "toward one soft voice within a few replies" and that example dialogue works better than banned-word lists. — [arXiv 2503.20623](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.20623); [ACL Anthology 2023.nlp4convai-1.11](https://preview.aclanthology.org/moar-dois/2023.nlp4convai-1.11.pdf); [roborhythms](https://www.roborhythms.com/?p=33697)
- Consistency test from a 2026 thesis: more event states raised the chance of out-of-turn errors; remembered world knowledge varied with prompt design. — [LIACS thesis 2025-2026](https://theses.liacs.nl/pdf/2025-2026-VerberneTTim.pdf)
- A survey notes "human-authored dialogue often remains preferred in some contexts." — [alphaxiv overview 2601.18785](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2601.18785)
- Emily Short's blog has no post specifically on LLM NPCs; her 2023 Utah lecture "Building Responsive NPCs in the Age of GPT" drew on Versu, Character Engine and Mask of the Rose and promised "what the new generation of LLMs can and cannot offer." Her 2018 mailbag on neural story generation concluded models "are generating surface text rather than plot." — [University of Utah listing](https://games.utah.edu/news/guest-lecture-building-responsive-npcs-in-the-age-of-gpt/); [emshort.blog AI category](https://emshort.blog/category/ai/)
- Convai shipped MCP servers for Unreal (July 2026) and Unity (Aug 2026) so coding agents can set up characters. — [tech-insider comparison](https://tech-insider.org/inworld-ai-vs-convai-vs-nvidia-ace-2026/)
- Regression practice from the guardrails post: automated scenes with fixed inputs and controlled world state, snapshot comparisons, seeds and replay for every decision. — [Ixie Gaming](https://www.ixiegaming.com/agentic-npcs-without-breaking-balance/)
- Evaluation sizing: label 200-500 production NPC turns as a starter eval set; canary a revised prompt on 5% of players. — [Respan](https://respan.ai/resources/npc-dialogue-consistency)

### Inferences
- The authoring-time workflow with the strongest support is: human writes the schema (character sheet, relationships, schedule slots, storylet preconditions as data), the agent generates instances into that schema, validators check structure (times, locations exist, flags exist, no contradictions with canon), and a human reads for voice. Every academic system that worked (Drama Llama, Dramamancer, knowledge trees) is a schema plus a model, never a model alone.
- The Generative Agents "interview-based" result (Section 1) and Teammates' "thousands of words" both suggest the single highest-leverage authoring artifact is a long per-character document; generated schedules and lines should be derived from it, and the same document is what a runtime LLM would be given.
- For style, few-shot example lines per character outperform negative instructions; a per-character style memory (the NLP4ConvAI approach) is the engineering version of that.

### Gaps
- No published case study of a coding agent (Claude Code, Cursor, etc.) generating and testing NPC schedules or event scripts for a shipped game was found; all pipeline descriptions are blog-level or research prototypes.
- No controlled measurement of voice homogenization across LLM-written game dialogue exists; evidence is a tabletop-RPG linguistic analysis and commentary.
- Ubisoft Ghostwriter's current status and any quality metrics are unpublished.

## 8. Known failure modes (incoherence, memory, agreeableness, rule-breaking, over-chattiness, voice homogenization, player exploits)

### Takeaway
The same short list recurs from Smallville (2023) through PUBG Ally (2026): the model says yes to everything and drifts in tone after 10-15 turns; it invents facts and then believes them; it says it will act and then does something else; it leaks modern knowledge into the setting; it talks when it should be quiet; players immediately social-engineer it. All shipped mitigations are structural (closed output enums, canon retrieval, verification passes, short stateless turns), not prompt wording.

### Cited Findings
- Agreeableness: instruction tuning made Smallville agents "overly cooperative," Isabella "rarely said no." — [arXiv 2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)
- Counter-evidence on resistance: in AREG (ICML 2026 benchmark), resistance scores exceeded persuasion scores across all evaluated models; verification-seeking responses were more common in successful defenses than flat refusals; incremental commitment-seeking raised extraction success. — [arXiv 2602.16639](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16639v1); [ICML 2026 listing](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/80201)
- Saying vs doing: Project Sid's Abby says "Sure thing!" while her function call picks "explore"; PUBG Ally answered "Okay" without acting. — [arXiv 2411.00114](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00114v1); [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/26592/built-a-teammate-got-a-chat-partner-kraftons-first-scorecard-for-pubg-ally)
- Hallucination compounding: false outputs feed future prompts, so small rates "poison downstream agent behavior." — [arXiv 2411.00114](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00114v1)
- Over-chattiness: Ally "kept talking during late-game combat when players needed to listen for footsteps." — [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/26592/built-a-teammate-got-a-chat-partner-kraftons-first-scorecard-for-pubg-ally)
- Memory: Ally showed "inconsistent memory"; Smallville's retrieval failures; Respan's "re-offers a completed quest or references events that never happened." — [alphaxiv 2609.29837](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.29837); [Respan](https://respan.ai/resources/npc-dialogue-consistency)
- Anachronism and lore leakage: ketchup in a Five Dynasties setting; a medieval guard mentioning technology; Yuriko citing the real Adam Smith. — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/?p=632199); [Respan](https://respan.ai/resources/npc-dialogue-consistency); [arXiv 2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)
- Player exploits: Where Winds Meet players made a guard believe he had fathered their child and that the child died; got NPCs to complete quests; pushed NPCs into memes. — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/?p=632199); [itemlevel](https://itemlevel.net/where-winds-meet-player-convinces-npc-theyre-pregnant-with-his-baby/)
- Fourth-wall breaks: "Without explicit refusal training, the model agrees" it is an AI; mitigation is refusal rules plus a post-generation character-integrity classifier with regenerate-or-templated-deflection fallback; "no system is fully jailbreak-proof." — [Respan](https://respan.ai/resources/npc-dialogue-consistency)
- Role-play jailbreaks (ACL Findings 2026): role-play improves safety for benign personas but increases unsafe compliance for malicious ones; a "Knowing-but-Doing" failure where models recognize risk and comply anyway. — [ACL Findings 2026](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-acl/2026.findings-acl.349/)
- Cliché and flatness: 3 of 6 Drama Llama authors called the stories clichéd; character development scored lowest. — [arXiv 2501.09099](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09099v1)
- "Customer-service bot in costume": many demos read this way "and players notice"; unconstrained NPCs "invent facts, drift off-lore, and can spoil quests"; "character consistency matters more than raw intelligence." — [Cinevva guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/ai-npcs-dialogue)
- Pacing/spoilers: without gating, mystery NPCs reveal clues early; the knowledge-tree system removed premature disclosure entirely at a usability cost. — [arXiv 2609.23043](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23043)
- Service dependency: Vaudeville's external AI service went down during its biggest stream; billing surprises followed launch. — [Game Developer pre-mortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/press-release/vaudeville-a-pre-mortem)
- Performance: Smart Zoi on-device inference reportedly caused sharp frame-rate drops on high-end GPUs (single review). — [36Kr](https://www.36kr.com/p/3248594734276870)
- Player cost: LLM-NPCs raised cognitive load and lowered usability and trust in a 130-participant randomized study. — [arXiv 2604.10107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10107)
- Hallucination rate as a floor: 1.3% of awareness responses hallucinated even in the original Smallville. — [arXiv 2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)

### Inferences
- For a Stardew-like, the exploits that matter are relationship exploits (talking an NPC into friendship or gifts). Where Winds Meet's shape (affinity moves only when the authored "problem" is addressed, judged by the model, with a cap) is the proven mitigation; an LLM should never be able to write to a relationship value directly.
- "Agreeableness" is partly a model-era artifact (gpt-3.5 instruction tuning) and partly structural; the AREG result suggests modern models can hold a position if the position is explicit in state. Give each NPC an authored list of immutable beliefs and have the verifier check replies against it.

### Gaps
- No NPC-specific sycophancy study exists; the AREG and ACL work is on negotiation and safety, not game characters.
- No published rate of guardrail-firing or exploit frequency from any shipped game.

## 9. Synthesis for a Stardew-like explorable world: where the LLM should and should not be in the loop

### Takeaway
The evidence supports the stated design intent. Put the LLM at authoring time (frontier model, batch pricing, schema-constrained, human-read) for schedules, dialogue variants and storylets; at runtime use scripts for everything that touches world state, and reserve LLM calls for two narrow jobs: judging whether free-text player input satisfies an authored condition, and phrasing a reply from facts the script hands it, with a small-model budget and a closed set of allowed flags.

### Cited Findings
- Scripts-first precedent from the research side: Affordable Generative Agents' "Lifestyle Policy" reuses routine plans instead of regenerating them to cut Smallville's cost. — [arXiv 2402.02053](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02053v2)
- Scripts-first precedent from shipped games: Where Winds Meet (text plus flags, no quest rewriting), Teammates (action trees for core actions), PUBG Ally (behavior tree at tick rate, LLM event-driven), inZOI (on-device SLM only adjusts the next day's schedule from experiences). — [allthings.how](https://allthings.how/where-winds-meet-and-ai-how-the-mmos-chatbot-npcs-work/); [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/?p=310806); [alphaxiv 2609.29837](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.29837); [NVIDIA](https://www.nvidia.com/en-gb/geforce/news/nvidia-ace-naraka-bladepoint-inzoi-launch-this-month/)
- Authorial control as the key variable in the only large player study: "AI-powered experiences with strong authorial control outperform those with weak authorial control." (studio-affiliated study) — [GDC 2026 session](https://schedule.gdconf.com/session/what-good-are-ai-npcs-lessons-from-a-large-scale-player-study-presented-by-nvidia/917528)
- "Consider whether a branching dialogue tree would do the job before adding a live model call." — [Cinevva guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/ai-npcs-dialogue)
- Cheapest viable runtime models and per-turn arithmetic are in Section 6; local 1-4B models produce labels and short lines at sub-second latency on 8 GB GPUs or phones. — [Arm blog](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/first-contact-a-mobile-architecture-for-llm_2d00_powered-game-characters); [bestllmfor](https://bestllmfor.com/verticals/local-llm-for-game-dev/)

### Inferences
- Runtime "high-plot" LLM use is best modeled as a Dramamancer scene: an authored scene with a fixed opening, authored events with natural-language conditions and authored outcomes, one interpretation call per player input, one instantiation call per NPC line, and a hard scene budget (e.g., 20 turns). Everything outside such scenes is scripted.
- inZOI's "adjust tomorrow's schedule from today's experiences" is the one place a cheap nightly LLM call over a scripted schedule could add life without touching state mid-day: the LLM proposes schedule deviations from an allowed list, code validates them against locations and opening hours (Smallville's spatial-norm failure), and the result is just another authored-format schedule for the next day.
- Build the runtime and authoring pipelines on the same schema. The character document that the coding agent writes from is the same document the runtime model is grounded in, and the validators that check generated schedules are the same ones that reject runtime proposals.

### Gaps
- No source directly tests the "scripts by default, LLM for high-plot scenes" hybrid in a life-sim; the inference is assembled from the detective-game, storylet, and shipped-MMO evidence.
