# Presenting interiors, exteriors and floors in 2D top-down / isometric games

Researched 2026-10-23. Scope: presentation techniques (scene swap, roof hiding, cutaway/floor slicing), the player-experience trade-offs designers and players have described, and the rendering mechanics each commits a web/canvas renderer to. World data model and map editors/formats are out of scope (other researchers).

A note on sourcing: several canonical primary pages were unreachable during this session (Bulbapedia, TV Tropes, Prima Games, sims.fandom.com, the Dwarf Fortress wiki and stardewvalleywiki.com rate-limited or returned 403/429/503). Where a claim rests only on a search-result excerpt of a page I could not open, the cited URL is the page the excerpt came from and the finding is marked "(excerpt)". Claims I could not source at all are in Gaps, not in Findings.

## Key question 1: Scene swap on entering a building (Stardew, Pokémon, Harvest Moon, Moonlighter, Graveyard Keeper)

### Takeaway
The scene-swap convention treats each interior as a separate map joined to the exterior by a door-tile warp, which is exactly how Stardew Valley's data is organised ("maps" are physical spaces, "locations" hold the data, and interiors are standalone maps linked by warps). The "bigger on the inside" convention is openly acknowledged in Pokémon (Pokopia documents a 3x3x3 exterior with a 6x5x4 interior and a loading screen on entry) and is explained by commentators as a way to keep the overworld uncrowded; forum designers add that the alternative (seeing the interior in place) forces buildings to be much larger than a few tiles.

### Cited Findings
- Stardew Valley separates "maps" and "locations": "A location holds most of the area's data, such as fishing, forage, music, and NPC access. A map is the physical space the player experiences. It holds tiles and some properties." A house interior "is a standalone map, not a patch, so it needs its own location data." — [Stardew Modding Wiki, Tutorial: Map Patches and Warps](https://stardewmodding.wiki.gg/wiki/Tutorial:_Map_Patches_and_Warps)
- The door is a tile with an `Action` property; the tutorial's example is `LockedDoorWarp 9 16 {{ModID}}_DaoHouse 900 2200 AirynDao 1000`, which "takes you to these coordinates INSIDE the house," then names the target map, then opening and closing times (900 to 2200), optionally an NPC and a friendship requirement. Leaving uses `AddWarps` entries like `9 18 Custom_SBV_SunberryVillage 38 65` (tile stepped on, then arrival tile outside). Placement advice: the exit tile should sit just below the map's edge, and the entry warp must be at least one tile further in than the exit warp "or players get stuck warping back out." — [Stardew Modding Wiki, Tutorial: Map Patches and Warps](https://stardewmodding.wiki.gg/wiki/Tutorial:_Map_Patches_and_Warps)
- Interior maps have a `DefaultArrivalTile` so that any warp into the location (including the debug warp) "put[s] the player at the door instead of in the void." — [Stardew Modding Wiki, Tutorial: Map Patches and Warps](https://stardewmodding.wiki.gg/wiki/Tutorial:_Map_Patches_and_Warps)
- Because interiors are separate maps linked by warps, "the interior's size isn't tied to the outside building's footprint." (excerpt; the modding wiki documents buildings and interiors via the `Data/Buildings` asset) — [Stardew Valley Wiki, Modding:Buildings](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Modding:Buildings)
- Stardew NPC schedules are lists of points, each naming "The location name the NPC should walk towards. If omitted, defaults to the previous map," so NPC movement is defined across maps, not within one continuous space. Married NPCs leave "through the bus stop" ("the warp out of BusStop back to the Farm"). A `bed` destination sends the NPC home. The page does not describe door handling or whether NPCs are visible while between maps. — [Stardew Valley Wiki, Modding:Schedule data](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Modding:Schedule_data)
- Players report that sheds are "smaller on the inside" relative to a barn's cost and that the farmhouse exterior stays the same size no matter how the interior expands (a German GamePro piece relaying a Reddit question about the unchanging outside footprint). (excerpts) — [Steam Community, Stardew Valley](https://steamcommunity.com/app/413150/discussions/0/343787920138166609); [GamePro, Stardew Valley Haus-Ausbau-Theorie](https://www.gamepro.de/artikel/stardew-valley-haus-ausbauen-theorie,3425665.html)
- Pokémon Pokopia's stand-alone building kits document the convention explicitly: "Unlike buildings constructed from blocks, its interior is larger than its exterior, and a loading screen occurs when entering or exiting the building." The City hut kit is "Tile size 3x3x3 ... Interior size 6x5x4"; the City house kit is "Tile size 8x6x5 ... Interior size 14x10x4" over two floors. (excerpts; Bulbapedia returned 403 on direct fetch) — [Bulbapedia, City house kit](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/City_house_kit); [Bulbapedia, City hut kit](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/city_hut_kit)
- A 2020-era meme article summarises the convention: "Pokemon buildings have historically been tiny on the outside but huge on the inside, just like many Animal Crossing buildings," framing it as saving overworld space ("the developers tried to keep the game more open with less big buildings taking up space") and suggesting Sword and Shield moved away from it. (excerpt; secondary source, not developer statement) — [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/redditor-posts-viral-meme-unites-pokemon-animal-crossing-fanbases)
- A player's explanation of why games fade to black on doors (The Elder Scrolls Online, but the same logic applies to 2D swaps): "the indoor portion of a building is actually located about 10-20ft underground," its dimensions "don't perfectly match the outside of the building," and "The fade-to-black effect is there to disguise the sudden teleport your character does." (player opinion) — [ESO forums](https://forums.elderscrollsonline.com/en/discussion/comment/7571328)
- Forum consensus (gamedev.net, 2015): "Interiors in games are almost always made as separate scenes from the exteriors. This is so you can easily remove unseen detail." An itch.io commenter agrees: "Most games keep their house interiors on separate maps." (excerpts; nene.gamedev.net was unreachable for full-thread fetch) — [gamedev.net, How to do RPG interiors?](https://nene.gamedev.net/forums/topic/668452-how-to-do-rpg-interiors/5231571); [itch.io post](https://itch.io/post/2964852)
- The same gamedev.net thread gives the rule of thumb for when to swap versus stay: for "just an item vendor" the commenter "would rather not deal with the area transition," while "For an interior with exploration or puzzles, a separate area is preferred." (excerpt) — [gamedev.net, How to do RPG interiors?](https://nene.gamedev.net/forums/topic/668452-how-to-do-rpg-interiors/5231571)
- A hybrid proposed on Unity's forum: preload the (simpler) indoor scene "under the map while approaching the building, then teleporting at the door," to avoid the cost of unloading and reloading the outdoor scene. (excerpt) — [Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/going-from-world-to-interiors-of-buildings-in-simple-3d-rpg-game-teleport-or-new-scene/243432)
- A 2020 devlog (Fallen Worlds, oblique 2D view) chose separate rooms per scene: "Opening a door loads the new room as its own scene, and leaving it returns the player to the hallway," because the oblique camera "prevented placing a wall behind the player or adding a ceiling," and top-down interior layouts from the design docs "often didn't work in this view." The author calls separate rooms "the only practical way to make multi-room interiors in this perspective, at the cost of some creative freedom." — [Fallen Worlds devlog, Interior Inception](https://precipice-games.itch.io/fallen-worlds/devlog/144628/interior-inception)
- The Camelot scripting language (an academic narrative engine) exposes the door transition as a primitive: its `enter` command has a flag so "The screen fades in as the character is entering through the door." — [Camelot docs, enter](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/projects/camelot/v1-3/enter.html)

### Inferences
- The scene-swap convention costs the town its visual continuity but buys three things the sources keep returning to: interiors can be any size, unseen detail is never rendered, and schedule logic reduces to "which map, which tile." Stardew's schedule format (destination location + tile, defaulting to the previous map) only makes sense under scene swap.
- The door-warp data shape (door tile with action, arrival tile, opening hours, friendship gate) is the complete interface between exterior and interior. A renderer that adopts scene swap needs camera bounds per map and a transition effect, and nothing else from the interior.
- The "bigger on the inside" mismatch is tolerated by players as a genre convention; the complaints found are about relative value (a shed "smaller on the inside") rather than the mismatch itself.

### Gaps
- No ConcernedApe interview or statement on why Stardew uses separate interior maps, or on how he thinks about NPCs vanishing at doors, was found in this session.
- I could not verify from a source how Pokémon's mainline games animate door entry (door-opening tile animation followed by a fade) or how Harvest Moon, Moonlighter and Graveyard Keeper transition; searches returned nothing on-topic. Treat any such description as unverified.
- Stardew's exact in-game behaviour when an NPC reaches a door (walks onto the door tile and is removed from the exterior map, appears inside) is not documented on the schedule page fetched; the wiki pages that might say so (Villagers) were rate-limited.

## Key question 2: Seamless worlds with roof hiding (Ultima VII / Exult, RuneScape Classic, modern pixel RPGs)

### Takeaway
Exult's implementation of Ultima VII's roof hiding is a single integer cutoff: every object has a "lift" (height, 1 lift = half a tile), the engine finds the lowest blocking object four lifts above the camera actor's tile ("Roof can be any object, not just a literal roof"), and the painter skips every object whose lift is at or above that value. That gives roof removal, upper-floor hiding and "inside" detection (used for ambient sound) from one mechanism, at the cost that everything above the cutoff on screen disappears, neighbours included. Modern engine advice converges on either a per-building roof group toggled by a trigger area or a shader fade, and warns that one shared roof layer turns every roof off at once.

### Cited Findings
- Exult's render state: `int skip_above_actor; // Level above actor to skip rendering.` and `int skip_lift; // Skip objects with lift >= this. 0 means 'terrain-editing' mode.` The effective cutoff is `get_render_skip_lift()` which returns `skip_above_actor < skip_lift ? skip_above_actor : skip_lift`. "Inside" is derived from it: `bool is_main_actor_inside() const { return skip_above_actor < 31; }`. The default is `skip_above_actor(31)`, so nothing is culled until a roof is detected. — [Exult source, gamewin.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/exult/exult/master/gamewin.h); [Exult source, gamewin.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/exult/exult/master/gamewin.cc)
- The painter's rule is one comparison: in `Game_render::paint_object`, `const int lift = obj->get_lift(); if (lift >= skip) { return; }` where `skip = gwin->get_render_skip_lift()`. The parameter is documented only as "Don't display above this lift." Selected-object outlines also respect it (`obj->get_lift() < render_skip`). — [Exult source, gamerend.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/exult/exult/master/gamerend.cc)
- Roof detection happens when the view scrolls (`Game_window::set_scrolls`), under the comment "// Set where to skip rendering.": `set_above_main_actor(nlist->is_roof(tx, ty, camera_actor->get_lift()));` followed by `set_in_dungeon(...)` and `set_ice_dungeon(...)`. — [Exult source, gamewin.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/exult/exult/master/gamewin.cc)
- `Map_chunk::is_roof` checks for a "'roof' above lift in tile (tx, ty)" by probing "4 above lift"; "Roof can be any object, not just a literal roof." It calls `get_lowest_blocked(lift + 4, tx, ty)` ("Lowest lift that's blocked by an object, or -1 if none") and returns that height, or 255 if nothing is found. A comment says "A return of 31 means no roof," which the fetched code contradicts (it returns 255); the two sentinel values are inconsistent in the current source. In `Map_chunk::add`, the comment "Looks like a roof?" precedes code that sets `roof = 1` for building-class objects at lift 5 or higher. Another comment: "SI has roofs at random levels!!" — [Exult source, objs/chunks.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/exult/exult/master/objs/chunks.cc)
- Lift units: "// 1 lift = 1/2 tile." and screen offset "const int lft = 4 * t.tz;" (4 pixels per lift at 8-pixel tiles). The main actor "Up on something?" check is `main_actor->get_lift() % 5`, consistent with 5 lifts per storey. — [Exult source, gamewin.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/exult/exult/master/gamewin.cc)
- The cutoff is also lowered for NPCs the camera is asked to locate: `const int above = pos.tz + npc->get_info().get_3d_height() - 1; if (skip_above_actor > above) set_above_main_actor(above);` so the engine can reveal an NPC under a roof by dropping the cutoff to just above that NPC. — [Exult source, gamewin.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/exult/exult/master/gamewin.cc)
- "Inside" drives audio, not just rendering: "// Testing. When outside play birds during daytime or crickets at night" guards on `!gwin->is_main_actor_inside()`. — [Exult source, gamewin.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/exult/exult/master/gamewin.cc)
- Dungeons are the other case: "If there is a dungeon roof over our head we black out every tile on screen that doens't have a roof at the height of the roof that is directly over our head." (`paint_blackness`, enabled when `in_dungeon >= skip_above_actor`). — [Exult source, gamerend.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/exult/exult/master/gamerend.cc)
- Exult's `--buildmap` flag exposes three roof modes for whole-world map renders: paint all roofs, skip second-floor roofs, or paint none. (excerpt) — [Debian manpage, exult(6)](https://dyn.manpages.debian.org/trixie/exult/exult.6.en.html)
- Ultima VII's view: walls and characters are drawn at a leftward angle "creating a pseudo-isometric appearance"; maps are "more visibly multi-layered, and objects and things can be stacked on top of each other," and some areas are reached by stacking items into makeshift stairs. Tiles exist internally but no longer restrict movement or placement. — [Wikipedia, Ultima VII: The Black Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultima_VII:_The_Black_Gate)
- A forum poster describes the projection as "a -135 oblique projection," the kind "used in the ol' days for design/architecture." (excerpt, forum opinion) — [OpenGameArt comment](https://opengameart.org/comment/1366)
- Ultima VII's engine had multi-floor content ("the huge upper floor of an equally huge building" housed the Ethereal Void) and the designers duplicated buildings off-map for cutscenes "for programming reasons"; Exult's multi-map support removes the need. — [Ultima Codex, Quirks of the Ultima VII engine](https://www.wiki.ultimacodex.com/wiki/Quirks_of_the_Ultima_VII_engine)
- RuneScape Classic used the same convention in an isometric world: "When you enter a building the roof will be removed automatically, so that the player may see the interior of the building." Its documented failures are the authoring-cost cases: the Sinclair Mansion roof "does not display while you are between the hedges" (trigger region too large), and the Digsite shed "does not remove itself when you enter it." — [RuneScape Classic Wiki, Roof](https://classic.runescape.wiki/w/Roof)
- Godot forum (Jan 2024) lays out the mechanisms: (a) roofs on their own TileMap layer, `set_layer_enabled(roof_layer, false)` on entry, with the trade-off that "turning one off would turn of other ones" unless each building gets its own layer, which "gets messy quickly"; (b) each roof as its own node with an Area2D, `hide()`/`show()` on enter/exit ("easier in a sense"); (c) viewports; (d) a shader that hides "only circle inside it, or not hide it but make it half-transparent." The asker wanted alpha so roofs stay "a bit transparent but yet visible." — [Godot Forum, How to make tiles transparent when the player goes behind them](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/how-to-make-tiles-transparent-when-the-player-goes-behind-them/44438)
- A Godot RPG tutorial fades rather than hides: temporarily set the exterior sprite's alpha modulate low so the floor shows through. (excerpt) — [DEV Community, Godot 4 RPG part 18](https://dev.to/christinec_dev/lets-learn-godot-4-by-making-an-rpg-part-18-scene-transitions-day-night-cycle-4mnm)
- A GameDev.tv course thread notes the trigger-shape problem: for L- or T-shaped buildings use several trigger colliders and a counter that increments on enter and decrements on exit, so the roof returns only when all are left. (excerpt) — [GameDev.tv community](https://community.gamedev.tv/t/transparency-on-a-specific-section-of-the-tilemap/224516)
- Tile-based engines that bake layers make this hard: an Intersect/Ascension engine developer said roof hiding "would actually be a pretty involved modification because tile layers are cached into big images." (excerpt) — [Ascension Game Dev forum](https://ascensiongamedev.com/topic/3248-walking-inside-building)
- The design cost named most often: the transparent-roof approach "requires less power and space and is easier to edit," but "Using the transparent roof option means houses need to be much bigger than they otherwise could be"; "You can't make a 3x3 tile house work if you go the transparent roof option," and medium houses "can fill a whole screen." (excerpt) — [gamedev.net, How to do RPG interiors?](https://nene.gamedev.net/forums/topic/668452-how-to-do-rpg-interiors/5231571)
- A 3D-engine variant of the same idea: each building is two models, bottom and top, and a zone script hides the top while the player is inside. (excerpt) — [GameGuru forum](https://forum.game-guru.com/thread/218877)
- The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening (Switch) made the overworld seamless ("the overworld has no screen transitions because areas load in their entirety") and reviewers reported frame-rate dips "when exiting areas"; none of the sources found say whether houses are separate loads. (excerpts) — [Zelda Wiki](https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Link%27s_Awakening_(Nintendo_Switch)); [Stuff.tv review](https://www.stuff.tv/?p=349356)

### Inferences
- Exult's design shows the minimum a seamless renderer needs: a height value on every drawable, a "cutoff" computed from what is above the player, and a paint loop that skips above the cutoff. Doing it per building (RuneScape, Godot per-roof nodes) is more work to author but avoids the "everything above lift N vanishes, including the neighbour's roof" effect the single-cutoff approach produces.
- Roof hiding reuses "is there something over my head" for ambience (Exult's birds/crickets), so one interior test can drive sound, lighting and rendering together. Scene swap gets this for free from "which map am I in."
- The two documented RuneScape bugs are the authoring failure modes: trigger regions that are too generous (roof vanishes from the garden) or missing (roof never lifts). A per-building roof group tied to the building's footprint polygon, not a hand-drawn trigger, avoids both.
- The Godot thread's shader option (a circle of transparency around the player) is the only technique found that lets the player see inside without exposing the whole building, which matters for not revealing NPCs in rooms the player is not in.

### Gaps
- Exult's official documentation and FAQ (exult.sourceforge.io/docs.php, exult.info) returned 403/404, so I could not cite the user-facing description of roof behaviour, cheat keys for lift, or the Exult team's notes on diverging from the original engine. The source code is the primary evidence here.
- No sources were found for how Hyper Light Drifter, Eastward, Sea of Stars, CrossCode or Chained Echoes handle interiors or roof fading; searches returned general comparisons only. CrossCode's height-level system in particular has no devlog I could locate.
- No source was found on A Link Between Worlds' house presentation beyond its shared map with A Link to the Past; the "roof lift" behaviour asked about could not be confirmed.
- No source found that discusses the specific "see into neighbours" failure of seamless roof hiding as a design problem; it is inferred from Exult's single-cutoff mechanism.

## Key question 3: Floors in a 2D top-down game (Project Zomboid, The Sims, Dwarf Fortress, Prison Architect 2, Shadows of Doubt)

### Takeaway
Every multi-floor 2D game found slices by floor: it draws the player's current level and hides or partially hides the ones above, and each has a documented readability failure at the stairs. Project Zomboid players describe the upper room appearing "as if it just appears" only when the character's Z crosses 1.00; The Sims offers three wall modes (walls up, cutaway, walls down) plus explicit floor stepping, and players still complain about cutaway in small rooms; Dwarf Fortress shows each Z-level as a 2D plane with a coloured depth indicator; Prison Architect 2 and Shadows of Doubt escaped the problem by going 3D.

### Cited Findings
- Project Zomboid: a July 28, 2022 thread, "Visibility at the top of stairs problem," complains that at the top of stairs "the entire room is revealed as if it just appears," and that the character on the top step cannot see or target zombies there. A reply explains the rule: "If you're on the floor below, you're on the floor below. Don't loiter on the stairs," and "if your Z coordinate is 0.99 instead of 1.00, you are not the second floor." Another: the upper floor "will be completely invisible until you are literally on top of it." One player suggested "a smoother graphical transition, such as a faded view of the floor below." — [Steam Community, Project Zomboid](https://steamcommunity.com/app/108600/discussions/0/3459345750024537580)
- A 2017 PZ thread says the view "looks like you are walking off the top step onto nothing." (excerpt) — [Steam Community, Project Zomboid](https://steamcommunity.com/app/108600/discussions/0/343787283750680023)
- Workaround mods exist because of this: "Check Around" (formerly "Check Upstairs") lets players look up or down stairs with a keybind, "for those nasty floors hiding zombies upstairs, or even downstairs!"; "Peek a View" (Build 42) adds "a wall cutaway at greater range" and "a stair view that renders the upper floor while you climb," while promising "No X-ray, no see-through walls, no enemy outlines" and following "the same rules as vanilla." (excerpts) — [Check Around mod](https://catalogue.smods.ru/archives/280480); [Peek a View mod](https://catalogue.smods.ru/archives/430878)
- PZ's cutaway system is described by players as "primarily for indoors," with a persistent visual bug outside; in Build 42 walls are said to cut away when the character is in combat stance, which "doesn't always work for certain types of objects." A 2022 commenter argued below-ground floors are harder to add than floors above "because of the nature of the viewpoint and the cutaway mechanics." (excerpts, player opinion) — [Steam Community, Project Zomboid](https://steamcommunity.com/app/108600/discussions/0/4297067919526958205); [Steam Community, Project Zomboid](https://steamcommunity.com/app/108600/discussions/0/3459347019170937208)
- The Sims 3 guide: "Change Wall Mode Home and End : Toggle through three different views of your house—walls down, cutaway walls, and walls up," with separate keys for changing floors. (excerpt; Prima returned 403) — [Prima Games, The Sims 3 eGuide, Build Mode](https://primagames.com/eguides/the-sims-3-eguide/essentials/creating-a-house/build-mode)
- The Sims 4 cutaway: "only the exterior walls stay up, and the rest cut away to show the rooms inside"; interior walls drop to a thin strip and appear fully only in walls-up view; half walls stay up at every setting. Players find it frustrating ("I can't even place a mirror above the sink bc the wall is invisible") and raise walls temporarily while placing items. One player asked for a "peekaboo" view showing "whole walls opposite the camera in both live and build modes," especially in small rooms, because "Cutaway varies considerably if you have small rooms and some other types of wall setups and room shapes." (excerpts) — [EA Forums](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/the-sims-4-mods-and-custom-content-en/sims-4-inside-house-walls-are-invisible/9151597/replies/9151603); [EA Forums](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/the-sims-4-gameplay-en/request-to-add-another-walls-up-view/8654173); [EA Forums](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/the-sims-4-gameplay-en/inner-walls-wont-cut-away-anymore/8694620)
- Multi-unit buildings break cutaway: an EA bug report (marked Fixed) says in some apartments "the ceiling of the adjacent unit doesn't disappear" in cutaway and walls-down views. (excerpt) — [EA Forums bug report](https://forums.ea.com/idea/the-sims-4-bug-reports-en/cl-adjacent-apartment-units-ceiling-always-showing-in-cutawaywalls-down/11956190)
- The opposite complaint exists for automatic floor hiding: an inZOI player asked for "an option to stop upper floors from disappearing automatically (Floor Cutaway Toggle)," calling it a problem "that does not exist in The Sims 4," where floor stepping is manual. (excerpt) — [inZOI forum](https://forum.playinzoi.com/t/topic/6261)
- Dwarf Fortress: "Z-Level, also known as elevation, describes vertical space (depth or altitude) within Dwarf Fortress." Levels are changed in classic mode with `<` and `>` and in the Premium version "using the scroll wheel and the e (up) and c (down) keys"; the Steam version also has "move one z level up/down and move 10 z levels up/down." The depth indicator: "blue is above ground, brown is below ground, and the bright spot in the middle (yellow or cyan) shows the current level," and "if there are mountains, or any form of uneven terrain on your map, these numbers will vary in the same z-level." (excerpts; the wiki rate-limited direct fetches) — [Dwarf Fortress Wiki, Z-level](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/Z-level); [Steam Community, Dwarf Fortress](https://steamcommunity.com/app/975370/discussions/0/3709307511570055016)
- A Dec 12, 2022 Steam thread on DF's view: each Z-layer is "displayed to the player as a 2D plane," lower layers can be seen from above, "The viewport is 2D," the camera cannot rotate, and 3D visualisation existed only as the unported Armok View mod. The thread does not describe shading of lower levels. — [Steam Community, Dwarf Fortress](https://steamcommunity.com/app/975370/discussions/0/3716062978749212573)
- Multilevel rendering (current level plus levels beneath, with fog and shadow parameters) "is well known to users of TWBT plugin on desktop"; a 2016 mobile client refreshed only the top X levels (default 3). (excerpt) — [Dwarf Fortress Remote 1.15](https://mifki.com/?p=594)
- The Stonesense isometric overlay that replaced DF's 2D tiles worked up to v47.0 and "is not working in v50 Steam/Premium." (excerpt) — [Dwarf Fortress Wiki, Utility:Stonesense](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/Utility:Stonesense)
- RimWorld (single floor, roofs as data): the "show roofs" toggle overlays all roofed tiles in green, light green for constructed/thin roofs and dark green for overhead mountain; players call the overlay hard to read ("incredibly tedious to see where the 2 holes in my roof over a room happen to be"), and a single missing tile lists a room as "unroofed (1)." Overhead mountain roofs "cannot be created by the player," cannot be removed short of dev mode, and collapse without support within 6 tiles. (excerpts) — [RimWorld Wiki, Roof](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Roof); [RimWorld Wiki, Overhead mountain](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Overhead_mountain); [Steam Community, RimWorld](https://steamcommunity.com/app/294100/discussions/0/1736588252417483940)
- Prison Architect 2 (released March 26, 2024) moved to 3D specifically to add floors: players change floors "with a control on the right side of the screen or with keyboard hotkeys," floors must be linked by staircases, utilities run through vertical pipes and wiring, a crawlspace/ceiling layer sits between floors (and lets upper-floor prisoners tunnel down), and the developers suggested designs like "placing a canteen above a kitchen." (excerpts) — [TheSixthAxis preview](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2024/05/16/prison-architect-2-preview-incarcerating-the-third-dimension/); [Paradox, Feature highlight: building tools](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/prison-architect-2/news/feature-highlight-building-tools)
- Shadows of Doubt "started life as a top-down detective management game" and moved to first person so players could "live among the citizens of the world itself"; the developer's March 2020 devlog says the game shifted "entirely to first-person," with voxel art chosen for asset speed and a per-room shader that recolours one model with a 5-colour palette "generated for each individual room." Players report multi-floor towers where "the first 9 floors have small apartments" and the upper floors are single apartments, and that "floors above 9 in most residential buildings are Echelon Zones." (excerpts) — [Wireframe](https://wireframe.raspberrypi.com/articles/walking-the-voxelated-city-streets-in-shadows-of-doubt-a-fully-simulated); [Shadows of Doubt DevBlog #21](https://colepowered.itch.io/shadows/devlog/133150/shadows-of-doubt-devblog-21-how-voxels-saved-the-project); [Steam Community, Shadows of Doubt](https://steamcommunity.com/app/986130/discussions/0/3829791550540904223)
- A Nov 2019 Unity thread asking how to do PZ-style same-scene interiors with multiple floors in 2D got only teleport-on-door (with a coroutine "to make the transition seem a bit smoother rather than instantly warping") and camera-based hiding as answers; the asker worried "zombies won't 'understand' the concept of floors" and ended with "kinda feel like i'll have to go 3D." — [Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/inside-building-and-floor-management-with-top-down-view-in-2D-unity/766851)

### Inferences
- The stairs are where floor slicing fails for players: PZ's hard switch at Z=1.00, the "walking off the top step onto nothing" perception, and the existence of two mods to peek up and down all say the same thing. A Stardew-like game that adds floors should treat the staircase as a transition with its own presentation (fade the destination floor in as the player climbs, as the "Peek a View" mod does), not a hard flip.
- The Sims' three wall modes plus manual floor stepping is the most-tested UI for floor slicing in a "living house" game, and even it draws complaints in small rooms and multi-unit buildings. Automatic cutaway and manual floor stepping are a genuine trade-off (the inZOI request), not a solved problem.
- For NPC visibility, floor slicing hides everyone on other floors, so "where did the baker go" becomes "which floor is she on." Dwarf Fortress accepts this and gives a depth indicator; nothing found in 2D games shows NPCs on other floors except through a world map or list.
- Two recent games that wanted dense multi-floor interiors (Prison Architect 2, Shadows of Doubt) stopped being 2D rather than extend slicing; that is a signal about cost, not a requirement.

### Gaps
- No official Indie Stone (Thursdoid) explanation of PZ's cutaway or floor rendering was found; all PZ evidence is player and modder descriptions.
- The Dwarf Fortress wiki's Steam-version description of how lower levels are drawn (dimming, fog) could not be fetched because of rate limiting; the Steam thread found does not describe shading. The TWBT multilevel claim is from a third-party client's page.
- No developer statement from Sabotage, Radical Fish or others about rendering floors in pixel RPGs was found.

## Key question 4: Isometric vs top-down vs 3/4 view: what each affords for interiors and floors

### Takeaway
The sources found describe the trade-offs mainly by their failure cases: the oblique/3/4 view (Ultima VII, Fallen Worlds) shows wall thickness and lets objects stack but cannot show a wall behind the player or a ceiling, pushing designers to separate rooms; isometric (PZ, RuneScape Classic) makes interiors visible only with cutaway or roof removal and makes stairs ambiguous; pure top-down keeps depth sorting trivial but gives no wall faces or door silhouettes. Direct comparative design writing was thin.

### Cited Findings
- Ultima VII's walls and characters are "depicted at a leftward angle, creating a pseudo-isometric appearance," with a top-down base and visibly multi-layered maps where "objects and things can be stacked on top of each other." — [Wikipedia, Ultima VII: The Black Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultima_VII:_The_Black_Gate)
- Ultima VI "takes full advantage of the hardware with 256-color VGA graphics and the first use of the series' unusual perspective." (excerpt) — [StrategyWiki, Ultima VI](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Ultima_VI:_The_False_Prophet)
- In Exult the vertical axis is explicit in rendering: "1 lift = 1/2 tile" and objects are offset on screen by `4 * t.tz` pixels (and `(c_tilesize * t.tz) / 2` for shape rects), so height is drawn as an upward screen shift, which is what makes stacking and upper floors legible in the 3/4 view. — [Exult source, gamewin.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/exult/exult/master/gamewin.cc)
- The oblique view's cost for interiors, from a 2020 devlog: it "prevented placing a wall behind the player or adding a ceiling," so interiors "can't be connected the way a top-down layout would allow," and the team split every multi-room interior into separate scenes. — [Fallen Worlds devlog, Interior Inception](https://precipice-games.itch.io/fallen-worlds/devlog/144628/interior-inception)
- Isometric with roof removal (RuneScape Classic) relied on per-building removal triggers and shipped with buildings whose roofs lifted in the wrong place or not at all. — [RuneScape Classic Wiki, Roof](https://classic.runescape.wiki/w/Roof)
- Isometric with floor slicing (Project Zomboid) produces the "appears as if it just appears" stair problem and a view that "looks like you are walking off the top step onto nothing." — [Steam Community, Project Zomboid](https://steamcommunity.com/app/108600/discussions/0/3459345750024537580); [Steam Community, Project Zomboid](https://steamcommunity.com/app/108600/discussions/0/343787283750680023)
- A Godot 3/4-view sorting problem shows why wall faces complicate top-down sorting: a tile "that has both background and foreground parts" has "no fixed y-sort value for the tile that will satisfy both," and the poster adjusted the player's z-index by position within the tile. (excerpt) — [Godot Forum, How to Y-sort player with 3/4 view TileMapLayer](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/how-to-y-sort-player-with-3-4-view-tilemaplayer/83392)
- Dwarf Fortress players note that even an isometric view "would still be a 2D display"; the Stonesense isometric overlay and the Armok View 3D mod were the community's answers to reading Z-levels, and neither works on the Steam version as of the sources found. — [Steam Community, Dwarf Fortress](https://steamcommunity.com/app/975370/discussions/0/3716062978749212573); [Dwarf Fortress Wiki, Utility:Stonesense](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/Utility:Stonesense)

### Inferences
- The 3/4 (Ultima/Stardew-style) view gives the renderer one rule, "later Y draws on top," plus a height offset for anything elevated. It shows front wall faces and doors, which is what a living-town game needs for "the door opened" and "lit window" cues. It cannot show the back wall of a room from inside, which is why Stardew interiors are drawn as a room seen from the front with the "fourth wall" at the bottom edge.
- Isometric buys wall thickness and elevation legibility but needs cutaway or roof removal to show interiors at all, and the sources show both mechanisms generating bugs and mods.
- Pure top-down is the only projection where a roof is literally a tile layer and a floor is literally a map; it is cheapest for seamless roof fading and worst for door and window cues.

### Gaps
- I found no design essay comparing projections specifically for interiors (e.g. from a GDC talk); the comparison above is assembled from per-game evidence.
- No source found on how Ultima VI (as opposed to VII) drew roofs or upper floors.

## Key question 5: Living-town legibility (NPC tracking, windows, lit windows, doors, sound)

### Takeaway
The found evidence says vanilla Stardew gives the player no in-world NPC tracking (1.6 only added real-time player position and other players to the world map), and the gap is filled by the NPC Map Locations mod, fan-made villager path maps and the wiki, with players calling the hunt "stressful." Majora's Mask's Bombers' Notebook is the canonical in-game answer: a timeline grid of people versus time with blue event bars and promise/reward stickers, extended in the 3DS remake with alarms and rumours. Stardew's exterior cues are lit windows and lamps at nightfall and the "Lamps inside houses don't turn on" bug report shows players notice them.

### Cited Findings
- Stardew Valley 1.6.0: "The world map now shows your actual position within the world in real-time" (instead of a fixed point per location); "In multiplayer, you'll see other players' positions in real-time too."; "Redrew the world map to better match the in-game locations and be more detailed." ### Inferences
- Under scene swap, the exterior can only tell the player about an interior through indirect cues: a lit window, an open/closed door with hours, a sound, or a map marker. Stardew ships the first two and leaves markers to mods; the mod's popularity and the "stressful" language are the evidence that the town's legibility is a felt cost.
- Under seamless roof hiding, the interior is visible when the player is near, but only that building; tracking across town still needs a notebook or map. Majora's Mask shows that a time-by-person grid is readable even with twenty entries, and the 3DS additions (rumours, alarms) are the features players evidently needed.
- Hitman's through-wall outlines and route lines are the maximal "instinct" end of the spectrum; the Shadows of Doubt camera-room design is the diegetic end. A Stardew-like could put NPC positions on the world map (what 1.6 did for players) without breaking fiction.

## Key question 6: Developer discussions and postmortems (2015-2026) on scene swap vs seamless

### Takeaway
The developer discussion found is forum-grade (gamedev.net 2015, Godot 2024, Unity 2019, a 2020 itch.io devlog) rather than talks or postmortems, and it is consistent: separate scenes are the default because unseen detail is never built or drawn; the seamless/transparent-roof route is "easier to edit" and cheaper on memory but forces large buildings and per-building authoring; the hybrid (preload the interior, teleport at the door) is proposed when load hitches matter. No GDC talk or ConcernedApe interview on this choice was located.

### Cited Findings
- gamedev.net (2015): "Interiors in games are almost always made as separate scenes from the exteriors. This is so you can easily remove unseen detail." The transparent-roof alternative "requires less power and space and is easier to edit" but "houses need to be much bigger"; "You can't make a 3x3 tile house work." Rule of thumb: fade-and-zone for rooms "you'll spend real time in (puzzles, exploration)," same map for "a single item vendor." (excerpts) — [gamedev.net, How to do RPG interiors?](https://nene.gamedev.net/forums/topic/668452-how-to-do-rpg-interiors/5231571)
- itch.io community (date not shown): "Most games keep their house interiors on separate maps." (excerpt) — [itch.io post](https://itch.io/post/2964852)
- Fallen Worlds devlog (c. May 2020): separate-scene rooms chosen because of the oblique camera; the author kept "the rooms needed for quests" and aimed to "give the player as much space to explore as possible." — [Fallen Worlds devlog, Interior Inception](https://precipice-games.itch.io/fallen-worlds/devlog/144628/interior-inception)
- Unity Discussions (2019-2021): hybrid preloading ("loading them under the map while approaching the building, then teleporting at the door") versus the cost of pure scene switching ("the outdoor scene unloads and must reload"); and the PZ-style same-scene multi-floor asker who concluded "kinda feel like i'll have to go 3D." (excerpts) — [Unity Discussions, teleport or new scene](https://discussions.unity.com/t/going-from-world-to-interiors-of-buildings-in-simple-3d-rpg-game-teleport-or-new-scene/243432); [Unity Discussions, floor management](https://discussions.unity.com/t/inside-building-and-floor-management-with-top-down-view-in-2d-unity/766851)
- Godot forum (Jan 2024): the four roof-hiding options and the "one layer turns off every roof" trap; the asker wanted alpha fade, not hide. — [Godot Forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/how-to-make-tiles-transparent-when-the-player-goes-behind-them/44438)
- Ascension/Intersect engine (date not shown): roof hiding "would actually be a pretty involved modification because tile layers are cached into big images." (excerpt) — [Ascension Game Dev forum](https://ascensiongamedev.com/topic/3248-walking-inside-building)
- Shadows of Doubt (2020-2022): abandoned top-down entirely for first person so players could "live among the citizens of the world itself." (excerpt) — [Wireframe](https://wireframe.raspberrypi.com/articles/walking-the-voxelated-city-streets-in-shadows-of-doubt-a-fully-simulated)
- Prison Architect 2 (2024): went 3D to get floors, with floors linked by stairs and a utility crawlspace between them. (excerpts) — [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2024/05/16/prison-architect-2-preview-incarcerating-the-third-dimension/)
- Ultima VII's designers, lacking multi-map support, duplicated whole buildings off-map "for programming reasons" to stage cutscenes; Exult's multi-map support "makes these workarounds unnecessary." — [Ultima Codex, Quirks of the Ultima VII engine](https://www.wiki.ultimacodex.com/wiki/Quirks_of_the_Ultima_VII_engine)
- Exult still exposes the map-wide render toggle for roofs (`--buildmap` with all roofs / no second-floor roofs / no roofs). (excerpt) — [Debian manpage, exult(6)](https://dyn.manpages.debian.org/trixie/exult/exult.6.en.html)

### Inferences
- Across a decade of forum threads the arguments did not change: scene swap wins on authoring and performance; seamless wins on continuity and editing but taxes building size and roof authoring; nobody found argues that seamless makes a town feel more alive, though several assume it. That claim is untested in the sources.
- The engines people reach for shape the answer: baked tile layers push toward scene swap; node-per-roof scene graphs (Godot) make per-building hiding cheap.

- No GDC or indie-conference talk specifically on 2D world structure (scene swap vs seamless) was found; no ConcernedApe, Radical Fish, Sabotage, Pixpil (Eastward) or Lazy Bear (Graveyard Keeper) interview on the subject surfaced. r/gamedev threads did not appear in search results.
- No written postmortem of a 2D game that shipped seamless roof fading (beyond RuneScape Classic's wiki bug list and Exult's code) was found.

## Key question 7: Rendering mechanics for a web/canvas renderer (depth sorting, layers, roof fade, camera, transitions)

### Takeaway
The practical mechanics documented across Exult, Godot, Phaser and PixiJS reduce to: sort dynamic things by Y (feet), put static tiles into "below" and "above" layers rather than trying to depth-sort tiles (Phaser tiles "have no depth"; PixiJS sorts only within one container), give roofs their own group per building with a trigger derived from the footprint, fade with alpha/modulate rather than a hard hide, and under floor slicing dim rather than drop the floor below. Scene swap needs only per-map camera bounds and a fade.

### Cited Findings
- Exult's painter: skip any object with `lift >= skip`; the cutoff is recomputed on scroll from the roof probe above the camera actor; outlines and hit-testing (`find_object`) use the same cutoff so the player cannot click hidden objects. — [Exult source, gamerend.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/exult/exult/master/gamerend.cc); [Exult source, gamewin.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/exult/exult/master/gamewin.cc)
- Phaser 3: asked whether a wall tile's depth can follow its Y, maintainer samme answered (Aug 29, 2023) "Yes. Tiles have no depth." Tiles rendered through `createLayer` cannot be depth-sorted; the tiles that must sort against the player are added as individual sprites (as in the official depth-sorting example). — [Phaser Discourse, Set tile depth](https://phaser.discourse.group/t/set-tile-depth-access-image-or-sprite-of-tile/13502)
- Phaser community practice: split a tile layer into "behind" and "in front" layers, or convert sortable tiles to sprites and set depth from Y; a GameMaker tool does the same by splitting a layer "into multiple layers set at various depths." (excerpts) — [Phaser Discourse, Depth sorting with tilemaps](https://phaser.discourse.group/t/depth-sorting-with-tilemaps/8144); [GameMaker Marketplace, dynamic tile depth demo](https://marketplace.gamemaker.io/assets/5708/dynamic-tile-depth-demo)
- PixiJS v8: set `zIndex` on children and `sortableChildren` on the parent (or call `sortChildren()`), and "Use this feature sparingly, as sorting can be expensive for large numbers of children." Sorting applies only among children of one container; "If you want to sort through the tree, ignoring container hierarchy, you have to use special plugin" (pixi-layers, where "now you have to use zOrder=sprite.y"). (excerpts) — [PixiJS Guides, Container](https://pixijs.com/8.x/guides/components/scene-objects/container); [pixi-layers README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/pixi-layers@0.3.1/README.md); [HTML5GameDevs, zIndex not working](https://www.html5gamedevs.com/topic/45703-zindex-not-working-pixi/)
- Godot 4 TileMapLayer: `y_sort_origin` "is added to each tile's Y-sort origin value. This allows, for example, to fake a different height level," which "can be useful for top-down view games"; `x_draw_order_reversed` reverses X draw order when Y-sorted; "The quadrant size does not apply on a Y-sorted TileMapLayer, as tiles are grouped by Y position instead"; `enabled = false` "disables this TileMapLayer completely (rendering, collision, navigation, scene tiles, etc.)". — [Godot docs, TileMapLayer](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_tilemaplayer.html)
- Godot 4.3 practice: Y-sort works when the TileMapLayer and the actors are children of the same Y-sorted parent and "All objects must share the same Z-index"; a user first saw cell sorting fail because it compared against the layer origin at (0,0). (excerpt) — [Godot Forum, y-sort for TileMapLayer in 4.3](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/how-to-make-y-sort-for-tilemaplayer-in-godot-4-3/87689)
- Roof fade mechanisms (Godot, Jan 2024): disable a roof layer; hide/show per-roof nodes via Area2D; a shader that clears a circle or halves alpha; or viewports. Trade-off stated: a shared layer "would turn of other ones." A GameDev.tv thread adds the enter/exit counter for multi-collider (L/T-shaped) buildings. Another Godot user "simply set the roof's alpha to 0 when the player gets close." (excerpts) — [Godot Forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/how-to-make-tiles-transparent-when-the-player-goes-behind-them/44438); [GameDev.tv community](https://community.gamedev.tv/t/transparency-on-a-specific-section-of-the-tilemap/224516); [Godot Forum, sprites disappear](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/have-sprites-disappear-when-receiving-light/135184)
- A tilemap canopy done as one layer "faded out when the player touched any part of it, so per-section control needed multiple layers." (excerpt) — [GameDev.tv community](https://community.gamedev.tv/t/transparency-on-a-specific-section-of-the-tilemap/224516)
- Transitions: a Godot 4 RPG tutorial covers scene transitions and a day/night cycle alongside roof alpha modulate (excerpt); the Camelot `enter` command's optional fade-in through a door shows the minimal door-transition primitive. — [DEV Community, Godot 4 RPG part 18](https://dev.to/christinec_dev/lets-learn-godot-4-by-making-an-rpg-part-18-scene-transitions-day-night-cycle-4mnm); [Camelot docs, enter](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/projects/camelot/v1-3/enter.html)
- Floor slicing: Project Zomboid players asked for "a faded view of the floor below" instead of a hard flip at Z=1.00; the Peek a View mod "renders the upper floor while you climb." Dwarf Fortress multilevel rendering (TWBT) shows the current level plus levels beneath with "fog and shadow parameters." (excerpts) — [Steam Community, Project Zomboid](https://steamcommunity.com/app/108600/discussions/0/3459345750024537580); [Peek a View mod](https://catalogue.smods.ru/archives/430878); [Dwarf Fortress Remote 1.15](https://mifki.com/?p=594)
- Camera and interiority under scene swap: each Stardew interior is its own map with a `DefaultArrivalTile`, and warps carry hours and gates, so the camera's world bounds are simply the current map's size. — [Stardew Modding Wiki, Tutorial: Map Patches and Warps](https://stardewmodding.wiki.gg/wiki/Tutorial:_Map_Patches_and_Warps)

### Inferences
- A canvas renderer for a Stardew-like 3/4 view can get away with: ground layers (no sorting), one Y-sorted list of actors and tall props (sort key = foot Y, Exult-style height offset if anything is elevated), an "above" tile layer for roofs and canopies, and a per-building roof group with an alpha value. That is the whole renderer commitment for either scene swap (where the roof group is never faded) or seamless (where it is).
- If roofs will fade, author them as one drawable group per building keyed to the building's footprint, not as a shared "roof" tile layer; both Godot threads and the RuneScape bug list are about the shared-layer and hand-drawn-trigger failure modes.
- If floors are added later, the same alpha mechanism (dim floor N-1 to, say, 30 percent while on floor N; fade floor N in during the stair climb) addresses the PZ complaint directly and needs nothing the roof fade did not already require.
- Hit-testing must respect whatever is hidden (Exult filters `find_object` by the same cutoff); otherwise players click NPCs they cannot see.

### Gaps
- No Excalibur-specific or PixiJS-tilemap-specific roof-fade write-up was found; the PixiJS guidance is about `zIndex`/`sortableChildren` only.
- Phaser's official docs for `TilemapLayer.setAlpha` / `setDepth` were not fetched; the per-layer alpha capability is stated only in the search summary and should be checked against the current API before relying on it.
